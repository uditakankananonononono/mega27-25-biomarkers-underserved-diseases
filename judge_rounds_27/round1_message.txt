 ToxinAudit-LabelScope: an enzyme EC provenance gate before
 family-transfer modeling
 Candidate-specific working report, September 25, 2026. This report does not extend the previous toxin family's
 AUROC result or count that earlier work as a new result. It records a new exact-accession enzyme evidence audit. Its
 principal scientific outcome is that a predictive benchmark should not be run without measured function-negative
 labels, even after the positive accession set was expanded.

 Abstract
 A random-versus-family generalization gap in the earlier VenomFunction project prompted a distinct question: would
 a new protein-function benchmark with enzyme EC labels also reveal annotation shortcuts when sequence families
 are held out? We inspected direct UniProtKB reviewed entries for carboxylesterase EC 3.1.1.1 and hexokinase EC
 2.7.1.1. Twenty-six individual entry JSON records were fetched, with original URLs and SHA-256 hashes. Seven
 esterase and five hexokinase entries have an experimental ECO:0000269 citation attached specifically to the target
 EC. Fourteen other entries have the target EC annotation but no direct experimental evidence attached to that specific
 target EC in the parsed entry. None is a measured function-negative control. A subsequent frozen API-ranked
 expansion independently fetched 120 reviewed EC entries, 62 esterases and 58 hexokinases, with 22 and 19 target-
 EC experimental citations respectively. The original 26 are preserved but not added to the 120. Neither panel
 contains assayed target-function negatives. A 3-mer sequence-overlap proxy finds 22 clusters in the original panel
 and at least one cluster crossing a seeded random train/test split, but no classifier or AUROC can be justified from a
 panel with no assayed negatives. Direct QuickGO, InterPro and ChEMBL crosschecks provide annotation context,
 not negative function measurements. The output is a runnable fail-closed benchmark auditor, not a validated enzyme-
 function model.

 1. Biological question and distinction from the parent toxin work
 The earlier VenomFunction analysis compared random splits to snake-toxin family-disjoint splits using a curated
 keyword. A large gap there is motivation for a different annotation task, not an independent test of this enzyme
 problem. Enzyme Commission (EC) terms describe reactions under specified substrates and conditions; they cannot
 be treated as measured binary labels for arbitrary proteins without examining evidence. Our two target EC values,
 3.1.1.1 (carboxylesterase) and 2.7.1.1 (hexokinase), have different chemistry and enzyme-family composition. The
 planned question was whether a predictor trained on one set of enzyme families would maintain performance on
 held-out groups compared with a random split. A defensible binary benchmark requires positives with direct
 experimental support and negatives assayed for absence of the same target reaction. A protein with a different EC is
 not automatically negative for a multifunctional enzyme, and a record lacking ECO:0000269 is not a negative.
 We used a build-time feasibility gate: at least five direct experimentally supported positives in each function class, at
 least five experimentally verified negatives for that function, and homolog-disjoint train/test groups before
 computing a predictive metric. The threshold was scoped during the build, not registered with a third party. It is a
 refusal criterion, not a claim that five labels are enough for precise field-scale AUROC.

 2. Direct accession retrieval and label parsing
 build_data.py requests each selected accession's UniProtKB JSON record separately at https://
 rest.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/<accession>.json. Each record in accessions.json stores accession, sequence,
 organism, taxonomy ID, all parsed EC terms, target-EC experimental evidence codes, URL and response digest. Both
 recommended and alternative protein names are parsed, because a target EC can appear under an alternative name.
 The first convenience panel was 12 reviewed proteins. Fourteen more carboxylesterase records were retrieved as a
 new fixed tranche, yielding 26 entries: 19 esterase and seven hexokinase. This selection is nonrandom and not
 representative of all enzymes.
 For each record, the code checks that the expected target EC occurs among the parsed EC annotations. It then
 distinguishes a direct ECO:0000269 citation on that EC from a citation on another EC or a general annotation
 elsewhere in the protein. Among 19 esterase entries, seven have direct target-EC experimental citation; among seven
 hexokinases, five do. The positive count minimum is therefore met after expansion. The remaining 14 records must

 3. Approximate homology contamination audit
 audit.py computes each sequence's set of amino-acid 3-mers and pairwise Jaccard overlap. At a fixed 0.55 overlap
 cutoff it takes connected components as an approximate contamination proxy. This is not sequence alignment,
 orthology assignment or a family phylogeny. It yields 22 groups for 26 entries. A deterministic random quarter-test
 split (seed 27) shares at least one proxy cluster across train and test. That exposes a concrete failure mode for a future
 random fold: a homolog-like group can occupy both sides. It does not prove the old toxin model's full AUROC
 difference was caused by this exact mechanism, and no new enzyme AUROC was fitted.
 The threshold and split were specified as audit defaults, not chosen for predictive performance. Even if a group-
 disjoint assignment were made, there would still be no assayed negative class. Recording a family split alone would
 not rescue the benchmark. A larger panel with independent structure or curated orthology groups and harmonized
 enzyme assays would be needed before comparing random and family-held-out metrics.

 4. Evidence crosschecks from distinct sources
 To test whether two named accessions mapped to the expected annotation families, crosschecks.py queried EBI
 QuickGO, InterPro and ChEMBL, for O00748 and P19367. The source ledger stores response hashes and interpreted
 records. O00748 maps to InterPro carboxylesterase type B domain IPR002018 and ChEMBL target CHEMBL3180.
 P19367 maps to InterPro hexokinase family IPR001312 and ChEMBL target CHEMBL2688. QuickGO supplies
 molecular-function annotation evidence identifiers; its GO:0004340 records for P19367 include computational or
 inferred references as well as curated context. GO and domain hits are not direct absence assays. ChEMBL target
 indexing is not a standardized EC activity outcome for every construct.
 The original scientific source inventory was UniProtKB, QuickGO, InterPro and ChEMBL (four); a later UniRef50
 sequence-cluster audit adds a fifth materially used resource. EBI hosts two distinct annotated data resources; if the
 program counts all EBI endpoints as one conservative parent service, the count would be lower. Repeated accessions,
 model libraries, transport infrastructure and alternate endpoint paths do not turn five sources into forty independent
 tools.

 6. Implementation and tests
 build_data.py retrieves entry JSON in parallel with bounded workers. audit.py checks uniqueness, target EC
 presence, positive evidence grade, proxy sequence groups and deterministic split crossings. test_build_data.py
 asserts that all 26 accession URLs and hashes are present and no fetched entry is silently relabeled as an experimental
 negative. test_audit.py tests duplicate refusal, wrong-target EC rejection, group formation and null AUROC.
 crosschecks.py can be rerun to refresh the three contextual annotations for two named accessions. Run python3
 projects/toxin_audit/build_data.py, python3 projects/toxin_audit/audit.py, and python3 -m pytest
 -q projects/toxin_audit from repository root. Remote UniProt records may change; compare response hashes
 before treating a rerun as identical data.

 7. Limits and next valid experiment
 First, ECO:0000269 on an EC annotation is evidence-linked curation, not a complete re-analysis of substrate, rate
 and assay context. Second, the accession panel mixes organisms and substrates, so a numerical split comparison
 would confound lineage and label provenance. Third, 3-mer Jaccard components can miss homologs with
 rearrangements and collapse unrelated low-complexity proteins. Fourth, the convenience accession list is not
 sampled from a defined background population. Fifth, no cross-protein target-function absence assay was found in
 this original accession retrieval, and no outside wet-lab cohort has been validated. A later single-protein variant assay
 is scoped separately below. The correct present state is a tested source/label auditor and a blocked predictive
 benchmark.
 A future experiment could gather independently measured positive and negative substrate assays for the same target
 reaction, freeze family groups from a credible alignment/orthology resource, reserve a truly external measured cohort
 and only then fit a fixed linear baseline. If those data are unavailable, preserving the negative feasibility result is
 scientifically preferable to relabeling a curated database as ground truth. This report does not promise that future data
 will produce a positive classifier.

 Post-report accession expansion, September 25
 The original 26-entry cohort and its numerical cluster results above remain an archival small-panel analysis. For the
 distinct retrieval-count gate, expand_panel.py froze the first 62 reviewed UniProtKB EC 3.1.1.1 and 58 reviewed
 EC 2.7.1.1 identifiers in search order before inspecting evidence. It then fetched 120 exact entry JSON responses
 separately, saving URL, response-byte SHA-256, sequence and parsed EC evidence in data/
 expanded_accessions.json; the fixed search URLs and chosen IDs are in data/expanded_selection.json.
 Included multifunctional proteins required parsing EC terms under the entry's includes components, not only its
 main name. The expanded audit finds 22/62 and 19/58 target-EC direct ECO:0000269 citations, 79 records without
 that grade, and 97 approximate 3-mer components. These are nonrandom, heavily cross-species accession
 annotations. No activity-negative assay cohort appeared; a missing citation remains unknown, so the function-
 prediction AUROC is null. data/expanded_results.json records that refusal. This establishes 120 separately
 retrieved accession records, not 120 independent activity assays, biological discoveries, or a benchmark win.

 External sequence-cluster audit
 Each of the 120 expanded enzyme accessions was separately mapped to a UniRef50 sequence cluster, saving URL,
 response SHA-256 and ID in data/uniref50_membership.json. The panel spans 73 UniRef50 groups; 19 contain
 multiple selected entries, with a largest group of 11. The two EC target classes do not co-occur within a selected
 group in this mapping. This is an external 50%-identity grouping reference rather than the report's original 3-mer
 proxy, and a fifth materially used annotation source. It still does not establish substrate-specific negative activity or a
 measured family-disjoint benchmark; the AUROC remains null.

 Separate measured variant-assay scope check, September 26
 Gersing et al. 2023 tested full-length human glucokinase GCK variants by expressing them in yeast lacking
 endogenous hexokinases, then selecting growth on glucose. The original workbook provides 9,362 unfloored
 continuous amino-acid-level scores. The paper reports coverage of 9,003/9,280 possible missense and nonsense
 variants and a 38-variant comparison with in-vitro catalytic efficiency (Spearman 0.76, its own result). Our
 independent source parsing found 8,570 missense, 433 nonsense and 359 synonymous variant records, all with
 HGVS reference residues matching the separately fetched 465-aa UniProt P35557 (EC 2.7.1.1) sequence. At a
 descriptive, not validated classifier, threshold of score <0.2, 2,788 missense, 332 nonsense and nine synonymous
 records fall below it. The original assay, score normalization, abundance effects and error distribution matter, so this
 is not proof that each low-score variant is biochemically inactive. One protein's thousands of related variants are one
 experimental dataset, not thousands of independent enzyme families, and a within-P35557 diagnostic cannot supply
 measured inactive other-protein controls for the planned family-disjoint esterase or hexokinase classifier. We make
 no AUROC claim and retain the no-benchmark verdict. The exact source links, workbook and sequence digests,
 independent parser and focused tests are in data/gck_variant_scope.json and gck_variant_scope.py.
