 GraphHoldout: publication-time gene-disease ranking with cold-
 start refusal
 Candidate-specific working report, September 25, 2026. This is separate from the aggregate item-27 manuscript and is
 not a validated gene-discovery paper. The build produces a reproducible ranker, source inventory and leakage audit. It
 does not prove causal disease interactions or use physical protein-interaction evidence.

 Abstract
 Graph methods can look accurate if a target gene-disease edge is duplicated in reverse, if evidence from the same paper
 crosses train and test, or if future curated labels are treated as negative until their publication. We used the original CTD
 curated gene-disease report and PubMed publication-year metadata to construct 556 evidence rows for four named
 diseases. A 2012 cutoff produces 356 unique training pairs and 114 novel later pairs. Of the later pairs, 106 involve
 genes absent from the training graph, so a ranker limited to known nodes cannot retrieve them. Among eight reachable
 later positive pairs, top-10 recall is asthma 2/2, breast neoplasms 0/5, prostate cancer 0/1, and diabetes unevaluable.
 Unknown pairs were never treated as experimental negatives; no precision or AUROC is claimed. A current Open
 Targets query provides additional context but not an independent historical validation, since underlying literature and
 CTD contributions may overlap. The result is a working temporal-leakage/cold-start audit, not a validated biological
 predictor.

 1. Question and task boundary
 The original candidate called for graph-based gene-disease ranking with source- and time-disjoint edge holdouts. Named
 biological interaction evidence must remain distinct from text-mined co-occurrence. A strict version would train on a
 genuinely historical graph snapshot from one source and assess prospectively new, independently curated edges from
 another source, with interaction provenance. This build tests only a narrower question: under a publication-year split
 within one modern CTD snapshot, can a simple training-only neighbor ranker recover subsequently published CTD-
 curated gene-disease associations for genes already in the training graph?
 This question matters because a model evaluated on random edges can receive nearly the same biological relation
 through reverse arcs or duplicated publications. Even when leakage is removed, a cold-start gene cannot be ranked by a
 graph-only method that has no representation for it. The implemented software makes those limitations observable rather
 than returning a seductive performance score on mislabeled unknowns.

 2. Data lineage and exact identifiers
 The direct source is the CTD curated gene-disease report, downloaded as a compressed TSV. data/manifest.json
 records its SHA-256 digest. We read only rows with a direct curated evidence code and PMID, not inferred or text-
 mined edges. Four disease IDs were fixed by exact MESH term: D001249 asthma, D001943 breast neoplasms, D003920
 diabetes mellitus and D011471 prostate neoplasms. We chose every third numerically sorted PubMed ID among
 matching rows, capped at 275 distinct PMIDs, before querying dates. This is a tractability sample, not a representative
 random biological sample. It produces 556 gene-MESH-PMID evidence rows from 275 publications. The row-level
 data/edges.json records gene symbol, NCBI Gene ID, disease ID, evidence type, PMID and publication year.

 Publication years were read through the NCBI PubMed E-utilities summary endpoint. The year of an article is not
 necessarily when CTD added a curated edge. This distinction is fundamental: a 2026 CTD snapshot cannot be made into
 a genuine 2012 graph merely by filtering contemporary rows on the original article's date. The experiment is a
 retrospective illustration of a split, not a historical replay. Rows with no usable year are excluded rather than assigned
 one.

 3. Edge representation and holdout
 Each gene-disease relation is represented once by canonical (NCBI Gene ID, MESH ID). A publication through 2012
 puts that relation in training. A later publication contributes a novel test pair only when that exact relation was absent
 from training. We also require the source PMID sets on opposite sides to be disjoint, rejecting any overlap. Representing
 one canonical relation makes a reverse disease-gene arc equivalent to the same edge; it cannot be left in training when
 the target pair is held out. An explicit test covers this recurrence and a second test rejects a PMID shared across split
 years.

 The candidate universe for each disease includes only genes observed in training through 2012 whose target disease
 edge is not already known. All other candidate pairs remain unlabeled, not measured biological negatives. The ranker
 can put a later positive high in this universe, but top-10 recall is not precision, because the noncurated remainder can
 contain true associations. We separately count later positive pairs whose gene never appeared in training as cold-start
 failures rather than hiding them from the denominator of task feasibility.

 4. Model and metrics
 For each candidate gene and disease, the score sums the size of shared training-gene neighborhoods between the target
 disease and every other training disease to which the gene connects, plus 0.01 times the candidate gene's training disease
 degree. This is a simple two-hop graph heuristic, not a graph neural network. The 0.01 degree term and top-10 cutoff
 were fixed as software constants; the resulting values have not been optimized against the held-out pairs. The exact
 ranking and tie-breaker by gene ID are reproducible from evaluate.py.
 For a target disease with reachable later positives, positive recall@10 equals the number of those positives ranked in the
 first ten candidate genes divided by the number of reachable later positives. The report also gives the expected top-10
 fraction under a uniform random permutation of the same candidate list. That random expectation is a comparison of
 ranks, not an empirically validated negative control or a statistical significance test. Where a disease has zero reachable
 positives the recall is explicitly null.

 The graph contains 356 unique pre-cutoff pairs and 114 novel post-cutoff pairs. Only eight test positives involve genes
 available to rank. A high-looking 2/2 for asthma is too small and selected to support generalization, especially against six
 other reachable positives with zero hits. The 106 cold-start pairs make a stronger practical point: a graph-only ranker
 limited to known genes has no defined prediction for most subsequently curated edges in this sample. No gene is
 nominated for laboratory testing from this exploratory output.

 6. External source context without false independence
 The Open Targets Platform GraphQL endpoint was queried for four current MONDO concepts, mapped to the four CTD
 MESH terms by concept name. Exact-symbol overlaps between sampled CTD genes and the platform's current top-100
 associated targets were 9/39 asthma, 19/220 breast neoplasm, 27/197 prostate cancer and 4/14 diabetes mellitus. data/
 external_source.json retains the query, disease ID, returned ranking, response digest and matched symbols.

 That endpoint is not an independent 2012 validation. It may include the same papers or CTD-derived evidence, and
 symbols are not stable accession mappings. Current target ranks include evidence accumulated after 2012. A second
 website with overlapping source content is not a second scientific discovery. This analysis therefore checks modern
 cross-service context only and is not added to the temporal benchmark denominator.

 7. Missing interaction and source gates
 The CTD rows are curated associations such as marker/mechanism or therapeutic links. They are not direct, named
 physical gene-product interactions. A path through CTD disease neighbors can suggest co-annotation; it cannot establish
 protein binding, an intervention effect or causal disease mechanism. A future mechanistic graph would need interaction
 records with their experiment type, partners, provenance and date, and it would keep text-mined co-occurrence in a
 separate layer. No such interaction layer was added here, so the build's graph score is expressly a disease co-annotation
 heuristic.
 A genuinely source-disjoint evaluation would require a historical training snapshot that can be dated by curation time
 and an independently generated future test set with evidence overlap checked at paper, gene and disease level. At least
 20 reachable positives per disease and several independent diseases would allow a less fragile recall estimate. We have

 8. Reproducibility and adversarial checks
 Run python3 projects/graph_holdout/prepare.py to download CTD and query PubMed dates; compare CTD
 source hash to committed data/manifest.json. Run python3 projects/graph_holdout/evaluate.py on
 committed data/edges.json to regenerate rankings and primary result JSON. Run python3 projects/
 graph_holdout/external_source.py to query current Open Targets ranks, noting those results can change. Run
 python3 -m pytest -q projects/graph_holdout for tests of reverse-relation leakage, shared-PMID rejection,
 unseen-gene handling and the cross-source query shape. CTD's publication/citation and linking terms need separate legal
 review for downstream redistribution; see https://ctdbase.org/about/legal.jsp. The repo retains a small sampled
 derivative, not the full CTD report.

 9. Interpretation
 The task is not solved. The usable result is a transparent auditor that refuses to turn absent labels into negatives, keeps
 one edge and its reverse together, separates publication sources across time, and exposes the cold-start denominator. The
 apparent asthma top-10 result cannot offset the breast/prostate failures, tiny denominators or single-source retrospective
 leakage risk. No clinical, mechanistic or therapeutic inference is supported. A new prospectively registered historical-
 source and experimentally grounded graph would be a different study, not a retrofit of this result.

 Post-report PMID provenance audit, September 25
 The separate verify_pmid_metadata.py audit individually retrieved 120 PubMed ESummary records, the first 60
 numeric IDs on each side of the 2012 publication-time split. All 120 IDs, response-byte SHA-256 digests, parsed
 publication dates and linked row counts are in data/pmid_metadata_audit.json; every year matches the existing
 CTD edge table. This is a check against batch parsing errors and bibliographic accession identity. It is not 120
 independently fetched biological association datasets and does not turn a modern CTD snapshot into a historical one.
 The original ranker results and refusal stand.

 Actual March 2016 GWAS archive eligibility
 The GWAS Catalog original March 6, 2016 release is a dated source, unlike the current CTD snapshot retrospectively
 split by paper year. Its original 24,413 SNP-trait rows were pinned by SHA-256. Exact trait names, not synonym
 searches, yield 55 Asthma, 199 Breast cancer, 201 Prostate cancer, 97 Type 1 diabetes and 340 Type 2 diabetes rows.
 Only 31, 146, 104, 60 and 205 rows respectively carry a single unambiguous numeric SNP-locus Entrez mapping; the
 rest have missing or multi-ID mappings. Three original CTD breast cancer publication IDs and one prostate cancer
 publication ID recur in the corresponding GWAS trait subset. Asthma and the separate Type 1/2 diabetes subsets show
 no same-PMID match within our small CTD panel, but that alone does not establish independent evidence, trait
 equivalence or temporal train/test integrity. A GWAS SNP's locus-mapped gene is not a directly assayed causal gene-
 disease link, and a plain-text trait is not a verified MeSH disease crosswalk. In particular, the CTD target's broad
 Diabetes Mellitus term cannot silently absorb both Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes. We do not treat these rows as biological
 association negatives or as source-disjoint positive truth, and calculate no new recall or AUROC. The source digest,
 exact rules, per-target publication overlap, executable scope audit and tests are in data/gwas_archive/ and
 gwas_archive_scope.py. This is a substantive negative validation finding, not a newly validated predictor.
