7. Residue-attribution proxy: retained negative
The pre-specified site test replaces a curated active-site position with X and compares the absolute model-logit
change with a within-protein control position more than five residues away. This is an input-sensitivity proxy.
Catalytic active sites can belong to non-neurotoxin enzymes, so a failure here is not a definitive absence of a
neurotoxin causal residue. It cannot prove binding, treatment benefit or counterfactual protein behavior.
                           D_site = |logit f(sequence with site -> X) - logit f(original sequence)|.
                                R_site/control = mean(D_site) / mean(D_matched_control).
The audit covers 568 protein records and 1103 site-control pairs. Site mean absolute delta was 0.10132, control
mean 0.15047, ratio 0.67338; 40.53% of sites exceeded controls. Predicted enrichment failed in this proxy. We
retain the negative rather than retune sites after results. The complete positions, provenance and limitations are in
venom/data/active_site_audit.json.


8. Distinct motif and structure context: annotations, not potency
ScanProsite direct scans found SNAKE_TOXIN PS00272 on P01400 and phospholipase A2 His/Asp signatures
PS00118 and PS00119 on P00604. This corroborates broad family identity but not potency. HMMER phmmer
queried the P01400 mature sequence against its PDB protein set and returned a Bungarus candidus weak-toxin
homolog 2jqp_A (E=1.67e-28). PDBe maps that homolog accession Q8AY51 to a 65-residue solution-NMR
structure at 100% coverage; RCSB confirms the same PDB chain. It does not show P01400 has a resolved
structure. Shared PDB data from PDBe and RCSB count as one structural evidence source.
Exact live-returned URLs and records: external_tools/prosite_check.json, hmmer_check.json and
structure_check.json. The RCSB UniProt text-search attempt returned HTTP 400, and an earlier PDBe P00604
mapping returned 404; those attempts were not counted. Motif, homology and structure are orthogonal checks of
identity or fold availability, not a new potency assay or a validation of the predictor.


9. PhosGene: coding-sequence arithmetic and physiology gap
GenBank U00096.3 resolves the AppA CDS and translation corresponding to UniProt P07102. The older M58708
CDS has an ambiguous base at one codon; the K-12 genome provides a resolved codon with the same translated
amino acid. The Kazusa taxon 83333 codon table has 64 codon entries based on only 14 coding sequences and
5,122 codons, so its reference weights are sparse. The optimizer replaces each amino acid codon with the highest-
frequency synonymous codon and retains a terminal stop. It checks translation identity and input bounds. No
construct was synthesized or tested for host expression.
                  w(c | a) = observed_count(c) / max_{synonymous c* encoding a} observed_count(c*).
                          CAI(sequence) = exp[(1/L) sum_{i=1}^{L} log w(c_i | amino_acid_i)].
Observed AppA CAI is 0.610734 against the sparse table; the unrestricted maximum is 1.0 by construction. A
ceiling reached by replacing all codons is an arithmetic test, not evidence for increased expression or secretion.
GC-window constraints, repeat filtering and host-specific secretory regulation are not supported in the current
optimizer.

9.1 Reaction identity and source disagreement
KEGG eco:b0980 maps appA to a 4-phytase/acid phosphatase context, while b4055 maps another phosphatase
AphA. UniProt P07102 assigns an unspecific EC and one positional pentakisphosphate product. ExPASy
ENZYME and Rhea list generic enzyme-class reactions and can use different inositol phosphate positional
conventions. It would be incorrect to use any one generic EC step as proof of six phosphate-release steps in vivo.
All source claims and exact URLs are in external_tools/phos_reaction_check.json.
BRENDA Km entries span assay contexts, not validated pig luminal conditions. STRING resolves AppA to the
b0980 gene neighborhood without showing dietary phenotype. EcoCyc independently links AppA monomer to
appA/b0980 and periplasmic location; QuickGO returns 21 evidence-coded annotations including IDA/EXP
activity/localization, often citing EcoCyc or UniProt. IntAct returns three partner rows with two interaction AC
values from one culture pull-down study, not independent animal assays. PRIDE Archive protein P07102 search
maps to three project identifiers, but direct project metadata lists horse/mouse/bovine, human and C. elegans, no
pig context. That mapping is not itself evidence of organism-specific AppA peptide assignment. These checks
locate a gene and a function-context disagreement rather than filling the kinetic gap. Source files:
external_tools/taxonomy_and_brenda.json, string_check.json, appa_annotations.json, intact_check.json,
pride_check.json.
The 2018 multi-enzyme pigs study at https://elifesciences.org/articles/34286 reports fecal P lower by 45.8% under
a corn-soybean diet and 34.8% under a wheat/corn/soybean/bran diet (its abstract pools N and P into a 23.2%-
45.8% range; the 23.2% lower endpoint is fecal N), whereas the original Enviropig report at
https://europepmc.org/article/MED/11479566 reports up to 75%. These are different systems and comparisons. A
pooled 20%-60% band is not a defensible prespecified verification target. No pig-gut measurement has been
generated by this repository. The next within-PhosGene direction is a parameter-identifiability tool: report the
entire feasible fecal-P outcome range over evidence-bounded inputs, and leave unknown dimensions visibly
unbounded instead of selecting values to hit a published pig outcome. This is a planned pivot, not a completed
scientific result.
