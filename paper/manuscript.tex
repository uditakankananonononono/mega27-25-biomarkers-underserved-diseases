\begin{abstract}
We evaluate expression-derived gene candidates across ten disease labels (ten with at least one analyzable arm, although evidence across diseases remains uneven), including postpartum depression. A prespecified duplicate-sample audit materially changed earlier apparent replications. In the deduplicated analysis, top-50 signatures passed an empirical-null sign test in polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and interstitial cystitis, though the latter has a small validation cohort; five other testable disease signatures did not pass. A named preeclampsia candidate, GNG2, did not replicate. Independent fresh RNA-seq cohorts falsified the directional ST3GAL2 prediction. A held-out cohort convolutional neural network (CNN) scored below logistic regression on average. The graph neural network (GNN) benchmark is within-project only; no published state-of-the-art comparison or validated new biomarker has yet been established. This manuscript is a working report, not a clinical test claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Effect estimates and derivation}
For cohort $c$ and gene $g$, with $n_1$ case samples and $n_0$ controls, estimate the pooled sample variance as
\begin{equation}s_{p,gc}^2=\frac{(n_1-1)s_{1,gc}^2+(n_0-1)s_{0,gc}^2}{n_1+n_0-2}.\end{equation}
The standardized mean difference is $d_{gc}=(\bar x_{1,gc}-\bar x_{0,gc})/s_{p,gc}$. We correct its small-sample bias with $J(n)=1-3/(4n-9)$:
\begin{equation}g_{gc}=J(n_1+n_0)d_{gc}.\end{equation}
The plug-in within-study variance is
\begin{equation}v_{gc}=\frac{n_1+n_0}{n_1n_0}+\frac{g_{gc}^2}{2(n_1+n_0)}.\end{equation}
These formulas follow by first pooling the two unbiased sample variance estimates using their residual degrees of freedom, then standardizing the case-control mean contrast, then multiplying by the first-order Hedges correction. For $k$ studies of a gene, define inverse-variance fixed weights $w_c=v_c^{-1}$ and pooled fixed effect $\bar g_F=(\sum_c w_c g_c)/(\sum_c w_c)$. Cochran's dispersion statistic is
\begin{equation}Q_g=\sum_c w_c(g_c-\bar g_F)^2.\end{equation}
The DerSimonian--Laird moment estimator subtracts the $k-1$ expected dispersion under zero heterogeneity:
\begin{equation}\hat\tau_g^2=\max\left\{0,\frac{Q_g-(k-1)}{\sum_cw_c-\sum_cw_c^2/\sum_cw_c}\right\}.\end{equation}
Using $w_c^*=(v_c+\hat\tau_g^2)^{-1}$ yields the random-effects estimate and its normal-reference uncertainty:
\begin{equation}\hat\mu_g=\frac{\sum_cw_c^*g_c}{\sum_cw_c^*},\qquad \operatorname{SE}(\hat\mu_g)=\left(\sum_cw_c^*\right)^{-1/2}.\end{equation}
The heterogeneity fraction is truncated at zero to avoid negative estimates:
\begin{equation}\hat I_g^2=\max\left\{0,\frac{Q_g-(k-1)}{Q_g}\right\}.\end{equation}
A two-sided Wald test takes $z_g=\hat\mu_g/\operatorname{SE}(\hat\mu_g)$ and $p_g=2\Phi(-|z_g|)$. False discovery rate for $m$ ranked tests uses the backward minimum
\begin{equation}q_{(i)}=\min_{j\geq i}\left\{1,\frac{m p_{(j)}}{j}\right\}.\end{equation}
The backward minimum enforces monotonicity of adjusted values in sorted $p$, while $m/j$ accounts for rank and family size. A single-cohort disease does not estimate between-cohort heterogeneity, irrespective of its many genes.
\section{Graph, CNN and evaluation design}
The gene network comes from STRING physical links; candidate labels use Open Targets non-expression evidence. Expression, graph degree and random-walk features feed GCN, SAGE, MLP, logistic, and propagation baselines. A gene's label is kept out of its own fold's training set. GCN uses symmetric graph normalization,
\begin{equation}\tilde A=D^{-1/2}(A+I)D^{-1/2},\qquad H^{(\ell+1)}=\sigma(\tilde A H^{(\ell)}W_\ell).\end{equation}
The CNN classifier orders discovery-selected genes using a PPI Fiedler vector; random ordering is an ablation. Cohort-level z-scoring does not make cross-tissue prediction a valid clinical test. Per held-out validation cohort, ranking performance is
\begin{equation}\operatorname{AUROC}=\Pr(s(X^+)>s(X^-)) + \tfrac12\Pr(s(X^+)=s(X^-)).\end{equation}
This probabilistic definition explains why AUROC is insensitive to decision thresholds yet remains sensitive to shifts in score ordering. The GNN task is disease-specific gene recovery, not patient classification. A published drug--disease graph prediction AUROC cannot be compared numerically with our gene-ranking AUROC. KG-Bench (\url{https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13171177/}; DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag159}) reports RGCN 0.908 on a temporal drug--disease association task; its targets and splits differ.

\subsection{Preeclampsia effect-estimator sensitivity}
The P3 ST3GAL2 decision used the registered DerSimonian--Laird pooled effect across all four fresh cohorts; it fails the one-sided down-regulation threshold ($p=0.3850$). As an estimator check, we recalculated from the same cohort effect sizes and variances with the independent \texttt{statsmodels} meta-analysis routine. Its DerSimonian--Laird result agrees numerically with the project implementation: $\hat\mu=-0.1034$, SE $0.3536$, and one-sided normal-reference $p=0.3850$. The iterative Paule--Mandel estimator returns $\hat\mu=-0.1016$, SE $0.3831$, $p=0.3955$. Leave-one-out iterative estimates also fail the $0.05$ down-direction threshold (one-sided $p$ range $0.0969$ to $0.6369$). Omitting the blood cohort yields the most favorable subset, $p=0.0969$; that is post-hoc and still negative. The independent-library check, generated from the same four cohort estimates, is in the PE P3 sensitivity CSV in the results folder. It does not add new cohorts. A small-$k$ random-effects model carries material uncertainty; choosing a different heterogeneity estimator cannot turn P3 into a discovery.

The queries are not equivalent; the previous zero cannot support a novel-biology claim. \subsection{P12-P13 placental challenges of a fixed six-gene panel}
We next checked the same frozen six DOWN directions in three placental GEO deposits, with the decisions registered before each deposit's expression matrix was opened. They are not new discoveries: the panel was chosen using old discovery and validation outcomes and examined in P10 and P11 before these studies. A fresh study-level outcome cannot restore the original prospective status. The studies also differ in disease severity, fetal-growth-restriction status, gestational timing and expression processing. The statistical unit is one woman in each of these three deposits, rather than the twin placenta in P10 or repeated draw in P11.

In GSE190971 we used only the thirteen placenta mRNA samples, seven PE and six controls, rather than the 10K and 150K extracellular-vesicle arms, which share participant identifiers with the placenta samples (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE190971}). On log$_2$(CPM+1), FES was down with Hedges $g=-2.800$ and uncorrected two-sided Welch $p=0.000372$, below the six-gene Bonferroni threshold $0.00833$. GPAT3 was up, $g=+0.161$, $p=0.757$; the other four genes were down. The registered all-six-down panel criterion therefore fails at five of six, despite the strong FES component. Recounting the repeated EV arms as independent people would be false replication. All six gene effects and per-GSM count mappings are in \texttt{results/pe\_plac\_p12*}.

\section{Limitations and next work}
Sample labels may be wrong where GEO metadata are incomplete; all included labels require audit. Cross-platform effect sizes standardize units but do not remove tissue, gestational-age, or case-definition confounding. Four PE cohorts differ in tissue, and a single PCOS cumulus cohort is insufficient for discovery. The exploratory PCOS gene signs in GSE304677 (17/18 measured) are not a registered confirmatory discovery; a post-hoc, direction-matched null gives $p=0.0021$, while GSE277906 does not pass the naive $p=0.05$ threshold. After registration, GSE155489 showed 16/17 signs and direction-matched $p=0.0139$, but PBMC GSE262735 had 11/17 signs ($p=0.1951$), pooled-library granulosa GSE193123 had 12/18 ($p=0.1795$), and a new 9-case/9-control follicular-granulosa cohort GSE293353 had 16/19 ($p=0.5884$ under its direction-matched null). These are mixed, not consistent validation. GSE173160 supplied 177,889 transcript IDs with no direct HGNC symbol matches and was excluded from this test by the registered mapping rule. A mostly upward selected gene set against an overall upward cohort shift makes a half-probability binomial null anti-conservative. One study can have multiple data depositions: unique accessions do not equal independent studies. We will count both levels and keep the accession-to-study mapping explicit. Only a comparison on matching published benchmark inputs and splits or an untouched, corrected, named discovery can satisfy the discovery gate. No clinical diagnostic inference is warranted.

\section{Interstitial cystitis: a registered cross-tissue stress test}
The original top-50 signature passed in only seven labeled GSE57560 bladder-tissue libraries. To test whether it traveled beyond that small setting, we froze the discovery ranking and sign before inspecting the GSE28242 matrix. The historical GSE28242 urine-sediment series provides five normal controls, five PBS patients without lesions, and three with Hunner lesions. No GSM overlaps the split cohorts. The exact label audit, gene-level effects, and stratum summary are in three \texttt{results/ic\_urine\_p7*} files. The GPL6244 probe map yields 43 of the fixed 50 measurable genes. We compare sign agreement against 10,000 random sets from measured, unselected genes while preserving the selected set's numbers of positive and negative discovery signs; each stratum uses the same frozen ranking and seed.

In the primary all-PBS contrast, 22 of 43 signs match, versus null mean 0.528, empirical $p=0.641$. The lesion-free subgroup has 15 of 43 matching, $p=0.991$. The three Hunner-lesion cases, contrasted with the same five controls, show 40 of 43 matching, $p=0.0001$ (the minimum resolution of this permutation test). These strata have shared controls and were not separate untouched confirmatory cohorts: the lesion-specific outcome cannot be selected after seeing the primary failure to claim an all-IC diagnostic signature. An exploratory exact permutation assigns three of the eight PBS patient labels to the lesion group in all $\binom{8}{3}=56$ possible ways, keeping the five controls fixed. The observed difference in sign-concordance fractions (lesion minus non-lesion) is 0.5814; two of 56 partitions are at least as large, yielding one-sided $p=0.0357$. This tests a subtype contrast under conditional exchangeability, not an independent cohort, and was devised after seeing P7. Forty-three concordant gene directions are not 43 independent patient observations. A physical-STRING graph check finds just one edge among the 35 selected IC genes represented in that graph; this does not support a claim that the panel forms a tightly connected physical-interaction module, nor does it prove statistical independence of expression effects. The split is consistent with the GSE28242 authors' published conclusion that the urine-sediment inflammatory signature was evident with Hunner lesions, not lesion-free disease (Blalock et al., 2012; DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.juro.2011.09.142}; data \url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE28242}). An independent, adequately sized pre-registered lesion-stratified study would test whether the fixed panel transports. \section{Position against published work}
A 2025 IC/BPS analysis pooled GSE11783, GSE28242, and GSE57560 across platforms, reported PLAC8, S100A8, and PPBP as candidate markers, and included an 8-case/10-control tissue check (\url{https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2025.1511529/full}). Its bladder tissue case selection and pooled batch correction also differ from our study-level holdout. This overlap sharply limits any claim that the observed Hunner-lesion split is new.

The purpose of a literature comparison is to define what our evidence can and cannot establish, not to import an unrelated accuracy number. A recent postpartum-expression study deposited GSE290313 and analyzed transcript abundance at pregnancy and postpartum timepoints separately, with additional medication-related exclusions. Its own stratification shows why a pooled perinatal-depression contrast may not transfer to a postpartum-only cohort. GSE290313 already appears in the locked validation addendum as postpartum and predictive pregnancy subsets. A later stricter no-SSRI postpartum subset is a sensitivity reanalysis of the same study, not fresh independent evidence. The overlap was detected before testing its reprocessed expression results. Source: \url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-025-03068-z} and its GEO accession GSE290313. We must not silently assign the same patient across pregnancy and postpartum to separate model folds.

A newly deposited PRIDE study, PXD080843 (14 September 2026), describes a prospective 64-person first-trimester serum proteomic case-control cohort (32 future preeclampsia cases, 32 controls) and targeted follow-up; its protein-level prognosis endpoint is not numerically comparable to our placental/blood expression gene-recovery ranking (\url{https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pride/ws/archive/v3/projects/PXD080843}). These are relevant biological context but their inclusion rules, populations, gene mappings and outcomes must be reconciled before any numerical benchmark comparison. Our four-cohort ST3GAL2 test deliberately includes blood and villus sensitivity, so its all-cohort value cannot be read as a replication of a placenta-only meta-analysis.

A relevant MetaboLights entry MTBLS12367 describes LC-MS profiling of human PCOS granulosa cells in a study of glutamine transport and androgen excess; its metabolite readout is distinct from our fixed 20-gene RNA sign panel and offers context, not replication (\url{https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/ws/studies/MTBLS12367}). A PCOS study published in January 2026 combined bulk and single-cell transcriptomics and reported a three-gene HLA-DRA/SRM/CTSL signature, with additional qPCR and western-blot checks in clinical samples (\url{https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13048-025-01956-0}). Its published signature and sample-level endpoint are different from our discovery-derived 20-gene directional sign test. Neither one small positive cumulus cohort nor the failure in a pooled ovarian granulosa cohort directly reproduces that published panel. A 2026 peripheral-blood PPD-versus-healthy postpartum case/control study reported 11 cases and 16 postpartum controls with 20 non-postpartum major depression comparators, but noted imbalance in postpartum duration and used nominal rather than multiplicity-adjusted p values for its 289 reported differential genes (\url{https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13590264/}). The paper lists BioProject PRJNA1456230 as the raw-data source; at this retrieval, the public ENA read-run query and NCBI BioProject accession search returned no record, so we cannot count, analyze, or claim external validation from those data. The article offers phenotype and publication context only. KG-Bench's temporal drug-disease link prediction used a relational graph and reports AUC 0.908; our disease-specific held-out gene-label ranking uses a different unit, label and split. The proper comparison would rerun our models on its exact data, task and split (or published baselines on ours) under the same evaluation metric. None of that head-to-head work has been completed.

\section{Threats to interpretation and reproducibility contract}
\subsection{Independent study versus independent record}
An accession is an identifier for a data object, not a statistical replicate. We separately list series, sample and platform identifiers in the machine manifest. The 1,202 individually inspected GSM records were used to verify sample identity and labels but are nested within their GSE studies; the explicit \texttt{gsm\_to\_study.csv} map is essential to prevent accidental inflation of the study count. Because a superseries may redeposit the same GSM, we also disallow a GSM in both discovery and held-out validation after the deterministic duplicate audit. The original pre-correction estimates remain in provenance but cannot be used as final validation.

\subsection{Protocol, tissue and phenotype}
A CellxGene human reference dataset of 75,042 normal trophoblast cells from decidua and placenta is catalogued as secondary data, rather than an independent preeclampsia case/control cohort (\url{https://cellxgene.cziscience.com/e/ecf2e08e-2032-4a9e-b466-b65b395f4a02.cxg/}). It illustrates the cell-composition context that bulk placenta mixes; it cannot validate ST3GAL2. Standardizing a within-cohort mean contrast removes measurement scale, not confounding. Different cell fractions, gestational weeks, fertility treatment, blood collection, pooling, medication and array probes can change the sign of a gene's effect. Preeclampsia results illustrate this: ST3GAL2's blood cohort points up while the registered direction was down. The placenta-only sensitivity remains below the decision threshold and is not a replacement for the prespecified four-cohort test. Pooling two patients per library in GSE193123 changes the sampling unit; the variance formula uses three case and three control libraries, not inferred individual measurements.

\subsection{Multiplicity and model-selection boundaries}
The BH formula controls expected false-discovery proportion only under its assumptions for the chosen family; it does not transform a post-hoc shortlist into a registered candidate. The first PCOS exploratory check using a half-probability binomial null looked more promising than the direction-matched null. We retain both numbers, identify the corrected test, and avoid choosing whichever looks favorable. Similarly, a disease panel selected after looking at held-out data would require a new untouched evaluation set. Prediction registration records the accession, feature set, direction, null, threshold and exclusions before processing its expression matrix.

\subsection{Runnable artifacts and audit trail}
The \texttt{ubiomark} Python package exposes reproducible project commands; scripts reconstruct GEO processing, random-effects meta-analysis, graph baselines, CNN evaluation, and figures from local accession downloads and captured annotations. the final split, excluded-series, preregistered-predictions, and per-cohort CSV files record the decisions needed to reproduce negatives as well as positives. Eighteen hermetic tests pass; one tiny-group variance test emits a degrees-of-freedom warning by design. Test passage shows code-path consistency, not external validity or absence of data leakage. Publication requires a fresh clean checkout, pinned sources and complete provenance re-run, and a comparable external benchmark or defensible independently validated discovery; that endpoint is still open.

\subsection{Seeded CNN rerun on the locked 17 held-out cohorts}
After correcting CNN weight initialization so that the declared seed applies before model construction, we reran every cohort in the old split rather than picking favorable rows. The corrected PPI-ordered CNN mean AUROC is $0.576230$, versus $0.575203$ for a random-order CNN and $0.627559$ for logistic regression. CNN minus logistic is $-0.051330$ averaged without cohort-size weights, or $-0.037865$ weighting by held-out sample counts. Across the seventeen cohorts the CNN wins seven, loses eight and ties two. A seed-fixed ten-thousand-resample cohort bootstrap of paired differences has a descriptive 95\% percentile interval $[-0.135824,0.029833]$; it does not establish population superiority in either direction because cohorts overlap disease families and differ in tissue, patient counts and assay. Fourteen of seventeen CNN holdout scores changed from the historical pre-fix table, while logistic scores were unchanged. The corrected row-level CSV and summary JSON are in the repository's \texttt{results/} folder, with the common stem \texttt{cnn\_crosscohort\_seeded}; the summary file ends in \texttt{\_summary.json}. The older figure and table are retained as historical provenance but are not the current corrected comparison. This remains an in-project loss, not a same-task published benchmark win.

\section{Benchmark comparability and quality-control ledger}
A request for a stronger research tool is not evidence that this tool is stronger. The user's operational threshold is either a measured improvement over the best relevant benchmark, or accuracy matching that benchmark together with a separately measured benefit. No current item-25 score meets either condition. The patient-classifier result in P33 is below the published Lasso test number on the same cohort split; the whole-project CNN mean is below its logistic control. The gene-recovery task uses contemporaneous Open Targets non-expression annotations as a proxy label, not subsequent clinical target success, and no published tool was run on that same target, split, and candidate universe. Even a high within-project AUROC cannot be compared with a drug--disease prediction score or a clinical-trial target score.

The closest open disease-to-target comparator inspected was OTRec (\url{https://github.com/LinialLab/OTRec}). Its released retrospective temporal predictions use past Open Targets evidence to rank later clinical-trial disease--target associations. We downloaded the authors' \texttt{S7-temporal\_predictions.parquet}, verified its SHA-256 \texttt{6a1e9457880a26231c35ff7e9b43d931a91953f3f521431eb75dc4e1ad48fa34}, and checked exact disease ontology identifiers for the ten project labels. Seven have no matching released pairs, Chagas has 32 pairs with no positives, interstitial cystitis has 17 with no positives, and only long COVID has both classes: 90 positives and 96 negatives among 186 uniquely identified genes. An empty exact-ID match is not proof of a biological absence; a future ontology equivalence must be source-checked rather than guessed. The released data have 403,132 total disease--target pairs and 16,074 positives across 11,624 disease IDs; these are pairs, not independent cohorts or new item-25 patient datasets.

On exactly the 186 long-COVID rows, our read-only \emph{evaluation of the authors' released scores} gives OTRec AUROC $0.939583$ and area under the precision--recall curve $0.874571$. Their released OTTree scores give $0.920370$ and $0.835192$; the past Open Targets scores are zero for every one of these pairs and yield the no-ranking AUROC $0.5$ and prevalence PR-AUC $90/186=0.483871$. These values are not a ubiomark model result and not the authors' cross-disease average; the high fraction of positives makes precision--recall averages sensitive to this candidate universe. The published output file and exact script are named in \texttt{results/otrec\_temporal\_comparability.md}. We inspected these labels while auditing source feasibility; they can no longer function as untouched validation for a model developed in response. A same-task comparison requires a genuinely independent temporal label period or a held-out benchmark fixed before model decisions, historical expression and graph features available prior to that period, identical disease/target universe and exclusions, and measured AUROC, PR-AUC, uncertainty, runtime and coverage for both systems.

TargetPro/TargetBench is another published reference for clinical-stage disease targets (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-47765-3}); its 38-disease five-fold evaluation reports area under the precision--recall curve against its own task, but the custom code is proprietary and available upon request. The disease and label sets cannot be assumed identical to ours. KG-Bench's temporal Open Targets network protocol (\url{https://github.com/cmbi/Benchmark_GNN_OpenTargets/}) predicts drug--disease links, not disease--gene links; it informs leakage checks but offers no directly comparable number. No claim of published-best accuracy, lower cost, broader comparable coverage or interpretability advantage follows from these sources alone.

The internal GNN implementation was also audited. Its earlier training routine seeded PyTorch \emph{after} model parameter initialization; two different initial RNG states yielded unequal predictions despite the same training seed. The corrected constructor now seeds before MLP, GCN and SAGE parameter creation, and two hermetic tests check identical initial weights, an unchanged caller RNG stream, and repeatable training. In the gene-label cross-validation, feature scaling previously used held-out gene features to compute a whole-universe mean and standard deviation; the revised benchmark fits these on training genes only and applies the frozen transform to its test fold. Graph topology remains transductive and labels come from the same evidence snapshot. Eighteen tests pass, but the old numerical \emph{gene-ranking} benchmark tables have \emph{not} been rerun after these changes. They must not be presented as corrected accuracy numbers, let alone a published benchmark break. The final all-label ranker and within-fold evaluation serve different purposes; a candidate score trained on all known proxy labels is not an out-of-sample claim.



\subsection{Completed corrected gene-label folds in three diseases}
To avoid discarding a long run at a platform timeout, we separated the corrected gene-ranking evaluation into one disease, one seed and one fold per invocation. The new runner \texttt{scripts/prioritize\_fold.py} reproduces the feature construction and fixed method hyperparameters of the original runner, fits feature scaling only on training genes, initializes MLP/GCN/SAGE parameters under the declared random seed, masks held-out labels from random-walk seeds, and fits the hybrid random-walk feature scaling on training genes. It writes a separate CSV for each completed fold, refusing to overwrite an existing shard. Source code and all thirty preeclampsia, leishmaniasis and postpartum-depression fold shards are committed; aggregated data are in three disease-named corrected CSVs under \texttt{results/prio/}. Six remaining disease labels in the original gene-ranking task do not have complete corrected folds here; the third completed label is postpartum depression, analyzed below. Nor does the new runner produce candidate rankings: its output is evaluation scores only.

In preeclampsia, 1,982 of 15,809 genes in the STRING-derived human physical graph meet the existing Open Targets non-expression evidence threshold. Two seeded repetitions of five-fold stratified gene-level splitting give a mean GCN AUROC $0.689634$ and PR-AUC $0.280923$. Random walk with restart (RWR) yields $0.662790$ and $0.259595$ on the identical fold test genes. The paired GCN-minus-RWR differences are $+0.026844$ AUROC and $+0.021328$ PR-AUC, with ten of ten AUROC and nine of ten PR-AUC fold differences positive. GraphSAGE has a slightly higher mean AUROC $0.690357$ but a lower PR-AUC $0.268002$ than GCN. Neither two-seed repetition nor method selection after examining it creates a new test cohort. The ten-fold paired bootstrap interval for GCN-minus-RWR AUROC $[0.020770,0.031959]$ is descriptive of reused folds, not an interval over independent patients or a claim of a general clinical gain.

Leishmaniasis supplies an informative conflict between metrics rather than a second uniform win. Its 537 positives occupy the same 15,809-gene graph under its disease-specific proxy labels. GCN averages AUROC $0.763481$ and PR-AUC $0.227723$, while RWR gives $0.776853$ and $0.178609$. The paired GCN-minus-RWR AUROC difference is $-0.013372$ with only two of ten fold wins; its PR-AUC difference is $+0.049114$ with ten of ten fold wins. A method can improve prioritization among rare positives yet worsen all-pair ranking; reporting only one chosen metric would hide a real tradeoff. GraphSAGE gives $0.766108$ AUROC, $0.211571$ PR-AUC. The fold bootstrap intervals are descriptive because the same underlying graph, source studies and current label snapshot recur across folds, and because labels rather than patients are the resampling unit.

The first two corrected reruns demonstrate that corrected GNN code can produce internally measurable comparisons and that the outcome is disease- and metric-dependent. They do \emph{not} satisfy the requested published-best threshold. The comparator is the project's RWR baseline, not a published tool run under the same candidate universe, evidence date and test outcomes. Labels are current Open Targets disease-gene links selected at the same broad time as some of the input evidence; network neighborhood and feature coverage may correlate with curation. A temporal benchmark would freeze pre-outcome sources, make untouched later disease-target labels available for both systems, match candidate universe and disease definitions, and then test AUROC and PR-AUC with an explicitly selected primary metric. The previously inspected OTRec released long-COVID label set has already been exposed to this investigation and cannot be retroactively repurposed as unseen validation. The corrected two-disease results support software debugging and a benchmark design, not a claim of drug-target clinical prediction or newly validated biomarkers.

\subsection{Corrected postpartum-depression graph-label folds: propagation wins}
The postpartum-depression disease label has only twenty-five positives among the 15,809 genes in the same physical-interaction graph. We completed the same corrected two-seed, five-fold gene-level evaluation used for preeclampsia and leishmaniasis, saving every fold before aggregation. This is an especially severe ranking-imbalance test: positive prevalence is $25/15{,}809=0.00158$, so an area under the precision--recall curve near a few hundredths should be judged against prevalence, not read as an apparently large absolute precision. The existing label definition is Open Targets non-expression evidence at threshold 0.1. It is not a postpartum clinical diagnosis, a later trial outcome, or an independent patient cohort.

Mean graph-convolution AUROC is $0.693703$ and PR-AUC $0.005920$. Random walk with restart (RWR), using only training-fold positive genes as seeds, reaches AUROC $0.803341$ and PR-AUC $0.027501$. The paired GCN-minus-RWR differences are $-0.109638$ AUROC and $-0.021581$ PR-AUC; GCN loses in all ten of ten folds on each metric. GraphSAGE reaches $0.655187$/$0.007225$; its hybrid with RWR reaches $0.795798$/$0.025485$, still below RWR on mean values. Logistic regression achieves $0.581668$/$0.011997$, showing that a method with weaker global AUROC can nevertheless have higher PR-AUC than the pure GCN in a very sparse-positive setting. These method rankings are descriptive within the current proxy task: the ten folds reuse the same graph and labels, and the two seeds do not create independent disease replications.

This negative cannot be turned into a GNN advantage by choosing an alternative metric after looking at results. It instead sets a disease-specific boundary on what the current expression-plus-graph architecture adds. The result is materially different from the preeclampsia fold comparison, where GCN exceeds RWR on both means, and leishmaniasis, where it has higher PR-AUC but lower AUROC. One cannot average across these label prevalences and clinical meanings as if three diseases were exchangeable patient prediction problems. The fold-level CSV, summary JSON and exact source script are in \texttt{results/prio/}; these newly corrected numbers replace the old pre-fix postpartum graph rows wherever the two conflict. Nothing in this test breaks a published clinical or disease-to-gene benchmark.

\subsection{A second ME/CFS cfRNA publication is not the P33 comparison}
A June 2026 Nature Communications study reuses GSE293840 plasma cfRNA to compare machine-learning workflows with language-model pipelines (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74077-x}). We downloaded the authors' 50-row ME/CFS accuracy table (\url{https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adb258/cfrna_ai_manuscript/main/data/joined_df_MECFS.csv}). In that published table, GLMNETLasso averages accuracy $0.579474$ and GLMNETRidge $0.599474$ across the authors' fifty test seeds; these are the authors' scores, not results for our tool. Their standard workflow uses training-set differential-abundance selection, DESeq2 variance-stabilizing transformation, five-fold training tuning and a training-optimized Youden threshold. Its ME/CFS selection rule is uncorrected differential-expression $p<.001$ with baseMean $>50$, then an assay-specific feature limit. Such a result is not numerically comparable with P33's $0.723214$ AUROC on thirty-one provided VST genes or its published source artifact's $0.810268$ AUROC.

The published repository also supplies sample-index CSVs in its end-to-end language-model archive (\url{https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adb258/cfrna_ai_manuscript/main/data/full%20pipeline/DONE_o3_MECFS_informed.zip}). We checked all fifty of those held-out indices: each identifies thirty-eight people, whereas P33's seed-22 source split holds out sixty of 168. Even the identically numbered seed 22 overlaps P33's test set in only fifteen people and includes twenty-three people from P33's training set. The union across fifty archive indices contains 147 unique people, all within P33's 168-person set; twenty-one P33 people never appear in any of the released held-out indices. Among those twenty-one, nine are ME/CFS men and twelve are controls (seven men, five women), but absence from this LLM archive is not proof of a published sample-exclusion rule. A partial population overlap is not a shared train/test evaluation. The archive is an LLM-run provenance source; its fifty different split files are not fifty independent cohort studies, and their sample indices do not reveal the standard-ML per-patient continuous scores or substitute for an exact rerun of the original feature-selection workflow.

This comparison has a positive implication for reproducibility, not for performance: patient IDs can expose split mismatch even when a seed number, disease name and GSE accession look identical. The ZIP SHA-256, CSV SHA-256, table means, all-fifty index audit and exact overlap counts are saved in \texttt{results/external/mecfs\_2026\_published\_accuracy\_comparability.json}. A future same-task test would need the authors' precise per-seed training/test indices, batch and site constraints, feature-selection procedure, classifier prediction convention and threshold before scoring our model on those same held-out people. Until then there is neither a demonstrated higher accuracy nor an equal-accuracy plus-point.

\subsection{Newer literature narrows postpartum and long-COVID novelty claims}
An August 2026 postpartum-depression study used proteomic and genetic summary data, Mendelian-randomization and transcriptomic or methylation association layers to prioritize SRA1 (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-026-00232-3}). Its reported PPD genome-wide association source covers 17,339 cases and 53,426 controls, while its two proteome resources summarize 35,559 Icelanders and 828 FinnGen participants. These figures identify distinct source populations and modalities, not a public postpartum whole-blood gene-expression holdout for our model. The study's SRA1 target and another 2026 MKRN1 target-prioritization report (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-026-03886-x}) weaken any blanket novelty assertion for genes found by a current-annotation graph. Neither source yields a like-for-like benchmark for our 25-positive, same-snapshot Open Targets gene-label recovery because they use inherited GWAS evidence and causal-interpretation assumptions rather than our fixed graph folds. The honest next test is to separate prior evidence dates and evaluate genuinely later targets, not to attach a causal label to our current graph score.

For long COVID, an August 2026 Communications Medicine study measured circulating microRNA ratios (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s43856-026-01854-6}). It recruited 206 long-COVID patients and 71 uninfected, unvaccinated healthy controls, trained a ratio-plus-age/sex logistic model with training AUROC $0.760$, and observed external-validation AUROC $0.676$ plus risk overestimation (calibration intercept $-1.88$, slope $0.77$). That is a real caution about apparent test performance, but not a baseline for our whole-blood mRNA, cfRNA or gene-network analyses: both the molecule type and healthy-versus-recovered comparator differ. A separate 2026 Scientific Reports cohort measured a small targeted set of PBMC mRNAs by qRT-PCR in fifty long-COVID and fifty recovered controls roughly ten months after infection (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-53455-x}). Its closer comparator is still a selected gene panel and another assay, with no inspected public patient-level matrix. These papers give explicit phenotype and assay choices for future prospective validation; they are not additional used accession records or an opportunistic source of a purported published-best break.

\subsection{Why metadata-rich cohorts still do not solve early PE prediction}
The PE panel's repeated late-placenta down-regulation is not interchangeable with early clinical prediction. GSE192902 plasma cfRNA at the first at-most-twelve-week draw supplied 127 women across three named internal arms, but the six-gene placental shortlist measured only five genes and missed its across-arm rule (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE192902}). The independent unit in that early-plasma analysis is a mother, not a gene, sequence read, repeat draw or internal dataset label. GSE272342's thirty-two twin placental specimens reduce to sixteen pregnancies, with seven affected pregnancies and nine controls: a birth-tissue PE signal may track fetal growth pathology as much as disease-specific hypertension (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE272342}). The pregnancy-level panel there agreed in four of six directions, so even that favorable tissue context did not meet the registered all-six rule. GSE148241 late-onset severe PE had six of six down signs and four corrected hits against normal term tissue, but early- versus late-gestation and case severity were not balanced by the available per-GSM fields (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE148241}). The same symbol FES appearing in several selected placenta tests is thus not evidence of a first-trimester maternal screening assay.

The design distinction can be made operational. A prediction intended at twelve weeks must lock its draw time before symptoms and diagnosis, use maternal blood obtainable at that time, and choose an external pregnancy-level cohort whose outcome is adjudicated later. A birth-placenta comparison can instead ask which disease-associated tissue states recur, conditional on gestational age, growth restriction, collection and placental pathology; it cannot answer the earlier decision problem without longitudinal evidence. The GSE303463 re-mapping explicitly aggregates older deposits and new material; because person-to-source membership is unresolved, apparent sample-level non-overlap does not prove independent participants (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE303463}). A postdiagnosis gestational-age adjustment may remove a mediator, not merely control a confounder. Neither a low conditional $p$ value nor a cross-archive duplicate record repairs cohort dependence.

Two source audits give a stopping rule rather than another opportunistic test. GSE295760's mRNA cohort description conflicts with its publicly labeled counts and with cross-modality tokens for four participants, so its early villus expression effects were excluded before inference (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE295760}). GSE262147's title \texttt{T\_10\_V1} contradicts its PE source/group fields; a second BioSamples record repeats the contradiction and its ENA run only proves sequencing identity, not diagnosis (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSM8158605}). Without a patient-to-library crosswalk, relabeling the single ambiguous library from its title or group would make the chosen contrast depend on an untested assumption. Their assay matrices may be real, yet neither is a clean untouched validation until the clinical mapping is obtained. This is why the accession tally stays separate from effect-used cohort counts.

\subsection{Long-COVID published-panel transport is a comparator-mismatch chain}
The long-COVID sequence P36--P38 illustrates why favorable signs in two cohort types do not amount to a published classifier break. The P36 source paper selected JAK1, CXCL8, BCL2L1, OSM, MAP3K8 and STAT3 in its own whole-blood population; these are \emph{published prior picks}, not six genes newly identified by this project (\url{https://www.nature.com/articles/s41590-025-02353-x}). A separate GSE226260 count arm has 103 libraries but only thirty-seven uniquely inferred participants at the selected comparison time: seventeen persistent-symptom cases versus twenty recovered controls (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE226260}). Five of the six genes point UP there, and the direction-matched random-panel tail is $p=.008099$; JAK1 reverses. The registered all-six criterion fails. That empirical tail describes a source-selected signature in a distinct arm with subject reuse risk, not an untouched diagnostic discovery. Repeated visits add within-person samples, not independent cases.

The P37 NanoString PBMC source measured a fixed, targeted 635-gene immune-exhaustion panel in fifteen long-COVID, eighteen healthy and fourteen ME/CFS patients (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE275334}; \url{https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.183810}). On its depositor-normalized scale, five of six published UP genes still point UP, but BCL2L1 reverses; only MAP3K8 meets the six-gene Bonferroni threshold, so the all-six rule again fails. Its healthy controls were not the infected-recovered comparators of P36. The PBMC cell mix, targeted assay and selected immunology panel each can affect rank and measured coverage. One favorable MAP3K8 result within a fixed, source-published set is descriptive recurrence, not a new marker or proof of disease specificity across control definitions.

The P38 source offers a more precise cell identity but even fewer people: twenty-five sorted 10x libraries from ten donors, with six long-COVID and four recovered controls (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE334857}). We prespecified one naive-CD4 pseudobulk per person and excluded additional memory and AIM-positive fractions from the primary test. Each retained sample has 152--1,387 nonempty cells, which improves count stability without raising the independent $n$ above ten. CXCL8 has zero naive-CD4 samples passing the expression cutoff and is not silently assigned a favorable sign; the fixed panel therefore fails its six-measured minimum before effects are selected. The results are a cross-cell-type stress test, not a head-to-head against the published whole-blood model. A future model would need a common infected-recovered endpoint, contemporaneous draw, distinct held-out people, identical assay features and untouched thresholds for both systems. These three arms instead identify concrete transport boundaries: disease comparator, blood-cell compartment and patient linkage.

\section{Cross-archive and annotation context: nine distinct services, zero new expression validations}
The project-level external-service count rose from thirty-one to forty by accessing, extracting and recording nine additional, distinct scientific resources. This satisfies the \emph{service-use} inventory as defined here; it does not increase the number of clinical validations or repair a missing benchmark. The source requests, exact identifiers and narrow summaries are in the committed external-results ledger, which maps one canonical service to each use. Some services aggregate the same upstream observations. We distinguish service count from independent evidence units rather than treating a cross-archive mirror as a new participant.

\subsection{Pathway annotations disagree without deciding the six-gene preeclampsia panel}
The six previously frozen P9/P44 genes are FES, GPAT3, FURIN, GRAMD1A, ZNF467 and LPGAT1, all predicted DOWN. WikiPathways' live JSON search corpus at retrieval had 2,218 pathways, including 1,131 human pathways (\url{https://www.wikipathways.org/json/findPathwaysByText.json}). Exact human gene-node membership returned four pathways for FES, three for GPAT3, four for FURIN, none for GRAMD1A or ZNF467, and one for LPGAT1. For FES, TSLP, IL11 and IL4 signaling as well as axon guidance occur in this annotation set. We did not test over-representation against a degree-matched gene background, nor disease-associated expression in these pathways. Source data mix curation and pathway selection; the membership count is not an effect size.

A second, distinct pathway source, KEGG REST (\url{https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/rest/keggapi.html}), returned fewer and differently named links after unique pinned-HGNC-symbol-to-Entrez mapping: FES one (axon guidance), GPAT3 three (glycerolipid, glycerophospholipid and aggregate metabolism), FURIN two (HIV-1 viral life cycle and cornified envelope formation), LPGAT1 one (glycerophospholipid), and none for GRAMD1A or ZNF467. This comparison shows that a candidate's apparent biological story changes with resource coverage. The sparse direct annotations cannot be converted into PE-specific mechanisms; the project cannot select whichever database sounds more compelling after seeing its panel outcomes. Both queries give context to discuss possible pathways, but the P44 archival-placenta DOWN-panel reversal still stands.

\subsection{Ontology and phenotype sources establish identifiers, not specimen truth}
Monarch v3 identifies the exact PE term MONDO:0005081, maps it to EFO:0000668 and other ontology identifiers, and lists twenty-seven direct phenotype terms (\url{https://api-v3.monarchinitiative.org/v3/api/entity/MONDO:0005081}). Its direct correlated-gene subset returned only CORIN, FLT1 and STOX1, all tagged to Orphanet as primary source (\url{https://api-v3.monarchinitiative.org/v3/api/entity/MONDO:0005081/biolink:CorrelatedGeneToDiseaseAssociation?limit=20&direct=true}). None overlaps the six fixed targets. That narrow absence is \emph{not} a proof that the six are novel or biologically irrelevant: the category and source filters exclude most published association types. Our conclusion is bounded to this direct Orphanet-derived subset.

We independently parsed the versioned September 2026 Human Phenotype Ontology annotation file (\url{https://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/hp/hpoa/phenotype.hpoa}), retaining forty-five rows with preeclampsia or pre-eclampsia in the disease name. They fall under ORPHA:275555 (twenty-seven rows) and three OMIM entries: 189800 (eleven), 609404 (four), and 614595 (three). During inspection we discovered that an initially considered OMIM:614592 is named \emph{Bent bone dysplasia syndrome}, and excluded it rather than retaining a plausible-looking but wrong identifier. HPO and Monarch share upstream rare-disease annotation provenance, so their similar clinical phenotype context is not independent PE biomarker support. Neither resource gives a sample-level clinical chart that could resolve the GSE262147 title/group inconsistency.

\subsection{Tissue occurrence, single-cell reference, and assay availability are different units}
TISSUES' human curated knowledge channel supplied 339,455 gene-tissue records (\url{https://download.jensenlab.org/human_tissue_knowledge_full.tsv}; format guide \url{https://tissues.jensenlab.org/Downloads}). Exact frozen symbol and tissue-name matches for blood, placenta, trophoblast, decidua, plasma, serum and PBMC yield one curated FURIN-in-blood row; the other five fixed genes have no row under these exact names. This is a heavily filtered \emph{knowledge-channel occurrence} check, not proof that FURIN is higher or lower in affected patients. A missing row does not imply protein absence; the large experimental and text-mining channels were not analyzed.

The Human Cell Atlas project \url{https://explore.data.humancellatlas.org/projects/ea9eec5a-4fc2-4c58-94d0-2fcb598732bc} describes PE/normal blood PBMC single-cell profiling with an estimated 80,429 cells, but only five donors in its project summary (\url{https://service.azul.data.humancellatlas.org/index/projects/ea9eec5a-4fc2-4c58-94d0-2fcb598732bc}). Its 10x 5-prime cell counts should not be read as 80,429 independent cases. We only screened metadata and did not download a matrix, separate pregnancy from postpartum at donor level, or test any of the six signs. It is a candidate source for a later cell-type-specific analysis with a verified donor crosswalk, not a new validation result here.

BindingDB's UniProt P07332 query for FES returned fifty-three affinity records representing forty distinct monomer identifiers, with twenty-seven Kd, twenty-four IC50, one Ki and one EC50 entries (\url{https://bindingdb.org/rest/getLigandsByUniprots?uniprot=P07332&cutoff=1000&response=application%2Fjson}). Values include a right-censored \texttt{>1000}; assay types, experimental designs and chemical series vary. We therefore report the count and heterogeneity, not a pooled potency. Biochemical binding is different from placental bioavailability, safety in pregnancy, clinical treatment or a patient-level biomarker. The FES preeclampsia expression sign cannot be rescued by counting compounds assayed against its protein.

\subsection{P45 cross-archive trace reproduces a contradiction rather than resolving it}
GSE262147's blood specimen GSM8158605 has title \texttt{T\_10\_V1} but the GEO source and experimental-group fields call it PE at diagnosis (\url{https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSM8158605&targ=self&form=text&view=full}). Its publisher article explicitly uses T for controls, PE for affected pregnancies, and V1 for the initial diagnosis blood collection (\url{https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2025.1576240/full}). This establishes the vocabulary but not whether this individual library was mislabeled in its title or its source field. The publisher XML supplementary captions do not supply a case-by-library crosswalk.

The EMBL-EBI BioSamples record SAMN40568562 names the same GSM and repeats both contradictory fields (\url{https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biosamples/samples/SAMN40568562}). It is mirrored depositor metadata, not a second clinical diagnosis. ENA Portal resolves that exact BioSample to one RNA-Seq run, SRR28411428, in PRJNA1090471 (\url{https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/portal/api/search?result=read_run&query=sample_accession%3D%22SAMN40568562%22&fields=run_accession%2Csample_accession%2Cstudy_accession%2Cexperiment_accession%2Clibrary_strategy%2Cfastq_ftp&format=json&limit=10}); it confirms sequencing identity, not which clinical group T\_10\_V1 belonged to. We did not download FASTQ or use the conflicting specimen for differential effects. All fifty-three GEO GSM records were individually screened against expression columns before this cross-archive trace; the P45 cohort remains paused, and none of those fifty-three are counted as \emph{effect-used} in the 1,325-accession manifest. Multiple service lookups of one patient cannot become multiple independent datasets.

Taken together, the nine additional services answer distinct questions about pathway coverage, ontology precision, tissue references, a potential PBMC archive, sample-to-run identity and ligand assays. The findings mostly sharpen boundaries on claims, not produce positive patient predictions. The 40-service inventory is auditable; the scientific performance gate remains open.


\section{Limitations of the fixed sign-panel test}
For the P8 PCOS panel, a naive null that each of nineteen measured genes independently agrees with probability one-half would give a deceptively small tail probability for 16 agreements. But the panel was constructed from discovery meta-effects with eighteen positive and one negative direction among the measured set, and those signs are not equiprobable under a cohort-wide shift. The direction- and discovery-coverage-matched null asks a narrower, more honest question: among measured genes with the same direction mix and at least four discovery cohorts, how often does an equally sized random set reach sixteen matches? It does so frequently ($p=0.5884$). The sampled null counts are archived rather than replaced by a fitted theoretical curve. This also warns that a signature's empirical p is conditional on its gene universe and cannot be compared directly with another tissue where the measurable genes differ.

The IC P7 study illustrates a separate limitation. The same urine-sediment cohort includes eight PBS cases, but three have Hunner lesions and five do not. An all-case disease label mixes them; a large lesion-only result on three participants is not a rescue of the all-case failure. The registered primary all-case test returned $p=0.6406$ against its direction-matched gene-set null. The exact post-result reallocation of lesion labels across all 56 possible three-of-eight case partitions yielded two as extreme as the observed lesion-versus-non-lesion contrast. That conditional number describes one historical cohort and selected panel, not an estimate of sensitivity in a future population. The original GEO study already characterized the lesion-specific inflammatory signal; a claim of novelty would be particularly inappropriate. The study also changes specimen from bladder tissue to urine sediment. These two effects are entangled, so the result does not isolate whether tissue or phenotype caused non-transport.

Across diseases, the weak evidence can be mistaken for a strong pattern if the units are blurred: 1,325 counted accession records are not 1,202 independent cohorts; ten gene-fold scores are not ten distinct external benchmarks; three pooled libraries per group are not six independent individuals per group; forty-three agreeing genes are not forty-three patients; a null gene-set score is not a clinical AUROC. Each claim should name its observational unit. A future study can falsify this project's fixed panels by preregistering specimen, cell type, phenotype definition, sample mapping, model and threshold, then collecting a patient-level cohort not chosen for a favorable result. Until then, the repository is a reproducible research tool and negative-results account, not a diagnostic product.
