# Chagas: proposed novelty test, not a result

**Current verdict:** No new named biological discovery, validated new method, or demonstrated same-task advantage. This is an unproven research direction, not a novelty claim or finished project.

## Falsifiable candidate contribution
A progression marker with measured added value over *existing* parasite-load, immune-protein and clinical predictors in baseline seropositive asymptomatic patients, rather than another late-stage heart-damage or infection classifier. This is a candidate aim, not novel by itself: prior prospective biomarkers already exist.

## What would establish or refute it
Train only on baseline seropositive asymptomatic participants whose future cardiac outcome is adjudicated; require an untouched longitudinal cohort, compare against existing parasite-load and inflammation-protein prognostic models plus clinical factors on the *same* people and endpoint, and quantify calibration and clinical decision benefit. The 33 cross-sectional GSE244827 blood libraries cannot substitute for a time-to-event outcome. Predeclare the endpoint, exclusion rules, analysis code and comparator before examining the held-out outcome; retain null and negative results. If the effect fails, the proposed contribution fails rather than becoming a post-hoc new marker.

## Why present evidence falls short
P31 heart-to-blood signs were fragile on leave-one-person-out and direction-conflicted; cross-sectional status is not progression. Published 2024 work already found baseline parasite DNA and 47 immune proteins associated with ten-year decline among 21 progressors and 31 controls (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38203212/). A 2021 blood-biomarker study reported cross-sectional asymptomatic-versus-symptomatic discrimination (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34479416/). A cohort of 499 seropositive former blood donors established ten-year cardiac outcomes but no omics crosswalk is established here (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23393012/). These studies constrain novelty; no biomarker superiority or patient-level data access is claimed. Source lead: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE244827. A source lead is not proof of novelty, matching participants, or clinical utility.
