Q1 — H1 redesign: strongest defensible path

Pick: (d) something else — abandon “benchmark-beat” as the primary claim and redesign around a locked, molecule-matched discovery + validation framework.

The current H1 is structurally unsalvageable as a benchmark-beat claim. The failure is not just the model; the comparison is scientifically mismatched:

Published benchmark: ELISA protein panel, n=60, C/S vs C/A.

Your model: serum miRNA, n=150 training universe, n=45 frozen test.

Different analyte biology, feature dimensionality, cohort, preprocessing assumptions.

A negative R² of −55.7 is not a “tuning problem”; it indicates catastrophic instability on the frozen test. With n=45, adding flexibility risks overfitting rather than recovering signal.

The redesign I would accept:

Primary claim: “Can serum miRNA signatures stratify Chagas cardiomyopathy severity and identify biologically coherent candidate pathways?”
Not: “Can miRNAs beat protein biomarkers?”

A stronger registered endpoint:

Keep binary CCC vs indeterminate only as a negative benchmark audit.

Make the primary model:

ordinal severity prediction (because disease biology is progressive),

nested cross-validation,

pre-specified metrics:

ordinal c-index,

macro-AUC,

calibration,

bootstrap confidence intervals.

Compare against:

clinical baseline (age/sex if available),

single-marker models,

published miRNA literature signatures if available.

Why the options fail:
(a) Keep binary, switch metric, log1p transform + class weighting

Weak.

This is the classic post-hoc rescue attempt.

Problems:

The preregistered pipeline failed.

Changing metric after seeing failure changes the question.

Log transformation may be biologically justified, but it cannot retroactively repair a broken benchmark.

Class weighting does not create information where the feature set lacks signal.

It might improve AUC from 0.588, but it does not restore a credible benchmark-beat claim.

(b) Re-register ordinal severity classifier

Closest of the listed options, and scientifically correct, but incomplete.

This is the direction I would choose if you explicitly abandon the benchmark-beat framing.

Why it is stronger:

Your own H2 already suggests severity-associated biology.

miRNAs are likely better suited to gradients than binary thresholds.

CCC is heterogeneous; mild/moderate/severe contains information the binary collapse destroys.

Weakness:

Benchmarking against DEMs is not a performance benchmark.

“Replication of direction” is weaker than a predictive advance.

Still, this is the best listed option.

(c) Published-marker subset performance

Not viable.

You cannot benchmark miRNAs against proteins without a bridge.

A reviewer will say:

“You changed the measurement space and then declared victory because the old marker panel cannot compete.”

Correct criticism.

Q2 — H2 replication routing

Ranking:

1. (a) miRNA → target genes → direction check in mRNA cohorts

Best route.

This is the only biologically defensible cross-platform bridge.

But it must be done carefully.

A weak implementation:

“TargetScan predicts miR-182 targets; those genes change in RNA-seq.”

That is basically fishing.

A rigorous implementation:

Lock candidate miRNAs first (already mostly done).

Obtain experimentally supported targets where possible:

miRTarBase,

CLIP-supported interactions.

Create predicted direction:

Example:

miR-182 decreases in severe CCC.

Therefore validated miR-182 targets should show increased expression if canonical repression dominates.

Test whether those targets are enriched among genes changing in GSE244827/GSE203525.

Do:

gene-set enrichment,

permutation null preserving gene-set size,

FDR correction.

A positive result would be much stronger than simple feature replication.

2. (c) Drop miRNA-level replication and convert H2 to gene-level convergence only

Second best.

Actually, this may be the safest publication-grade option.

The problem:

You cannot claim “miRNA replication” when no comparable serum miRNA cohort exists.

A strong reframing:

“Serum miRNA candidates converge on conserved Chagas cardiac remodeling pathways.”

That is defensible.

Loss:

You lose biomarker specificity.

You gain biological credibility.

Judges usually reward this trade.

3. (b) Same molecule replication in placenta small RNA cohort

Third.

Technically same molecule, biologically poor match.

Placenta congenital Chagas ≠ adult chronic cardiomyopathy.

A reviewer would immediately ask:

“Why should fetal/placental miRNA biology validate adult cardiac severity?”

You might find interesting conserved infection-response miRNAs, but it is not a severity replication.

4. (d) Internal monotonic trend substitute

Fourth.

Useful as supporting evidence only.

A monotonic trend:

control < mild < moderate < severe

is valuable, but it is still the same cohort.

Calling it replication would be rejected.

It is:

dose-response evidence,
not

independent validation.

Q3 — Single strongest novelty upgrade

The strongest improvement:

Build a Chagas multi-omics severity atlas that converts serum miRNA changes into mechanistic cardiac remodeling hypotheses using causal triangulation.

Not another classifier.

The current project's weakest point is that it risks becoming:

“We ran ML on GEO and found miRNAs.”

There are thousands of those.

Your unique asset is not the model. It is the integration opportunity:

16 GEO series

serum miRNA severity cohort

blood RNA-seq

cardiac cell models

methylation

pharmacogenomics

The novel contribution could be:

“A severity-linked miRNA regulatory network predicting molecular transition states from asymptomatic infection to cardiomyopathy.”

Concrete workflow:

Step 1 — Define severity-associated miRNAs

Already done.

Step 2 — Map regulatory consequences

For each candidate:

miRNA
↓
validated targets
↓
RNA-seq differential expression
↓
pathway activation
↓
cardiac phenotype relevance

Step 3 — Add pharmacogenomic layer

This is where novelty appears.

Ask:

Do severity-associated regulatory modules overlap with druggable pathways?

Examples:

fibrosis pathways,

mitochondrial dysfunction,

inflammatory signaling,

cardiomyocyte stress responses.

Step 4 — Create a reproducible “severity module score”

Instead of:

“miR-182 predicts CCC”

claim:

“A conserved regulatory module tracks transition toward cardiac disease.”

That survives the failed classifier.

Judge verdict

Current state:

Prediction: weak.
Biology: promising.
Novelty: moderate.

The failed H1 actually helps if handled correctly because you can say:

“Serum miRNA alone did not reproduce a protein biomarker classifier; however, multi-omic integration revealed conserved regulatory programs associated with severity.”

That is a scientifically mature pivot.

The biggest mistake would be trying to resurrect the binary classifier. The strongest project is no longer a biomarker competition; it is a mechanistic atlas of Chagas progression.
