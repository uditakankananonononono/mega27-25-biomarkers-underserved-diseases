!Series_summary = Inflammatory heart diseases affect millions worldwide, but the gene–environment interactions determining why only some exposed individuals develop cardiomyopathy remain poorly understood. Chagas disease provides a tractable model: despite persistent Trypanosoma cruzi seropositivity, only approximately 30% of infected individuals develop chronic Chagas cardiomyopathy (CCC), in which T cell-derived interferon (IFN)-γ contributes to myocardial mitochondrial dysfunction. Rare heterozygous mitochondrial-gene variants co-segregate with CCC in multicase pedigrees but remain clinically silent in seronegative carriers. We investigated whether the dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) R135C variant sensitizes cardiomyocytes to cytokine-induced mitochondrial dysfunction. We generated two complementary human cardiomyocyte models: heterozygous DHODH R135C knock-in AC16 cells and patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes with isogenic correction of the variant. Cells were exposed to IFN-γ alone or in combination with tumor necrosis factor-α and evaluated using mitochondrial functional assays and integrated proteomic, transcriptomic, and metabolomic profiling. DHODH R135C cardiomyocytes exhibited baseline mitochondrial stress that was amplified by cytokine exposure, including impaired bioenergetics, oxidative stress, mitochondrial fragmentation, and suppression of mitochondrial homeostatic pathways. These changes were accompanied by cytoplasmic double-stranded DNA accumulation and partially cGAS-dependent amplification of CXCL10 and interferon-stimulated-gene expression. Patient-derived R135C cardiomyocytes reproduced the principal mitochondrial abnormalities, which were rescued by isogenic correction. Increased CXCL10, CXCL9, and CXCR3 expression in CCC myocardium supported in vivo relevance. These findings identify DHODH R135C as a latent mitochondrial vulnerability unmasked by inflammatory cytokines and suggest a feed-forward interaction between cardiomyocyte dysfunction and IFN-γ-producing immune cells. This GEO Series contains the bulk RNA-sequencing data generated from the AC16 and patient-derived cardiomyocyte models.
!Series_overall_design = Bulk RNA sequencing was performed in two human cardiomyocyte model systems: isogenic AC16 clones 1.184 (DHODH rs201230446 C/C) and 1.27 (heterozygous p.Arg135Cys, C/T), and patient-derived iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes from line 6135 (heterozygous p.Arg135Cys, C/T) and its CRISPR-corrected isogenic clone 6135-H1 (C/C). Each line was analyzed under untreated and inflammatory stimulation conditions. AC16 cells were stimulated with IFN-γ and TNF-α, whereas iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes were stimulated with IFN-γ alone. Four biological replicates were analyzed per experimental group, yielding 32 paired-end RNA-seq libraries.
