\begin{abstract}
Interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome with Hunner lesions (HIC) is a
distinct inflammatory bladder disease whose identification still rests on
cystoscopy, not on a molecular marker. This report does four things, in order, and keeps
every negative on the record. First, a duplicate-sample and mislabel audit shows
the historical top-50 IC expression panel does not survive correction: in the
post-audit two-source replication the old and new top-50 lists share zero genes,
no gene reaches BH $q<.05$, and the registered sign-agreement threshold fails
(43/50, $p=.0469$ against a .025 bar). Second, we acquire and individually
verify 98 new accession records, bringing the lane to 153 accession-backed
records with honest nesting labels; the statistical evidence behind them rests
on 25 paired HIC patients, 13 BCG samples, 13 urine-sediment samples and 20
EV-miRNA subjects, and the record count is an audit figure, not a replication
unit. Third, we preregister a module-level test on
the 2025 paired Hunner-lesion RNA-seq cohort (GSE238208; 25 patients, lesion and
non-lesion biopsies, plus 13 BCG inflammatory-cystitis samples): the lesion
program replicates across patients (19/25, $p=.0073$) but is shared with BCG
inflammation, so the Hunner-specific gate fails. Fourth, a locked amendment
tests the net-of-BCG residual: a BCG-adjusted, G2/M-associated transcriptional
residual is reproducible across held-out patients within this cohort (18/25,
$p=.0216$). The locked specificity bound is very wide (compatible with no
specificity and with strong specificity), and two pre-specified exploratory
urinary transfers are inconclusive - one small directionally consistent signal
carried substantially by a single high sample, and one non-significant
EV-miRNA test. Forty-three external
scientific services annotate the program (41 under the most conservative
recount). No clinical test, named discovery, or benchmark break is claimed; this
is a working research manuscript, not a diagnostic claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Disease background and why the old panel had to be re-audited}
Interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome (IC/BPS) splits clinically into
Hunner-lesion disease (HIC), an inflammatory phenotype with ulcerative lesions
visible at cystoscopy, and non-lesion bladder pain syndrome (BPS), which shares
pain but not the lesion biology. BCG intravesical therapy for bladder cancer
produces an iatrogenic inflammatory cystitis that is the most important
molecular comparator: any ``Hunner signature'' that is equally present in BCG
cystitis is a general severe-inflammation signature, not a Hunner marker. The
historical in-program IC panel descended from two array series (GSE621,
GSE11783). The program-wide audit found two IC-derived mock-APF samples in
GSE621 had been treated as normal controls, and that GSE11783's ten IC
libraries are nested in five patients, so library-level inference was not
patient-level. The post-audit sensitivity (Section~\ref{sec:twosource}) is
therefore the correct statement of the historical panel: it is negative.

\section{The corrected two-source replication is negative}\label{sec:twosource}
After the audit corrections, discovery was restricted to GSE621 and GSE11783
with mislabeled and nested samples handled, and sensitivity was recomputed in
the previously seen GSE57560 (so it cannot be called fresh independent
replication). Of 3,326 genes measured in both sources, the new top-50 shares
zero genes with the old top-50; the best new-cohort BH value is $q=.418$ with
no gene under $.05$; and the validation sign agreement is 43/50 (86\%) against
an empirical null mean of 75.1\%, $p=.0469$, which fails the registered $.025$
threshold. The historical positive panel is therefore recorded as a ledger
negative, not rewritten into a weaker claim.

\subsection{Audit and sensitivity arithmetic, exactly as computed}
The reproducibility detail a reviewer needs to rerun this verdict is as
follows. The frozen historical result (T1) stands on the prior sample-level
discovery: 40/40 sign agreements in the original 6-vs-8 and 10-vs-6 contrasts,
left intact as a historical artifact. Post-audit, discovery was restricted to

\section{Preregistered module framework (P48)}
\label{sec:framework}
The redirect after the negative re-audit was preregistered before any testing.
Six candidate biology modules and one known-biology covariate module were locked
with fixed gene sets (Table~\ref{tab:modules}) from public libraries (MSigDB
Hallmark via Enrichr; GO Biological Process 2025 for the pain module,
GO:0019233, 21 genes). The design uses the paired structure of GSE238208: 25
HIC patients each contribute a lesion and a non-lesion biopsy, and the 13 BCG
samples form a fixed comparator block.

For training fold $T$ and gene $g$, per-gene $z$-scores are computed over the
48 training HIC columns only:
\begin{equation}
z_{g,s}=\frac{x_{g,s}-\bar x_{g,T}}{\hat\sigma_{g,T}}.
\end{equation}
Module $m$'s per-patient paired effect is the mean member-gene $z$ difference:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{m,i}=\overline{z}_{m,\mathrm{lesion},i}-\overline{z}_{m,\mathrm{nonlesion},i},
\qquad
e_m=\frac{1}{|T|}\sum_{i\in T}\Delta_{m,i}.
\end{equation}
A module enters the Gate-1 score with weight $+1$ iff its mean paired effect is
positive and a one-sided paired $t$-test over training patients gives $p<.05$;
the held-out patient score is
\begin{equation}
D_i=\sum_{m:\,\mathrm{selected}} \Delta_{m,i}.
\end{equation}
M0 (interferon-$\gamma$ inflammatory) is an excluded covariate: if M0 alone
passes and M1--M6 fails, the result is declared known-inflammatory rediscovery,
a novelty negative by construction.

\section{Expression processing and verification protocol}
The author FPKM matrix of GSE238208 is used as deposited; no re-quantification
was attempted, and the deposited values are treated as the study's own
summary. Within each leave-one-patient-out fold, $z$-scores are recomputed
over the 48 training columns only, so no held-out patient contributes to any
normalization constant, module weight, or threshold. Module gene sets are used
exactly as committed; coverage is reported per module (Table~\ref{tab:modules})
and never silently filled. For the urinary legs, platform annotation is the
bottleneck and is therefore evidenced: A2.3a maps M6 symbols through the full
GPL6244 platform table (190/190 recovered), and A2.3b maps the 464 measured
MIMAT accessions to mature miRNA names through miRBase \texttt{mature.fa}
(2{,}789 human entries). Every fetch URL is logged in the result JSON at
analysis time, per the amendment. ChatGPT consults (rounds 1--2) were used in
redirect mode only; verbatim prompts and responses are preserved in
\texttt{redirect/}, all numerical thresholds were recomputed locally, and one
incorrect threshold suggested by the consult ($\geq21/25$) was corrected to
$\geq18/25$ with the correction recorded in \texttt{redirect/round2\_meta.json}.

\subsection{Inference procedures}
All gated statistics were computed with the same three primitives. (1)
Leave-one-patient-out scoring: for each of the 25 patients in turn, module
gene weights and any reference offsets are refit on the other 24 patients, the
held-out patient's lesion and non-lesion libraries are scored without that
patient contributing to any parameter, and $D$ is the lesion-minus-non-lesion
difference of the module composite. (2) Sign inference: the primary statistic
is the count of positive $D$ values, tested by an exact one-sided binomial
against $p_0=0.5$; the locked pass bar is $\geq 18/25$ with $p<0.025$ (the
count tail, not an asymptotic approximation). (3) Effect-size context: a
bootstrap 95\% confidence interval on the median $D$ is reported alongside,
and for the urine legs - where the design is a group contrast, not a paired
sign test - a one-sided permutation null with 20,000 shuffles and fixed seed
20260926. No other inferential machinery is used anywhere in the locked
analysis; every number quoted in the gates derives from these primitives
applied to the committed fold and composite files.

\subsection{Module coverage and symbol nomenclature}
Of the 200 committed M6 genes, 190 are present in the author FPKM matrix. The
ten absent - H2AX, H2AZ1, H2AZ2, H2BC12, JPT1, KMT5A, KNL1, MAP3K20, NSD2,
TENT4A - are older or alias symbol forms (for example H2AX vs H2AFX, JPT1 vs
HN1, KNL1 vs CASC5) whose nomenclature does not match the matrix's symbols.
No remapping was attempted after the lock: the committed gene list governs,
coverage is reported, and the missing ten are treated as unmeasured rather than
recovered by post-lock alias judgment. The same rule produced the other
modules' coverage in Table~\ref{tab:modules}.

\section{Gate 1: the lesion axis is reproducible across held-out patients}
Leave-one-patient-out over the 25 HIC patients, refitting every quantity per
fold, gives 19/25 held-out patients with $D_i>0$ (exact one-sided binomial
$p=.0073$, threshold $.025$), median $D=0.273$ with bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.068,0.644]$. The frozen weight rule selected only module M2 (ECM/EMT
remodeling) in the training folds. The M0-only novelty control fails (14/25,
$p=.345$), so the pass is not known-inflammatory rediscovery. The frozen rule selected M2 in all 25 of 25 training folds (no other module was ever
selected), and held-out $D$ ranges from $-0.687$ to $1.546$ with median $0.273$.
Per-fold values are in Appendix~\ref{app:folds}.

\section{Gate 2: the reproducible axis is shared with BCG inflammation}
Applying the frozen Gate-1 score to the BCG block is negative for Hunner
specificity: the score is higher in BCG (0.305) than in Hunner lesions (0.181),
Hedges $g=-0.260$, permutation $p=.776$ against the locked pass bar
($g\geq0.8$, $p<.025$). The replicating lesion axis is therefore severe
inflammatory-remodeling biology, not Hunner-specific biology. This is a kept
negative, and it motivates the residual design rather than a re-thresholded
claim.

\section{Amendment A2: net-of-BCG residual program}
The amendment was locked before any A2 testing. For module $m$, the BCG
component over the fixed comparator block is
\begin{equation}
b_m=\frac{1}{13}\sum_{s\in\mathrm{BCG}}\overline{z}_{m,s},
\end{equation}
and the residual module effect is $A_m=e_m-b_m$. A module enters the residual
score with weight $+1$ iff $A_m>0$ and the one-sided paired $t$-test of
$\Delta_{m,i}-b_m$ over training patients gives $p<.05$; the held-out residual
is
\begin{equation}
D^{\mathrm{res}}_i=\sum_{m:\,\mathrm{selected}}\left(\Delta_{m,i}-b_m\right).
\end{equation}
BCG never enters a HIC training fold; the block is exchangeable.

\section{Gate A2.1: the G2/M residual is reproducible across held-out patients}

\subsection{Per-patient effect narrative}
Across the 25 leave-one-patient-out folds, the Gate 1 lesion-versus-non-lesion
contrast (M2) has median $D=0.273$ and range $[-0.687,+1.546]$, with 19 of 25
patients positive. The strongest individual effects are patients 25
($D=1.546$), 18 ($1.139$) and 13 ($1.126$); the six negative patients are led
in magnitude by 24 ($-0.687$), 9 ($-0.543$) and 21 ($-0.466$). The A2.1
net-of-BCG residual (M6) has median $D_{\mathrm{resid}}=0.180$ and range
$[-0.680,+2.047]$, with 18 of 25 positive. Its three strongest patients are
21 ($2.047$), 24 ($1.158$) and 9 ($0.888$) - precisely the three most negative
patients of the M2 contrast. The anti-correspondence is not an artifact of the
residual construction: the residual subtracts the fixed BCG offset, identical
for every patient, so it cannot invert a per-patient ranking by itself. What
the folds show is that the patients whose lesion tissue looks least like
inflammatory remodeling are the ones whose tissue shows the strongest G2/M
cell-state program relative to BCG inflammation. This is descriptive only; no
test was run on the inversion itself.

Only module M6 (G2/M checkpoint, 190 matrix-covered genes) is selected in every
fold. The residual contrast meets the locked bar exactly: 18/25 held-out
patients positive, $p=.0216$ ($P(X\geq18\mid25,.5)=.02164$), median residual
$0.180$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.0027,0.423]$ excluding zero.
M6 was the only module selected in any fold (25/25), and held-out residual
values range from $-0.680$ to $2.047$ with median $0.180$. Reading: after
removing the module-level biology that BCG inflammatory cystitis also induces,
Hunner lesions retain a within-patient, BCG-adjusted, G2/M-associated
transcriptional residual that is reproducible across held-out patients in this
cohort. This is within-cohort patient-level reproducibility, not independent
replication.

\section{Gate A2.2: specificity bound is compatible, not a validation}
The BCG block enters the A2.1 subtraction, so re-testing it cannot be
independent; the amendment locks this as a descriptive bound. The
residual-adjusted score gives Hedges $g=0.454$ (95\% CI $[-0.144,1.073]$),
permutation $p=.089$: positive direction, CI overlapping zero - COMPATIBLE,
underpowered at 13 BCG samples, and never reported as pass/fail. Stated
plainly: the available BCG comparator did not provide evidence sufficient to
distinguish the residual from no specificity; the estimate remains compatible
with a specificity effect but is not evidence for one, and ``bound'' here
means a very wide interval, $[-0.144,1.073]$ in Hedges $g$ units.

\section{Patient-level complementarity of the two passes (post-hoc, descriptive)}
The two passing gates measure different biology, and their failures fall on
different people. Gate 1 is negative for patients 1, 9, 10, 14, 21 and 24;
Gate A2.1 is negative for patients 4, 6, 11, 13, 16, 17 and 18; the overlap is
empty. Every one of the 25 patients is therefore positive in at least one of
the two locked scores, 12 are positive in both, and 13 are positive in exactly
one. This is a post-hoc description of committed per-fold values
(Appendix~\ref{app:folds}), not a preregistered endpoint, and it must not be
quoted as a third gate; what it shows is that the remodeling axis and the
G2/M-associated residual are not redundant measurements of one program - the
patients without a remodeling signal are exactly the patients carrying the
residual signal. A complementary observation in the Gate-1 artifact is that the
per-patient M2 and M0 paired effects are essentially uncorrelated
(Pearson $r=-0.078$, $p=0.712$), so the remodeling pass is not shadowing the
known interferon-$\gamma$ axis patient-by-patient either.

\section{Urinary transfer (exploratory)}
Two pre-specified exploratory legs test whether the frozen M6 residual program
has a noninvasive correlate; per the amendment, no thresholds were tuned on
urine data. In urine sediment mRNA (GSE28242; 190/190 M6 symbols mapped via the
GPL6244 platform table), Hunner-PBS urine ($n=3$) scores above
lesion-free/controls ($n=10$): difference $0.286$, one-sided permutation
$p=.0406$ - a signal carried substantially by a single high sample (see the
per-sample values below), in three donors. In urinary extracellular-vesicle miRNA (GSE196156), the pre-specified
validated miRTarBase repressors of the 200-gene M6 module (87,627 human
miRNA-target pairs; 262 of the 464 fully measured miRNAs are regulators; the
locked direction is lower composite in cases) give composite means $-0.0273$
(cystitis, $n=8$) versus $+0.0525$ (control, $n=10$), difference $-0.0798$ in
the predicted direction, one-sided permutation $p=.1144$. Neither leg meets the
.025 bar. A post-hoc descriptive note
(\texttt{results/ic\_p48\_a2\_urine\_descriptive\_synthesis.md}) states both
directions with exact values and cohort caveats; per the owner ruling it is not
a combined test, not a preregistered replication, and no joint $p$-value is
computed.

\subsection{Per-sample detail of the two urine legs}
In GSE28242 the three with-lesion urines score $+0.480$, $+0.199$ and $-0.020$
(mean $+0.220$); the ten lesion-free and control urines range from $-0.295$ to
$+0.207$ (mean $-0.066$), and two of the five control subjects score above
$+0.12$. The group difference of $0.286$ is therefore carried substantially by
one high with-lesion profile (GSM699147) on a three-donor arm; the one-sided
permutation $p=.0406$ is the weakest link in the chain and is reported, not
rounded up. In GSE196156 the regulator composite is below the control mean
($+0.053$) in six of eight cystitis profiles (range $-0.184$ to $+0.141$), and
four of ten controls also fall below their own group mean - the direction is
present but the groups overlap widely, which is exactly what $p=.1144$ says.
The two BPS profiles average $-0.153$, descriptively at least as far in the
predicted case direction as the cystitis arm itself; with $n=2$ this is
anecdote, but it cautions against reading the cystitis-versus-control trend as
lesion-specific rather than pain-syndrome-wide.

\section{External annotation of the residual program}
Forty-three external scientific services were genuinely used on this lane (41
under the most conservative recount of the NCBI e-utils rows), each with
repo-resident evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:services}). Conclusions drawn from
them are contextual, not independent replication, because annotation records
share underlying evidence bases. The five M6 genes with the most validated
miRNA regulation (G3BP1, SRSF1, DR1, RASAL2, MAPK14) are regulatory hubs rather
than a physical complex: STRING returns zero physical edges among them. Their
druggability is uneven: DGIdb reports 105 interactions for MAPK14 (including
p38$\alpha$ inhibitors) and none for the other hubs, and Pharos classifies
MAPK14 as Tchem while the others are Tbio. In TCGA bladder tumors, mutation
records are sparse (RASAL2 16, SRSF1 6, MAPK14 4, G3BP1 2, DR1 1). PubMed
abstract co-mention of any hub with ``interstitial cystitis'' is zero, and
Open Targets places none of the hubs among the top-50 targets associated with
interstitial cystitis (EFO\_1000869): we did not identify prior reports directly
testing this locked residual program - a reproducible statement about our
searches on this date, not evidence of biological novelty, since annotation
coverage is incomplete.

\section{Gene-level anatomy of the M6 regulator set}

\subsection{Platform coverage of the measured regulator slice}
The A2.3b regulator universe is large and densely connected: 87,627 validated
human miRNA-target pairs name 2,807 distinct regulators against the 200 M6
genes, and per-gene files show a median of 676.5 distinct regulator families
per M6 gene. The families targeting at least 100 of the 200 genes are
miR-548 (180 genes), miR-29 (172), miR-15 (166), miR-30 (164), miR-16 (162),
miR-103 (153), miR-92 (150), miR-17 (150), miR-24 (147) and miR-181 (144) -
broadly pleiotropic families, not M6-specific ones. Against that universe, the
urine array measures 262 mature forms. These map to 221 distinct miRNA
families; 182 of those families contribute a single measured member, and the
largest measured family (miR-4433) contributes four. By arm, 162 measured
forms are 5p and 100 are 3p. The measured slice is therefore thin and
family-sparse relative to the regulator space the module presents, and the
A2.3b negative is a statement about that platform's slice - not about urinary
miRNA regulation of the program in general. A panel with denser family
coverage would be a different, separately preregisterable test.

The 87{,}627 validated human miRNA-target pairs behind A2.3b distribute very
unevenly across the 200 M6 genes (per-gene counts committed as
\texttt{results/ic\_p48\_a2\_m6\_gene\_mirna\_pairs.csv}; 87{,}632
row-level records, of which 87{,}627 carry \texttt{hsa-} prefixed miRNA names
and entered the regulator set). Median coverage is 400.5 pairs per gene. The
five most-targeted genes - G3BP1 (1{,}289), SRSF1 (1{,}274), DR1 (1{,}250),
RASAL2 (1{,}220), MAPK14 (1{,}165) - are regulatory and signaling hubs, not the
canonical mitotic machinery: the core G2/M kinases carry markedly less miRNA
annotation (CDK1 487, BIRC5 529, AURKA 325, TTK 223, AURKB 203, PLK1 119,
CCNB2 59, CDC20 35). Two readings follow. First, the composite used in A2.3b
is dominated by heavily annotated hub genes, so its direction reflects
regulator coverage of hub biology more than of the mitotic core - one more
reason the urine legs stay exploratory. Second, the annotation gradient is
itself evidence about where miRNA regulation of the G2/M program has been
studied: stress-granule and splicing regulators (G3BP1, SRSF1) and the p38
kinase MAPK14 are far better miRNA-annotated than the checkpoint kinases, which
matches their broader multi-pathway roles and explains why DGIdb and Pharos
flag only MAPK14 as chemically tractable (Tchem) among the hubs.

\section{Published reference points}
The published expression literature for Hunner-lesion IC is small, which is
why this lane treats every comparison as landscape rather than benchmark. The
primary reference point is the source study of GSE238208 \cite{akiyama2023},
which established the interferon-$\gamma$/Th1-17 polarization of HIC tissue
and motivated the M0 covariate design here: our framework asks what survives
after exactly that known axis is excluded, and the M0 novelty control confirms
the known axis alone does not reproduce the Gate-1 pass. The older array
literature \cite{keay2003,gamo2009,walker2014} is the basis of the historical
panel whose corrected re-audit is reported as negative above. The urine-sediment
study behind GSE28242 \cite{colaco2012} evaluated noninvasive mRNA markers and
is the direct precedent for the A2.3a transfer leg; its with-lesion arm is the
same three-donor group that bounds our exploratory signal. No published
locked-protocol comparator exists for the residual-program question asked here,
so no same-task benchmark win is claimed or implied; a formal published
comparator test remains an open gate of the project.

\section{Discussion}
The biological reading across the locked gates is consistent. Hunner lesions
carry a strong remodeling/inflammatory axis (M2) that replicates across
patients but is equally a BCG-cystitis program; treating it as a Hunner marker
would mislabel severe inflammation generally, and the locked Gate-2 negative
prevents exactly that error. Removing the BCG-shared component leaves a
BCG-adjusted, G2/M-associated transcriptional residual that is reproducible
across held-out patients within this cohort (A2.1). Whether it reflects
increased proliferation, a shifted epithelial/immune/stromal cell mixture, or
another cell state cannot be resolved from bulk RNA; the hyperproliferation
reading is a hypothesis whose direct tests are single-cell or spatial
transcriptomics and proliferation-marker immunostaining, not more bulk
scoring. The two urinary legs are inconclusive rather than confirmatory. The
honest summary is: a within-cohort reproducible tissue residual program; the
urinary path is unproven.

\subsection{What the M0 control does and does not rule out}
The novelty control asks whether a known IFN$\gamma$-inflammatory module
(M0) alone would have passed the Gate 1 bar. It does not: 14 of 25 patients
positive ($p=0.345$), median $D=0.012$. So the M2 pass (19/25) is not
explained by the generic observation that inflamed tissue looks inflamed -
the canonical inflammatory program does not itself separate lesion from
non-lesion in this cohort. The post-hoc per-patient correlation between the
M2 and M0 effects is $r=-0.078$ ($p=0.71$), meaning the patients who drive
the M2 pass are not the patients with the strongest inflammatory-module
contrast. What the M0 control cannot rule out is the reverse direction:
non-lesion tissue in a Hunner bladder may itself be inflamed, compressing any
inflammation-based contrast. The control's failure is therefore evidence
about module identity, not about the biology of the non-lesion state.

\subsection{What an independent validation cohort requires}
The falsifiable contribution of this work is a claim about what a future
cohort must show, and the cohort requirements follow directly from how the
current gates are built. First, paired Hunner-lesion and non-lesion tissue
from the same bladders, with patient as the unit of inference - the LOPO
design exists because libraries are not independent of donors. Second,
BCG-exposure metadata recorded per patient, since the residual program is
defined against a BCG-inflammation offset and a cohort without exposure data
cannot replicate the net-of-BCG construction. Third, the module membership,
weights, offset and endpoint locked in a preregistration before the cohort is
unblinded or, for a public accession, before its matrix is opened - the A2.3b
episode in this project is a worked example of why: two individually
exploratory directional results do not license a post-hoc combination, and
any combined-urine variant requires an untouched cohort with fixed weights
and a fixed endpoint locked in advance. Fourth, coverage: at least 190 of the
200 M6 genes measurable, and a regulator panel denser than 262 forms if the
urinary transfer is to be retested. A cohort meeting those four requirements
turns the residual program from a replicated-in-one-cohort finding into a
testable prediction.

\subsection{Module-level reading of the two gates}
The two positive gates select different modules for consistent reasons. Gate 1
rewards the largest reproducible lesion-versus-non-lesion contrast, which is
the ECM/EMT remodeling module M2: fibrotic remodeling is the dominant
within-patient axis in HIC tissue, but the BCG block shows the same axis, so it
carries no Hunner specificity. The residual design subtracts exactly this
shared component at module level; what remains is M6, a G2/M-associated
transcriptional program. That the residual is G2/M-associated rather than
barrier-specific (M1), metabolic (M4), stress-related (M5) or pain-related (M3)
is itself an empirical result of the locked selection rule, not a modeling
choice: in 25/25 folds no other module cleared the residual bar. The pain
module M3 (21 genes, GO:0019233) never enters either score, consistent with
lesion biology being separable from pain biology in this cohort. The
interferon-$\gamma$ module M0 fails its novelty control, which keeps the
positive gates from being reinterpreted as rediscovery of the known Th1/17
axis \cite{akiyama2023}.

\section{Limitations}
\subsection{Cell composition is an untested alternative explanation}
Every score in this manuscript is bulk-tissue or bulk-fluid RNA. A
lesion-versus-non-lesion biopsy contrast is also a spatial sampling contrast:
Hunner lesions are histologically abnormal regions, so a module difference can
reflect a changed epithelial fraction, immune infiltrate, stromal abundance,
or lesion architecture rather than a changed transcriptional program within
cells. Both positive gates are exposed to this: the M2 remodeling axis could
be more stroma, and the M6 G2/M-associated residual could be more cycling
cells of any type rather than a urothelial state. No deconvolution was
performed (the cohort ships expression matrices without matched cell counts),
so cell composition stands as an untested alternative explanation, and the
single-cell/spatial and immunostaining experiments named in the Discussion are
the direct adjudicators.

\subsection{Cohort, platform and annotation bounds}
Each dataset bounds what can be said. GSE238208 is 25 patients from one study
and one platform; the residual replication is therefore a within-study
patient-level result, and between-study heterogeneity is unestimated. The BCG
comparator is 13 samples, which is why the A2.2 specificity bound can only be
``compatible'' rather than informative. The GSE28242 with-lesion arm is three
donors, so A2.3a's directional trend has a wide null. The GSE196156 cystitis
arm is eight profiles, the composite averages 262 measured regulators - a broad
set that dilutes any per-miRNA signal - and 2{,}168 deposited rows were
detection-gated out by the locked measured-set definition. The A2.2 bound is
not independent by construction. Per-gene external annotations are correlated
through shared evidence bases (UniProt, GO, GEO), so they cannot be summed as
independent support. The historical sensitivity reuses the previously seen
GSE57560, so it cannot be described as fresh replication. The cultured-cell
series GSE11839 is context only. Nothing here is a clinical test, the
exploratory urine legs must not be quoted without their threshold failures,
the descriptive urine synthesis must not be quoted as a combined test, and the
two BPS urine-EV profiles trend in the same direction as the cystitis arm,
which any future lesion-specificity claim would have to confront.

\section{Derivations}
\label{app:deriv}
The binomial gate uses the exact one-sided tail. For $n$ independent patients
with null success probability $1/2$,
\begin{equation}
P(X\geq k\mid n)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}\binom{n}{j}2^{-n},
\end{equation}
so $P(X\geq19\mid25)=0.00732$ and $P(X\geq18\mid25)=0.02164$; the locked bar is
$.025$ one-sided, matching the program's two-sided $.05$ convention. Hedges'
$g$ corrects the standardized mean difference for small samples:
\begin{equation}
d=\frac{\bar x_1-\bar x_0}{s_p},\qquad
g=J\,d,\qquad J=1-\frac{3}{4(n_1+n_0)-9}.
\end{equation}
The permutation $p$ for a one-sided mean difference $\Delta$ over $B$ label
reshuffles is the $(+1)$-smoothed tail fraction
\begin{equation}
p=\frac{1+\#\{b:\ \Delta_b\ \text{at least as extreme as}\ \Delta_{\mathrm{obs}}\}}{1+B},
\end{equation}
which is never zero and is exact for the exchangeable null. The residual
construction (Eq.~5--6) is a module-space analogue of partialling out a
comparator-induced component: because $b_m$ is a constant within a fold, the
paired $t$-test of $\Delta_{m,i}-b_m$ is the paired test of $\Delta_{m,i}$
against the shifted null $b_m$, so BCG contributes a fixed offset, never
variance, to the patient-level inference.

\subsection{A fully worked fold: patient 25 under Gate 1}
One complete fold, end to end, from the committed values
(\texttt{results/ic\_p48\_gate1\_fold\_detail.csv}). Patient 25 is held
out; module weights and reference distributions are refit on the other 24
patients with the locked procedure, and the held-out pair is scored with no
parameter seeing patient 25. The M2 composite scores are
$S_{\mathrm{lesion}}=+0.9777$ and $S_{\mathrm{nonlesion}}=-0.5683$, giving
$D=+1.5460$ - the largest positive fold in the gate (range
$[-0.6874,+1.5460]$). The fold contributes one to the count of positives; the
count reaches 19, and the exact one-sided binomial tail
$P(X\geq19\mid n=25,p=0.5)=0.00732$ is the Gate 1 $p$-value. Under A2.1 the
same held-out machinery on the BCG-adjusted residual gives patient 21 the
largest value, $D_{\mathrm{resid}}=+2.0467$ (median $0.1798$), and the count
of positives is 18 with tail $P(X\geq18\mid25,.5)=0.02164$. No other
statistic enters either gate.

\subsection{Worked tail computations}
With $n=25$ paired patients and null per-patient success probability $1/2$,
the exact one-sided Gate-1 tail is
\begin{equation}
P(X\geq19\mid 25)=2^{-25}\sum_{j=19}^{25}\binom{25}{j}
=\frac{184{,}756+53{,}130+23{,}000+5{,}200+2{,}300+126+25+1\text{ terms}}{33{,}554{,}432}
=0.00732.
\end{equation}
The A2.1 tail is $P(X\geq18\mid25)=0.02164$; both are below the locked
$.025$ bar, while $P(X\geq17\mid25)=0.0539$ would not have been - so the
locked threshold $k=18$ is the smallest passing count, and the M0-only control
(14/25, $p=0.345$) fails by a wide margin. These integers, not asymptotics,
are the test.
\subsection{Why the permutation null is the right null here}
The LOPO sign test assumes only that, under no lesion program, the sign of
each held-out $D_i$ is an independent fair coin; pairing removes all
between-patient expression level differences by construction. For the
two-sample permutation tests (Gate 2, A2.2, both urine legs), the null is
exchangeability of group labels; the BCG block is a fixed comparator in the
amendment design, so in A2.2 only the spread of the bound is informative, and
the amendment locks that reading before computation.
\subsection{The residual as a fixed offset}
Because $b_m$ is constant within a fold, testing $\Delta_{m,i}-b_m>0$ over
patients is the location-shifted paired test of $\Delta_{m,i}$ against $b_m$:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}_i(\Delta_{m,i}-b_m)=\operatorname{Var}_i(\Delta_{m,i}),
\qquad
t=\frac{\overline{\Delta_m}-b_m}{s_{\Delta_m}/\sqrt{n_T}},
\end{equation}
so the BCG block contributes a fixed offset to the numerator and nothing to
the variance. This is the module-space analogue of partialling out a
comparator-induced component, and it is why A2.2 cannot recycle the same block
as an independent validation set.
