How it can fail

The biggest danger is cell composition.

You might discover:

"Hunner lesions contain more immune cells."

That is biologically real, but it is not especially interesting if it collapses to the already-known inflammatory phenotype.

Another danger is that the signature merely reproduces the published IFN-γ/Th1/17 biology.

What makes it novel

The novelty must therefore be defined before looking at results:

The primary score cannot be based on genes/pathways constituting the published Th1/17/IFN-γ result.

Instead, explicitly partition candidate biology into:

previously reported inflammatory biology;

epithelial/barrier biology;

extracellular matrix/remodeling;

neuronal/sensory signaling;

urothelial differentiation;

metabolism;

stress/repair;

cell-state programs.

Then the interesting result would be something like:

A reproducible lesion-localized epithelial/barrier/repair program persists after controlling for the known inflammatory component.

That is considerably more novel than "IFN-γ is elevated."

The preregistration I'd actually lock

I'd structure the project around three gates.

Gate 1 — Biological discovery

Question: Does a reproducible within-patient molecular axis distinguish Hunner lesion from matched non-lesion tissue?

Primary endpoint:

≥21/25

paired directions in the same direction, exact one-sided p<0.025.

Feature selection occurs entirely within training folds.

Gate 2 — Inflammatory specificity

Freeze Gate-1 score.

Ask:

Does it distinguish Hunner-lesion biology from BCG cystitis?

No retraining.

Require:

pre-specified direction;

meaningful standardized effect;

permutation p<0.025.

This prevents "we discovered something that is merely inflammation."

Gate 3 — Noninvasive transfer

Freeze everything from Gates 1–2.

Map the frozen biological program to urine.

Test independently in:

GSE28242;

GSE196156.

Do not optimize thresholds separately for either dataset.

A positive result here becomes the really interesting finding:

A bladder-localized Hunner-lesion molecular program has a detectable urinary correlate.

What I would explicitly ban before analysis

This is almost as important as the preregistration.

No:

selecting the top 50 genes after seeing the whole dataset;

repeatedly trying gene counts until one works;

selecting genes using BCG and then calling BCG validation;

using GSE28242 to tune a classifier;

neural networks;

random forests on thousands of genes;

choosing between mRNA and miRNA after seeing which works;

And especially:

No "try 30 signatures and report the one that survives."

If you want to explore 30 signatures, record all 30 and treat the exploration as exploratory.

My actual recommendation

I would make the project:

"A preregistered computational discovery of a Hunner-lesion molecular state and its cross-compartment urinary correlate."

The core hypothesis becomes:

Hunner lesion→reproducible within-patient molecular state→BCG-specificity→urinary detectability

A is the statistical foundation. C is the specificity test. D/B is the translational payoff. E is the cleanest conceptual framing.

The old 50-gene panel tried to answer "what genes distinguish IC?" That is exactly the kind of question where tiny datasets and biological heterogeneity generate fragile discoveries.

The redirected question is substantially harder but cleaner:

"What molecular state is specifically associated with the Hunner lesion, does it survive a patient-level replication design and an inflammatory control, and can any part of that state be detected noninvasively?"

That gives you a genuine chance at a positive result without pretending the existing urine datasets are large enough to support a conventional diagnostic-AI claim.
