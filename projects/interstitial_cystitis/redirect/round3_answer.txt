1. Gate A2.1 is statistically valid as a within-cohort replication, but the manuscript risks presenting it as stronger evidence than it is

Section: Abstract; §10 “Gate A2.1: the G2/M residual replicates”; §17 Discussion

Weakness

The paper treats the A2.1 result (18/25 held-out patients, p=.0216) as a “replicating” residual program. That statement is technically defensible within the locked GSE238208 cohort, but the wording risks implying independent replication.

The LOPO procedure is not an external replication. It is a cross-validation estimate inside one cohort. Every held-out patient comes from the same:

study;

tissue-processing pipeline;

platform;

recruitment population;

clinical definition;

batch structure.

A skeptical reviewer will ask:

"How much evidence is this versus how much evidence is a robust leave-one-out pattern generated by one cohort?"

LOPO reduces patient leakage, but it does not address cohort leakage.

Why this weakens the claim

The paper repeatedly uses language such as:

“replicates across patients”

“replicated tissue residual program”

Those are acceptable if carefully qualified. They become problematic if readers interpret them as replication in the ordinary biomedical sense.

The danger is especially high because the old panel failed exactly due to lack of true validation. The manuscript must be stricter than average here.

Fix

Rewrite terminology consistently:

Instead of:

"the residual program replicates"

use:

"the residual program shows preregistered patient-level reproducibility within the GSE238208 cohort."

Then add a limitation:

"This is not an independent cohort replication; confirmation requires an external paired-lesion cohort."

A stronger future test:

another paired Hunner/non-lesion RNA cohort;

frozen M6 score;

no re-selection;

same endpoint.

2. The exact binomial sign test ignores a major source of uncertainty: the score-learning procedure

Section: §10; Appendix A.1

Weakness

The sign test treats each held-out patient direction as if the only uncertainty is whether the lesion effect is positive or negative.

But the procedure includes:

module selection;

fold-specific weighting;

residual construction;

module thresholding.

The final 18/25 count does not account for uncertainty introduced before the held-out score exists.

The concern is not that the held-out patient labels leaked. The concern is:

"How much variability comes from the fact that the scoring rule itself was learned 25 times?"

Why a skeptical reviewer cares

A pure sign test is very attractive because it is simple and robust. However, it can become overly optimistic when the model-building process is complex.

The manuscript says:

"Only module M6 is selected in every fold."

That helps substantially, but the reviewer will want evidence that this stability was not conditional on the chosen module universe.

Fix

Add:

A stability analysis:

frequency of every module being selected;

distribution of learned weights;

whether M6 selection remains after perturbing:

patient subsets;

module p-value threshold;

normalization choices.

A complete frozen-analysis statement:

Show that:

the module list was fixed before data access;

M6 was not chosen because it gave the strongest final narrative;

all modules had equal opportunity.

Consider a secondary permutation of the entire pipeline:

Permute lesion/non-lesion labels within patients, rerun:

module selection;

residual calculation;

LOPO scoring.

The null distribution should include feature selection.

3. The residual construction is the manuscript's most vulnerable conceptual point

Section: §10; §11; Appendix A.3; §17.3

Weakness

The manuscript describes the residual as:

"removing the module-level biology that BCG inflammatory cystitis also induces"

That is too strong.

The actual operation is:

HIC
lesion
	​

−HIC
nonlesion
	​

−BCG
offset
	​


This subtracts a mean BCG-associated module displacement.

It does not prove that the removed component is:

inflammation;

all shared inflammatory biology;

the causal BCG component;

the complete non-specific component.

It is a statistical adjustment, not biological purification.

Why reviewers may reject the specificity language

A reviewer could say:

"You subtracted one comparator mean from another disease state. Why does that isolate Hunner biology rather than create an artificial contrast?"

Especially because:

BCG lacks matched baseline tissue;

BCG patients differ from HIC patients;

treatment exposure differs;

cancer-related biology may exist.

Fix

Change terminology.

Avoid:

"after removing BCG-shared biology"

Prefer:

"after applying a predefined BCG-derived module offset."

Avoid:

"Hunner-specific residual"

Prefer:

"BCG-adjusted lesion-associated residual."

Then the specificity claim becomes:

"This residual is a candidate lesion-associated component not explained by the average BCG offset."

That is much more defensible.

4. Gate A2.2 is underpowered and may be interpreted incorrectly

Section: §11 “specificity bound is compatible, not a validation”; Abstract

Weakness

The manuscript correctly says A2.2 is not a validation. However, the abstract wording:

"with a descriptive specificity bound that is compatible but underpowered"

still risks being read as positive evidence.

The actual result:

g=0.454,CI[−0.144,1.073]

is compatible with:

no specificity;

moderate specificity;

large specificity.

It is almost non-informative.

Why this matters

A skeptical reviewer may say:

"You cannot claim a specificity direction from a confidence interval spanning negative to strongly positive values."

Fix

Be more explicit.

Suggested language:

"The available BCG comparator did not provide evidence sufficient to distinguish the residual from no specificity."

Then:

"The estimate remains compatible with a specificity effect but is not evidence for one."

Do not call it a "bound" without immediately defining that it is a very wide bound.

5. The urine transfer section is the highest risk of accidental overinterpretation

Section: §13; Abstract; §17 Discussion

GSE28242 issue
Weakness

The manuscript reports:

n=3 Hunner urine samples;

difference 0.286;

permutation p=.0406.

The paper correctly calls it exploratory, but the abstract includes:

"two pre-specified exploratory urinary transfers that point the locked direction"

This may attract criticism.

The entire signal may be driven by:

one high Hunner urine sample.

The paper itself admits this.

Fix

Move the urine result further down.

The abstract should say:

"Exploratory urine analyses were inconclusive, with one directionally consistent but small dataset signal and one non-significant EV-miRNA test."

Do not lead readers toward a "two-modal confirmation."

GSE196156 issue
Weakness

The miRNA analysis is especially vulnerable because:

the regulator mapping creates a many-to-many relationship;

the composite is heavily influenced by annotation density;

only 262 measured miRNAs are available.

The manuscript recognizes this, but still spends substantial space on regulator anatomy.

A reviewer may interpret the annotation analysis as retroactive support for a weak result.

Fix

Reduce the annotation section.

Frame it as:

"characterization of assay coverage limitations"

not:

"mechanistic support."

6. The biological interpretation of M6 is too confident

Section: §17; §17.3

Weakness

The manuscript states:

"coheres with the urothelial hyperproliferation described in HIC histology"

This is plausible but close to circular.

A G2/M Hallmark module means:

cell-cycle-associated transcription;

proliferation-associated expression.

It does not establish:

increased proliferation rate;

proliferative cell population;

urothelial origin.

Bulk RNA cannot determine this.

Why this matters

A skeptical reviewer may ask:

"Is this a true cell-state program or simply different cell composition?"

Possible explanations:

more proliferating epithelial cells;

immune infiltrate differences;

stromal differences;

epithelial injury response.

Fix

Replace:

"proliferative residual"

with:

"G2/M-associated transcriptional residual."

Then discuss proliferation only as a hypothesis.

Add a possible validation:

single-cell RNA-seq;

spatial transcriptomics;

immunostaining for proliferation markers.

7. The manuscript underplays cell composition as an alternative explanation

Section: Limitations; Discussion

Weakness

The paper discusses inflammation but not enough about bulk RNA mixture effects.

Both M2 and M6 could represent:

different epithelial fractions;

infiltrating cell populations;

stromal abundance;

lesion architecture.

Why reviewers will care

Hunner lesions are histologically abnormal tissue regions. Comparing:

lesion biopsy vs non-lesion biopsy

is inherently a spatial sampling comparison.

The molecular difference may be:

"you sampled damaged tissue"

rather than:

"you found a disease mechanism."

Fix

Add:

deconvolution analysis if possible;

cell-type marker sensitivity;

epithelial/stromal/immune proportion analysis.

Even if not used as correction, show awareness.

8. The novelty argument based on external annotation is vulnerable

Section: §14 External annotation; §15 Gene-level anatomy

Weakness

The manuscript states:

"the residual program is, as of this manuscript’s evidence, un-claimed by existing IC literature"

This is a dangerous novelty claim.

Absence of:

PubMed co-mention;

Open Targets ranking;

database annotation

does not establish novelty.

Literature search tools have incomplete coverage.

Fix

Replace:

"un-claimed"

with:

"we did not identify prior reports directly testing this locked residual program."

That is a reproducible statement.

Avoid using annotation absence as biological novelty evidence.

9. The accession count language may inflate provenance strength

Section: Abstract; §1; §3

Weakness

The manuscript emphasizes:

"153 accession-backed records"

A skeptical reviewer may ask:

"How many independent biological units?"

The answer is much smaller.

The actual evidence base is roughly:

25 HIC patients;

13 BCG samples;

20 EV-miRNA subjects;

13 urine sediment samples;

6 cultured cell donors.

The accession count is useful for auditability, but it must never sound like statistical replication.

Fix

In the abstract:

replace:

"153 accession-backed records"

with:

"153 verified accession records representing X biological cohorts and Y independent donors."

Keep the audit number, but foreground biological n.

10. The manuscript has some post-hoc narrative risk despite preregistration

Sections: §12; §17.3; §15

Weakness

The preregistration protects against many criticisms, but some interpretations appear after seeing the results:

Examples:

Patient complementarity

The statement:

"patients without a remodeling signal are exactly the patients carrying the residual signal"

is interesting but clearly post-hoc.

You label it correctly, but it is narratively powerful and could distract.

G2/M anatomy

The discussion of:

G3BP1;

SRSF1;

MAPK14;

miRNA hubs;

may read as explanation generated after finding M6.

Fix

Create a strict hierarchy:

Primary confirmatory:

Gate 1

Gate A2.1

Secondary descriptive:

patient complementarity

gene anatomy

external annotations

Move these to supplement unless directly needed.

11. The historical panel audit section needs maximal reproducibility because it motivates the whole paper

Section: Abstract; §2; historical sensitivity discussion

Weakness

The manuscript says the old panel is dead, but a reviewer will want:

exact original genes;

exact corrected labels;

exact replication procedure;

whether the failed replication was preregistered before seeing failure.

Because the old failure motivates the entire methodology.

Fix

Provide:

original panel source;

corrected sample table;

label audit evidence;

exact overlap calculation;

exact sign test.

This should be as transparent as the new gates.

12. The paper should avoid "diagnosis" language everywhere

Sections: Abstract; Introduction framing

Weakness

Even though the manuscript includes disclaimers, words like:

marker;

molecular diagnosis;

noninvasive marker path;

can trigger a clinical interpretation.

The data are nowhere near clinical validation.

Fix

Prefer:

"molecular correlate";

"candidate biological score";

"research assay."

Reserve "biomarker" only with explicit qualification:

"candidate research biomarker."

Highest-priority revisions before submission

If only five changes were possible:

Replace "replicated residual program" with "within-cohort patient-level reproducibility."

Downgrade all specificity language: the residual is BCG-adjusted, not Hunner-specific.

Move urine results clearly into exploratory territory; do not imply two-leg support.

Add cell-composition limitations or analyses.

Foreground biological sample sizes, not accession counts.

The strongest part of the manuscript is the methodological honesty around negative gates. The biggest danger is that some sentences unintentionally undo that honesty by turning a careful residual construction into a biological specificity claim that the current design cannot establish.
