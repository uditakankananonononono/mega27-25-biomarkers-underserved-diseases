I treated this as a verification/weakness pass, not a completion or “win” assessment. I also treated the manuscript itself as the evidentiary source; I did not assume that undocumented repo material exists merely because the manuscript says it does.

1. Scientific/statistical/methodological/presentational weaknesses
A. P53 is not actually documented as preregistered to the same standard as P51/P52

Severity: major.

The manuscript repeatedly calls P53 “preregistered,” but the reproducibility appendix explicitly identifies only P51 and P52 preregistration commits. 

1-manuscript

More importantly, §12.1 says:

“The arc was approved with GSE227375 … Pre-analysis reconnaissance changed that.”

and then GSE293840 was substituted. The manuscript says this was “parent-approved before any data touch,” but that is not the same thing as documenting a frozen preregistration. 

1-manuscript

Fix: code/data/provenance + rewriting.

You need an auditable P53 preregistration commit with:

timestamp before expression-data inspection,

exact original and substituted cohort decision rule,

exact P53 candidate set,

preprocessing,

selection criterion,

E1/E2 definitions,

stopping rule,

inversion interpretation,

comparator treatment,

seed,

and evidence that the substitution occurred before relevant data were analyzed.

If that record exists, cite it. If it does not, stop calling P53 preregistered and call it a locked/audited analysis instead.

B. The manuscript is internally inconsistent about the number of arcs

The abstract describes P51 and P52 but omits P53; §1 says there are “two preregistered analysis arcs, P51 and P52”; later the manuscript presents P53 as a third preregistered arc. 

1-manuscript +1

Likewise, the abstract says the report runs on four public cohorts, but the manuscript actually uses GSE226260, GSE275334, GSE334857, GSE251872, and GSE293840. 

1-manuscript

Fix: rewriting.

This is straightforward but important. A reviewer should never have to infer which arcs actually count.

C. “Powered” is not demonstrated

P53 is repeatedly called the “powered” ME/CFS-contrast test, and the manuscript says n=168 is “six times P52’s training power.” 

1-manuscript

But six times the sample size is not a power analysis. No target effect size, alpha, power, detectable effect, or calculation is presented.

And the relevant final question is not merely ME/CFS-vs-healthy cfRNA separation. The hard question is transport from cfRNA ME/CFS to cellular ME/CFS/Long COVID, where the evaluation arm is still small.

Fix: rewriting if “powered” is removed.
Fixable with computation if you can perform a genuinely prespecified/clearly labeled retrospective power or detectable-effect analysis—but it should not be used to retroactively certify the failed endpoint.

Better language: “larger-sample P53 analysis” or “higher-powered relative to P52,” not simply “powered.”

D. The P53 E2 result is overinterpreted as an “inversion artifact”

This is one of the most important scientific overstatements.

The manuscript observes:

E1 fails: cfRNA-trained L9 does not transport to cellular ME/CFS.

E2 is significant: ME/CFS vs Long COVID differs, g = −0.929.

Direction is opposite to the training contrast.

It then calls this an “inversion artifact of cross-analyte training” and says it “expos[es] a transport artifact class.” 

1-manuscript

But the data establish directional discordance plus failure of transport. They do not establish the causal mechanism as an “artifact.” Alternative explanations include:

genuine biology differing between cfRNA and cellular RNA,

cell-composition differences,

analyte-specific biology,

cohort differences,

technical/platform effects,

chance,

or some combination.

Fix: rewriting, unless you can produce an independent validation experiment specifically demonstrating the artifact mechanism.

The defensible claim is:

“The cfRNA-trained signature failed transport and showed an oppositely directed cellular contrast; the mechanism of this discordance is unresolved.”

That is substantially stronger scientifically than pretending the mechanism is known.

E. “Cross-assay replication” is too strong

§15.1 calls P51:

“a real, preregistered, cross-assay replication”

But this is not conventional replication. Training and evaluation differ in:

cohort,

disease/control composition,

tissue/cell mixture,

assay platform,

gene availability,

and module representation.

Training is whole-blood RNA-seq against recovered controls; evaluation is PBMC NanoString against healthy controls. 

1-manuscript

The evaluation is valuable transport validation, but calling it “replication” risks implying biological replication of the same contrast.

Fix: rewriting.

Use “cross-cohort, cross-assay transport validation” or similar.

F. The actual P51 “positive” is much narrower than the prose sometimes makes it

The signature is selected from 195 measurable genes in the Hallmark oxphos module, but only 12 genes survive onto the 635-gene evaluation panel. 

1-manuscript

Thus the evaluated object is not literally the same 195-gene score. It is a 12-gene projection of the selected module.

That is an important methodological fact and should be foregrounded, not buried in §5.2/limitations.

Fix: rewriting, plus ideally a code-produced sensitivity analysis.

Useful evidence would be:

the exact 12 genes,

their locked direction,

score correlation with the full training module if calculable,

sensitivity to alternative reasonable intersection rules,

and per-gene contribution.

The existing post-hoc table helps, but does not solve the construct-validity issue.

G. The evaluation panel was not designed to measure oxidative phosphorylation

The manuscript admits that the 635-gene NanoString panel is immune-focused and that metabolic coverage is incidental. 

1-manuscript

That means E1 is not a broad replication of an oxphos transcriptomic state. It is a test of whether a small available subset retains a compatible signal.

This is an inherent limitation of the available evaluation cohort.

Fix: cannot be fully fixed without another appropriately measured cohort.
You can make the limitation much clearer and perhaps perform sensitivity analyses within the available genes.

H. P51 selection has acknowledged anti-conservatism that materially affects the training evidence

The manuscript correctly admits that direction was chosen from the data and the permutation was one-sided in that observed direction. 

1-manuscript

That means p=.0432 is not a clean prespecified two-sided selection result.

The manuscript sometimes still uses language such as “selected under a frozen protocol” without immediately reminding the reader that the frozen protocol itself contained this statistical weakness.

The E1 result is protected much better because evaluation is independent, but the training selection evidence should not be described as if it were pristine.

Fix: rewriting.
A useful code analysis would be a two-sided post-hoc sensitivity result, clearly labeled non-preregistered, but it cannot retroactively replace P51's original result.

I. The nine-module selection problem deserves explicit multiplicity discussion

P51 tests nine candidate modules and selects one. The manuscript reports individual permutation p-values, with L7 at .0432. 

1-manuscript

Even though the ultimate evaluation was locked and independent, a reader needs to distinguish:

evidence that one of nine modules looked promising in training, from

evidence that the selected module transports to a new cohort.

The independent E1 protects the latter much more strongly than the former.

The paper currently makes the latter distinction implicitly but does not quantitatively explain it.

Fix: rewriting plus optional code.

A simple permutation-of-the-whole-selection-procedure or maximum-statistic analysis would quantify how unusual “best of nine” is under the null. Do not substitute that for the preregistered result; report it as a sensitivity analysis.

J. LOPO “37/37 stability” is a weak stability criterion

The manuscript repeatedly emphasizes 37/37 sign stability. 

1-manuscript

But the criterion is merely sign of D, not:

selection stability,

magnitude stability,

rank stability,

p-value stability,

gene membership stability,

or predictive performance.

With a tiny sample, an effect can retain its sign while its magnitude changes drastically.

Fix: code/data.

Useful locked/post-hoc diagnostics:

LOPO effect-size distribution,

confidence interval,

module ranking across folds,

number of folds in which the module actually meets the selection threshold,

influence diagnostics.

Do not retroactively make these selection criteria.

K. P52 does not establish a biological null

The manuscript does mostly acknowledge this, but §6.5 still says:

“there is simply no Hallmark-scale signal … at this n.”

That is acceptable if strictly read as a study-specific result, but the surrounding language risks turning it into evidence about ME/CFS biology.

The 27-subject cohort, with a severe platform imbalance, cannot exclude moderate effects.

Fix: rewriting.

Explicitly say “no module met the preregistered threshold in this cohort”, rather than “no signal” unless qualified by detectable-effect limitations.

L. P52 platform confounding remains serious despite stratification

This is correctly acknowledged but understated.

The platform-by-disease composition is 3/7 and 9/8. 

1-manuscript

Within-platform z-scoring and stratified permutation preserve the null appropriately, but they cannot create information that is absent. The first platform has only three cases.

If biological status and platform are imbalanced within strata, estimating a disease effect is intrinsically unstable.

The manuscript itself says this, but the scientific consequence deserves stronger treatment. 

1-manuscript

Fix: inherent limitation, partially characterizable computationally.

The leave-one-platform-out analysis should be shown quantitatively rather than merely mentioned.

M. P53 has analogous batch/site confounding that is not fully characterized

GSE293840 spans three sequencing batches and three collection sites. The manuscript says it uses within-batch z-scoring and batch-stratified permutation, but does not establish whether site and batch are independent, whether disease composition is balanced within site/batch, or whether collection-site effects could explain the apparent universal signal. 

1-manuscript

Fix: code/data.

Report:

disease × batch table,

disease × site table,

batch × site table,

per-stratum sample sizes,

per-stratum effect estimates,

and whether site is nested/confounded with batch.

If site is perfectly confounded with disease, this becomes an unresolved limitation.

N. “All nine modules are signal” is too strong

The heading itself—“at n=168, everything is signal”—is rhetorically stronger than the result.

The actual result is nine nominally significant module-level contrasts, p=.0008–.011. 

1-manuscript

Because Hallmark modules overlap heavily biologically and genetically, nine significant modules do not mean nine independent signals.

They may represent one underlying transcriptional axis, cell composition, batch-associated structure, or correlated gene-set response.

Fix: rewriting plus code if desired.

Show module-module correlations and, ideally, an enrichment/latent-axis analysis. Do not describe them as nine independent discoveries.

O. P53’s “power lesson” is not established by the design

The paper says the contrast between P52 and P53 is the “power lesson.” But P52 and P53 differ in:

cohort,

analyte,

sample size,

batch structure,

possibly disease phenotype,

and technical platform.

Therefore the change from null to strong signal cannot be attributed to sample size alone.

Fix: rewriting.

Call it “a larger-cohort contrast”, not a demonstrated power lesson.

P. “Six times P52’s training power” is mathematically misleading

168 is approximately 6.2×27 in sample count, but statistical power does not scale linearly with n.

Fix: rewriting.

“Six-fold larger sample” is accurate. “Six times the power” is not supported.

Q. The healthy-control E1 result is vulnerable to control choice

The manuscript appropriately says the evaluation healthy arm is not recovered-from-infection. 

1-manuscript

This is actually a major interpretive limitation, because the P51 training contrast was PASC vs recovered, whereas the E1 evaluation is Long COVID vs healthy.

The observed effect could therefore reflect differences between chronically ill participants and healthy people rather than the PASC-vs-recovered contrast.

Fix: inherent limitation unless a recovered-control cohort measured on a compatible panel can be obtained.

This is one reason “cross-assay replication” should be changed to “transport.”

R. No demographic/clinical confounding analysis is presented

There is essentially no reporting in the manuscript of:

age,

sex,

race/ethnicity,

vaccination status,

time since infection,

Long COVID duration,

symptom burden,

medications,

comorbidities,

disease severity,

ME/CFS diagnostic criteria/details,

or matching between groups.

Given expression data, these are potentially major sources of variation.

Fix: code/data if the GEO metadata contain these variables.
Inherent limitation for variables unavailable in the public data.

At minimum, the manuscript needs a cohort-characteristics table and an explicit statement of which covariates were unavailable.

S. Cell-composition confounding is central but insufficiently tested

The manuscript itself highlights whole blood vs PBMC/cfRNA differences, but there is no convincing decomposition of whether the L7 signal is:

intracellular transcriptional state,

cell-type abundance,

or some combination.

The “cross-cell-mixture” transport is actually evidence of robustness of a score, not proof of a cell-intrinsic pathway state.

Fix: computationally, to some extent.

If suitable marker genes are available, cell-composition proxies/deconvolution or sensitivity analyses could help. But a definitive cell-intrinsic interpretation requires richer measurements.

T. “Broad-based across its panel genes” is somewhat overstated

The manuscript says 10/12 genes have negative effects, which is useful. But the effects are heterogeneous, ranging from −1.39 to near zero, and two are positive. 

1-manuscript

“Broad-based” is defensible descriptively; “coherent module-level down-shift” is more interpretive.

Fix: minor rewriting. Report the distribution and avoid implying every gene independently supports the effect.

U. The post-hoc per-gene analysis has no multiplicity correction

The paper explicitly calls this analysis descriptive, which helps. 

1-manuscript

Still, listing 12 individual effect sizes invites gene-level interpretation. It should either:

remain clearly descriptive, or

include uncertainty intervals and explicitly say no gene-level inferential claims are made.

Fix: rewriting; CIs can be added computationally.

V. The comparator is methodologically asymmetric

The paper correctly acknowledges that the six-gene comparator was selected from the same source cohort and therefore has a different selection history. 

1-manuscript

However, calling the evaluation comparison “fair like-for-like” is only true at the scoring machinery level. It is not fair in terms of feature-selection provenance.

That asymmetry matters substantially when interpreting:

g = 0.958 vs 1.051 for E1,

g = 0.259 vs 0.776 for E2.

Fix: rewriting.

Do not frame the comparator as an equivalent independently derived benchmark.

W. The “published comparator fails” language risks obscuring what actually failed

The six-gene panel does not fail universally. On the E2 contrast its g=.776 misses the preregistered >.8 bar by a small amount, while it has a significant p=.0473. 

1-manuscript

The exact statement should be:

“It did not meet the preregistered combined effect-size criterion.”

Not “the published panel fails” without specifying the criterion.

Fix: rewriting.

X. The fixed-list p=.0081 is vulnerable to post-selection and the manuscript should be even clearer about its inferential status

The manuscript correctly recognizes this. 

1-manuscript

But calling it “nominally strong” is unnecessary and potentially confusing. The relevant fact is that the list was selected in the same cohort.

Fix: rewriting only.

Y. The statement that published lists “do not survive contact with independent cohorts” is broader than the tested evidence

Only one six-gene panel was tested, and one specific NanoString cohort was used.

That cannot establish that “published fixed gene lists” generally do not survive independent cohorts.

Fix: rewriting.

Use “this published six-gene panel did not meet the locked criterion in this evaluation cohort.”

Z. The manuscript makes a universal claim from one CXCL8 coverage failure

§4 concludes:

“fixed lists discovered in whole blood do not automatically exist in sorted-cell compartments.”

The underlying example supports that transfer cannot be assumed, not a general law about fixed lists.

Fix: rewriting.

AA. “Only public cohort” claims require a systematic search

The manuscript repeatedly says GSE275334 is “the only public cohort” containing LC + ME/CFS on one platform. 

1-manuscript

But §23 explicitly says the literature position is not a systematic review. 

1-manuscript

Therefore “only public cohort” is not adequately supported unless the repository search was systematically documented.

Fix: either perform/document a reproducible GEO/ArrayExpress/etc. search or rewrite to:

“the only such public cohort identified by our search.”

This is a meaningful distinction.

AB. The “field needs X more than Y” conclusion is too sweeping

§15.2 says the field needs a powered two-disease cohort “more than it needs another single-disease signature.” 

1-manuscript

That is an opinion/research-priority judgment, not a result of this study.

Fix: rewriting.

Say:

“These results motivate prioritizing…”

rather than claiming what the field objectively needs.

AC. The “three attempts” are not comparable enough to summarize as three failures of the same hypothesis

P51 tests a cellular signature selected from PASC-vs-recovered whole blood.
P52 trains nine modules on a tiny post-infectious ME/CFS PBMC cohort.
P53 trains on cfRNA and evaluates cellular data.

They are deliberately different falsification attempts, but they do not form three independent tests of the same statistical hypothesis.

Fix: rewriting.

The cumulative narrative should be “three distinct attempts with different failure modes,” not “three replications of the same null.”

AD. “The failure indicts the signature strategy” is too strong

The P52 interpretation says its failure “indicts this signature strategy.” 

1-manuscript

A 27-person study with platform confounding does not indict the strategy generally.

Fix: rewriting.

“This implementation of the nine-module strategy did not identify a qualifying signal in this cohort.”

AE. The manuscript sometimes turns null results into positive methodological lessons without enough evidentiary separation

Examples:

“cross-analyte transport can manufacture significant contrasts”

“exposing a transport artifact class”

“the field’s missing instrument…”

These are plausible hypotheses, but not experimentally demonstrated by this dataset.

AF. External annotation is much more prominent than its evidentiary value warrants

3. One particularly important conceptual correction

The paper currently has a slightly too neat narrative:

P51: positive against healthy, negative against ME/CFS
→ P52: small cohort null
→ P53: bigger cohort signal but inverted
→ therefore cross-analyte transport is the problem.

The actual evidence is messier:

P51 demonstrates that a selected module can produce a statistically significant score difference in a different cohort/platform, but specificity remains unresolved.

P52 fails to find a qualifying signal in a tiny, platform-confounded cohort.

P53 finds a strong cfRNA ME/CFS-vs-healthy signal, but that selected cfRNA signature does not transport to cellular ME/CFS and shows an opposite-direction LC-vs-ME/CFS contrast.

That is scientifically interesting. But the mechanism of the P53 discordance remains unknown.

The manuscript will actually become more credible if it stops one step short of explaining it.

6. Would it honestly represent the work after the fixes?

Yes — with an important qualification.

After the changes above, I think the manuscript could honestly represent the work as a transparent preregistered computational evaluation with one limited positive transport result and multiple negative/indeterminate ME/CFS-specificity results.

I would not represent it as:

a validated Long COVID biomarker paper,

a successful ME/CFS discriminator,

a demonstrated cross-analyte artifact study,

a powered specificity study,

or an independently replicated biological discovery.

The strongest defensible scientific contribution is narrower:

A locked module-selection/transport program found an oxidative-phosphorylation expression association that transported from whole blood to a limited PBMC NanoString panel for Long COVID versus healthy controls, but the signal did not distinguish Long COVID from ME/CFS; subsequent ME/CFS-focused analyses failed to establish a transported disease-specific signature, including a larger cfRNA-to-cellular attempt whose direction was discordant.

That is a legitimate research result even though the project's actual gates remain failed.

The most serious outstanding issue before submission is P53 provenance. If there is a real pre-data P53 preregistration commit, document it prominently. If there isn't, the manuscript must stop calling P53 preregistered. That is the one issue I would resolve before spending time polishing secondary prose.

And separately, the manuscript is currently only 17 rendered pages versus the required 50-substantive-page gate; since you explicitly do not want padding, I agree that this should remain an openly failed gate rather than being “fixed” editorially.
