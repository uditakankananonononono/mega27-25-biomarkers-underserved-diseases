B. Fixable by rewriting
1. “Transports across assay and cell mixture” is slightly stronger than the evidence

The manuscript says:

“a cellular oxidative-phosphorylation down-signal transports across assay and cell mixture to separate Long COVID from healthy controls”

in the cumulative interpretation. 

5-manuscript

The problem: the evidence shows that the score projection survives a change in platform and sample composition. It does not prove the underlying biological program is invariant to cell mixture.

The manuscript partially corrects this later by explicitly stating that transport across mixtures is score robustness, not proof of a cell-intrinsic pathway state. 

5-manuscript

Suggested wording change:

Replace “transports across assay and cell mixture”
with:

“retains association across a cross-assay and cross-cohort evaluation involving different sample compositions.”

Classification: rewriting only.

2. “The one preregistered positive transport result” needs repeated qualification

The paper is careful overall, but the phrase “positive transport result” risks being read as stronger than intended because “transport” has different meanings across biomarker literature.

The paper itself correctly says the result is:

“not a disease-specific discriminator and not a diagnostic.” 

5-manuscript

However, in a title/abstract/discussion context, repeatedly pairing “positive” and “transport” may invite overreading.

Suggested clarification:

“positive cross-cohort association transport result under the preregistered E1 definition.”

Classification: rewriting only.
