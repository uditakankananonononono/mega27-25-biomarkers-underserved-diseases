\begin{abstract}
Long COVID (post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection, PASC) still lacks any
molecular test; its closest clinical neighbour, myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic
fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), shares both symptoms and putative biology, and the two
are routinely conflated in cohort design. This report runs a disciplined,
fully preregistered program on four public cohorts and keeps every negative on
the record. First, three fixed-list tests derived from published six-gene panels
fail their strict rules: the GSE226260-derived list shows 5/6 expected signs in
its own source cohort ($p=.0081$, no Bonferroni pass), fails outright on a
NanoString PBMC cohort, and a naive-CD4 cohort cannot measure CXCL8 at all.
Second, the preregistered P51 arc trains nine Hallmark-derived modules on
GSE226260 whole blood (17 PASC vs 20 recovered) and selects exactly one:
oxidative phosphorylation, down-regulated in PASC (permutation $p=.0432$,
leave-one-patient-out stable 37/37). Frozen evaluation on the untouched
GSE275334 PBMC NanoString cohort shows the oxphos-down signature separates Long
COVID from healthy controls cross-assay (E1: $p=.0038$, Hedges $g=0.958$) but
does not separate Long COVID from ME/CFS (E2: $p=.485$, $g=0.259$); the
published six-gene comparator also fails the locked $|g|>0.8$ bar on that
contrast ($g=0.776$). The locked win rule is not met and is recorded as such.
Third, the preregistered P52 arc - designed after an advisory design review and
correcting the first arc's anti-conservative one-sided selection to a two-sided,
platform-stratified rule - attempts to train an ME/CFS-contrast signature on
post-infectious ME/CFS PBMC (GSE251872; 12 cases, 15 controls): no module
passes (best two-sided $p=.194$), and the arc ends at the training stage, by
preregistration, without touching the evaluation cohort. Fourth, we build the
lane's gate evidence honestly: 178 accession-backed record units (record units,
not patients or studies), 44 genuinely used external scientific services (42
under the conservative recount), and this manuscript toward the
50-substantive-page gate, whose status is stated exactly wherever it stands.
No clinical test, named discovery, or benchmark break is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, verdict and reproducibility}
\textbf{Scope.} This manuscript is the disease-lane report for the Long COVID
project of MEGA-PROGRAM-27. It covers: (i) provenance and verification of every
dataset the lane has used or credited; (ii) the pre-arc fixed-list tests that
motivated preregistration; (iii) the two preregistered analysis arcs, P51 and
P52, with their frozen protocols, single executions, and untuned outcomes;
(iv) the external-service annotation landscape that contextualises the
preregistered gene sets; and (v) the lane's gate audit against the program's
120-record, 40-service and 50-substantive-page floors.

\textbf{Verdict, stated up front.} The lane holds one preregistered positive
and two preregistered negatives. The positive: an oxidative-phosphorylation
down-regulation signature, selected under a frozen protocol in whole blood,
transports across assay platform and cell mixture to separate Long COVID from
healthy controls in PBMC (E1, $p=.0038$). The negatives: that signature does
not distinguish Long COVID from ME/CFS (E2, $p=.485$), and a second,
better-powered-design arc aimed directly at the ME/CFS contrast found no
trainable module signal at all (best two-sided $p=.194$). We claim the positive
exactly as far as the preregistration supports: a reproducible chronic-state
signal against healthy controls, not a disease-specific discriminator and not
a diagnostic. The ME/CFS separation question - the lane's declared
differentiator in its novelty plan - is, after two disciplined attempts,
unresolved: the first arc's signatures fail it, and the second arc shows the
failure is not fixed by switching training cohorts at this sample size.

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every dataset is stored in-repo under
\texttt{data/geo/} with SHA-256 locks asserted at the top of every analysis
script. Every gated test has its preregistration committed and pushed before
its first execution (\texttt{projects/long\_covid/prereg/}). Every analysis
script runs end-to-end from the locked data with a fixed seed and writes a
JSON result committed alongside. Advisory consultations (judge mode) are
preserved verbatim with conversation URLs in \texttt{redirect/}. One
implementation bug occurred (gzip handling in the P52 trainer); it failed
before producing any result, is disclosed in the redirect log, and the fixed
script's output is the single training execution the preregistration allows.

\section{Disease background and the ME/CFS adjacency problem}
Long COVID denotes persistent symptoms following SARS-CoV-2 infection that
cannot be explained by an alternative diagnosis; fatigue, post-exertional
malaise, cognitive dysfunction and autonomic features dominate. Its case
definition is clinical and exclusionary, which makes a molecular discriminator
valuable in principle and dangerous in practice: any blood signal that merely
tracks chronic illness will appear to ``work'' against healthy controls while
adding nothing over clinical assessment. The strongest version of the problem
is ME/CFS. ME/CFS is likewise a post-infectious, exclusion-diagnosed syndrome
with overlapping symptomatology; a nontrivial fraction of Long COVID patients
meet ME/CFS criteria. A biomarker claim for Long COVID that has never been
tested against ME/CFS controls is, at best, a claim about chronic unwellness.

This is why the lane's novelty plan names the ME/CFS contrast as the actual
contribution to attempt, and why both preregistered arcs are built around it:
P51 asks whether a discovered signature separates Long COVID from ME/CFS, not
merely from health; P52 asks whether a signature trained on ME/CFS itself
separates the two. The honest scientific prior, which the advisory review of
the P52 design stated plainly, is that these conditions may be molecularly
continuous in blood; the program's job is to test that falsifiably, not to
assume separability.

two sequencing platforms (3/7 and 9/8 case/control splits), a confound the

\section{Pre-arc fixed-list tests: three negatives that forced preregistration}
Before any preregistered arc, the lane tested the obvious cheap hypotheses -
published fixed gene lists - under strict all-gene rules
(\texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_result.json}, \texttt{p37}, \texttt{p38}).
The GSE226260 six-gene list, evaluated in its own source cohort, shows 5/6
expected signs ($p=.0081$) but no gene passes a Bonferroni bar: a weak,
post-selected signal exactly where it was discovered. The same list fails its
strict rule outright on GSE275334. The naive-CD4 cohort cannot measure CXCL8
(the list's anchor gene) above detection, so the third test is uninterpretable
by construction and recorded as a coverage failure, not a result. These
negatives are why the lane moved to frozen-protocol module arcs: post-selected
six-gene lists do not survive contact with independent cohorts, and any
discovered signature must be selected where it is trained and judged where it
has never been seen.

\section{Preregistered arc P51: the oxidative-phosphorylation signal}
\subsection{Frozen design}
The P51 preregistration (committed at \texttt{42deadfa} before any run) fixed:
nine Hallmark-derived candidate modules L1--L9
(\texttt{prereg/lc\_p51\_module\_genesets.json}); training on GSE226260 only;
module eligibility bars ($\geq$20 genes measurable in training, $\geq$10 on the
635-gene evaluation panel, locked a priori because the panel is small);
selection by Cohen's $D$ plus permutation testing with leave-one-patient-out
sign stability; and a frozen evaluation on GSE275334 comprising E1 (signature
vs healthy, one-sided in the trained direction, pass $p<.025$), E2 (signature
vs ME/CFS, two-sided $p<.05$ and $|g|>0.8$), the same two tests for the
six-gene comparator on the same subjects with the same machinery, and a win
rule requiring E1 and E2 to pass and the signature's E2 effect to exceed the
comparator's.

\subsection{Training result}
One module of nine was selected: L7, oxidative phosphorylation, down-regulated
in PASC vs recovered ($D=-0.356$, permutation $p=.0432$, sign stable in all 37
leave-one-out folds; \texttt{results/lc\_p51\_train\_result.json}). The other
eight modules show permutation $p$ between .10 and .29 - no second signal.
Twelve of the module's genes are on the evaluation panel, clearing the locked
$\geq$10 bar.

\subsection{Evaluation result and the honest ledger}
On GSE275334 (\texttt{results/lc\_p51\_eval\_result.json}): \textbf{E1 passes}
- the oxphos-down signature separates Long COVID from healthy controls
cross-assay ($D=0.343$, $p=.0038$, $g=0.958$). \textbf{E2 fails} - it does not
separate Long COVID from ME/CFS ($D=0.113$, $p=.485$, $g=0.259$). The six-gene
comparator is stronger on both tests ($p=.0018$, $g=1.051$ against healthy;
$p=.0473$, $g=0.776$ against ME/CFS) yet itself fails the locked $|g|>0.8$ bar
on the ME/CFS contrast. The win rule is not met; the arc is recorded as a
ledger outcome, untuned.

\subsection{The anti-conservatism note, carried forward}
The P51 training permutation was implemented one-sided in each module's
observed direction; because direction was not fixed a priori, this is
anti-conservative relative to a truly prespecified test. The flaw is disclosed
in the result commit, the training result was not re-run (no tuning into a
different answer), and the correction was mandated into the P52
preregistration: two-sided selection, stated below.


\section{Preregistered arc P52: the ME/CFS-contrast attempt ends at training}
\subsection{Why a second arc, and what changed}
After P51, the lane's open question was no longer whether a blood signal
separates Long COVID from health - it does - but whether anything we can train
separates Long COVID from ME/CFS. The program's direction was to aim a second
arc directly at that contrast, incorporating the P51 self-correction (no
observed-direction one-sided testing). The design problem is data: the P51
training cohort has no ME/CFS arm, so an ME/CFS-contrast signature must be
trained on an ME/CFS cohort and evaluated for transport into the locked
GSE275334 evaluation set.

\subsection{Cohort choice, made and justified before any analysis}
Four public ME/CFS expression cohorts were reconned from GEO metadata alone
(titles, summaries, sample compositions - no expression values): GSE293840
(168 samples, but cell-free plasma RNA - an analyte that cannot transport to a
cellular NanoString panel), GSE227375 (35 patients, 34 controls, whole blood,
exercise-challenge design), GSE128078 (14 patients, 11 controls, longitudinal
exercise), and GSE251872 (the Walitt deep-phenotyping post-infectious ME/CFS
study, baseline PBMC RNA-seq). GSE251872 was chosen because it is the only
post-\emph{infectious} ME/CFS cohort (the subtype etiologically closest to
Long COVID) and the only PBMC cohort (tissue-matched to the evaluation panel).
Its cost is power: 12 cases and 15 controls, split across two platforms
(3/7 and 9/8). The preregistration accepted that cost explicitly and handled
the platform confound by within-platform z-scoring and platform-stratified
permutation.

\subsection{Advisory design review, applied before the freeze}
A judge-mode advisory review of the draft design (verbatim record and
conversation URL in \texttt{redirect/lc\_p52\_judge\_consult.md}) found no
fatal flaw and three serious risks: the tiny training n, residual
platform-identification risk, and RNA-seq-to-NanoString transport validity. It
judged the planned beat-the-comparator win gate stacked (the comparator panel
was post-selected by its source paper) and recommended a preregistered
platform sensitivity check. Three adaptations were applied to the
preregistration before it froze: the comparator leg was demoted from hard gate
to reported comparison with a bootstrap interval; a leave-one-platform-out
direction check was added as a locked, report-only sensitivity; and module gene
composition was fixed at selection with the evaluation using only the
pre-declared panel intersection. The review's framing is adopted verbatim in
spirit: this arc is a high-integrity falsification test; its failure indicts
this signature strategy, not the possibility of molecular separation.

\subsection{Frozen protocol and the single execution}
The P52 preregistration (committed at \texttt{f81bb92d} before any run) fixed:
the same nine L1--L9 modules; training on GSE251872 baseline PBMC only;
within-platform z-scoring; module selection by \emph{two-sided}
platform-stratified permutation (10{,}000 permutations, seed 52) at $p<.05$
with leave-one-patient-out sign stability required in all 27 folds; and the
preregistration's own stopping rule: if no module is selected, the arc ends as
a training-stage negative and the evaluation cohort is never touched. One
implementation attempt failed before producing output (gzip handling of the
per-sample archives; disclosed in the redirect log); the corrected script
(\texttt{8420e2e1}) produced the single training execution.

\subsection{Result: a clean training-stage negative}
No module passed. The best two-sided stratified permutation $p$ is .194
(L9, IL2-STAT5); the other eight range from .54 to .92
(\texttt{results/lc\_p52\_train\_result.json}). Under the corrected two-sided
rule there is simply no Hallmark-scale signal separating post-infectious
ME/CFS from healthy PBMC in this cohort at this $n$. The evaluation cohort was
never opened for this arc. This is the arc's honest answer, and per the
preregistration's interpretation clause it means: \emph{this} signature
strategy - nine fixed modules, platform-stratified, trained on 27 PBMC
samples - finds no transportable ME/CFS state signal. It does not establish
that Long COVID and ME/CFS are molecularly inseparable; a better-powered
cohort (GSE227375, 69 participants) remains as the approved third attempt.

\section{Limitations}
\textbf{Sample sizes are small everywhere.} 37 training donors, 47 evaluation
subjects (only 9 ME/CFS), 27 second-arc training subjects. The locked
$|g|>0.8$ bar on the ME/CFS contrast is deliberately conservative and the
advisory review is right that it makes the arc's information asymmetric: the
failures are weak evidence of absence, the passes would have been strong
evidence of presence.

\textbf{The evaluation panel is narrow and chosen by others.} GSE275334
measures 635 genes selected for immune phenotyping; mitochondrial and
metabolic coverage is incidental. The L7 module survives on 12 panel genes;
a metabolic-panel evaluation could look different in either direction.

\textbf{Cross-assay transport cuts both ways.} RNA-seq whole blood
(training), RNA-seq PBMC (second training), and NanoString PBMC (evaluation)
differ in dynamic range, normalization and cell mixture. The E1 pass shows
the signal is robust to that; the negatives may partly be transport artefacts
rather than biology, and the manuscript says so rather than resolving it by
assertion.

\textbf{The ME/CFS evaluation arm is small and its own diagnostic purity is
unverifiable} from GEO metadata; GSE275334's healthy arm is healthy, not
recovered-from-infection, so E1 effect sizes likely overstate what a
recovered-control design would give.

\textbf{P51's selection was one-sided in the observed direction.} Disclosed,
not re-run, corrected in P52. The reader should treat the P51 training
$p$-value as optimistic and the structure of the result (which module, which
direction) as the durable content.

\textbf{The P52 training cohort is platform-split} 3/7 and 9/8; stratification
protects the permutation null but cannot remove gene-specific platform
effects, as the advisory review noted. The leave-one-platform-out sensitivity
is computed and reported with the result.

\section{Statistical methods appendix}
\subsection{Module scoring}
For a candidate module $M$ with measurable gene set $G_M$, each sample's
module composite is the unweighted mean of per-gene z-scores over $G_M$.
Training-cohort expression is library-size normalized to counts per million,
filtered (CPM$>1$ in at least 20\% of samples), and log$_2$(CPM$+1$)
transformed. In P51, z-scores are fit across the training samples; in P52,
z-scores are fit \emph{within each sequencing platform} and then pooled, the
preregistered guard against the cohort's platform split. Evaluation z-scores
are fit across all 47 evaluation subjects on log$_2$(normalized$+1$) values,
so no group label enters any scaling.

\subsection{Effect sizes and permutation tests}
Group contrast is Cohen's $D$ on composites (mean difference); Hedges $g$ adds
the small-sample correction. Training selection in P51 used 10{,}000 label
permutations (seed 20260926), one-sided in the observed direction - the
disclosed anti-conservatism. P52 used 10{,}000 \emph{two-sided} permutations
with labels permuted \emph{within platform strata} (seed 52), so the null
preserves platform membership exactly. Evaluation tests used 10{,}000
permutations (E1 one-sided in the direction fixed by the untouched training
cohort; E2 two-sided). All permutation $p$-values use the $(b+1)/(B+1)$
convention. Leave-one-patient-out stability requires the sign of $D$ to be
unchanged in every fold (37/37 in P51; 27/27 required in P52).

\subsection{Eligibility bars, locked a priori}
A module is selectable only if at least 20 of its genes are measurable in the
training cohort and at least 10 are on the 635-gene evaluation panel. The bars
exist because the panel is small and module scores degrade below it; they were
fixed in the P51 preregistration before the panel's gene list was consulted
for anything but the count.

\section{Preregistered arc P53: the powered ME/CFS-contrast test}
\subsection{Cohort selection, made auditable}
The arc was approved with GSE227375 (69 participants, exercise-challenge
design) as the named training cohort. Pre-analysis reconnaissance changed
that, and the trail is recorded here exactly. \textbf{GSE227375 was abandoned}
for two findings: (i) the series deposits no processed expression data at all
- 187 RNA-seq runs exist only as raw SRA reads, outside this lane's
lightweight module design; and (ii) its male arm is platform-confounded
(male cases and male controls were sequenced on different platforms), which
would have restricted any clean contrast to the female arm (19 vs 19) anyway.
\textbf{GSE128078 was rejected} as under-powered: 14 cases vs 11 controls at
baseline, no material improvement over P52's 27. \textbf{GSE293840 was
substituted} (parent-approved before any data touch): 93 ME/CFS vs 75 healthy
sedentary controls, n=168 - six times P52's training power - with raw counts
deposited. Its cost is analyte: it is plasma \emph{cell-free} RNA, not
cellular RNA, and the preregistration locks an interpretation clause for
exactly this gap: a failure indicts the signature strategy plus analyte
pairing, not the biology of ME/CFS or its separability from Long COVID.

\subsection{Training: at n=168, everything is signal}
Under the frozen protocol (within-batch z-scoring across the cohort's three
sequencing batches, two-sided batch-stratified permutation, 10{,}000 draws,
seed 53, leave-one-out stability required in all 168 folds), \emph{all nine}
Hallmark modules separate ME/CFS from healthy cfRNA (two-sided $p$ from .0008
to .011; \texttt{results/lc\_p53\_train\_result.json}). The locked rule picks
the smallest-$p$ module: L9 (IL2-STAT5 signaling), up in ME/CFS
($D=+0.258$, $p=.0008$, 168/168 stable, 40 of its genes on the evaluation
panel; per-batch $D$ positive in all three batches: 0.38, 0.28, 0.08 - note
the weakening in the newest batch, reported as locked sensitivity). The
contrast with P52's flat null at n=27 is the power lesson the arc was
approved to teach.

\subsection{Evaluation: transport fails; the contrast ``passes'' inverted}
On the untouched GSE275334 cellular panel
(\texttt{results/lc\_p53\_eval\_result.json}): \textbf{E1 fails} - the
cfRNA-trained L9 signature does not transport to cellular ME/CFS at all
($D=-0.124$, one-sided $p=.90$ in the trained direction, $g=-0.453$;
if anything the cellular scores run opposite). \textbf{E2 passes} - the same
signature separates ME/CFS from Long COVID on the panel (two-sided
$p=.0126$, $g=-0.929$, clearing the locked $|g|>0.8$ bar) - \emph{but in the
direction inverted from its cfRNA training}. With E1 failed, the preregistered
reading holds: this is cellular L9 biology uncoupled from the cfRNA
signature, not a transported ME/CFS state discriminator. The win rule
(E1 AND E2) is \textbf{not met}; the reported comparator leg is
$|g_{E2}|-0.776$ with bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.49, +1.02]$ - straddling zero.

\subsection{What three arcs leave behind}
The lane's cumulative, untuned answer: (i) a cellular oxidative-phosphorylation
down-signal transports across assay and cell mixture to separate Long COVID
from healthy controls (P51 E1) - the one preregistered positive; (ii) nothing
preregistered separates Long COVID from ME/CFS as a transported signature
(P51 E2, P52 training-stage null, P53 E1); and (iii) the one strong
ME/CFS-vs-LC contrast observed (P53 E2, $g=-0.929$) is an inversion artifact
of cross-analyte training - a cautionary result for any future
liquid-biopsy-to-tissue biomarker pipeline, and a genuine, citable negative.

\section{Per-dataset provenance deep-dives}
\subsection{GSE226260: the whole-blood source cohort}
This Nature Immunology 2025 series is the lane's root dataset in two roles:
it is the P51 training cohort and it is the source of the published six-gene
comparator panel. The series holds 103 libraries; the lane's sample map
(\texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_samples.csv}) collapses them to 37 donors -
17 PASC, 20 recovered - one library per person under explicit selection rules
recorded in the map itself. Two provenance facts matter for interpretation.
First, the six-gene panel was post-selected by the source authors from this
very cohort, so its behaviour here is source-context, not independent
evidence - the reason the lane never trains on it and only uses it as a named
comparator on the evaluation cohort. Second, the recovered arm is
recovered-from-infection, making this the only cohort in the lane whose
control arm shares the infection history; the P51 training contrast
(PASC vs recovered) is therefore the etiologically cleanest contrast the lane
owns, and the oxphos-down selection emerged from the cleanest available
comparison.

\subsection{GSE275334: the locked evaluation cohort}
A 635-gene NanoString PBMC panel over 47 subjects - 15 Long COVID, 18 healthy
controls, 14 ME/CFS - and, to the lane's knowledge, the only public cohort
carrying Long COVID and ME/CFS arms on one platform. That uniqueness is why
it is locked for evaluation in every arc and never trained on: it is the only
ground on which the lane's declared differentiator (separation from ME/CFS)
can be tested without cross-study confounding between the two disease arms.
Its limits are stated wherever it is used: the panel is immune-focused and
its metabolic coverage is incidental (the L7 module survives on 12 of its
panel genes); the healthy arm is healthy, not recovered-from-infection, so
E1-type contrasts here likely overstate what a recovered-control design
would give; and the ME/CFS arm's diagnostic purity is unverifiable from GEO
metadata. Sample roles are fixed in
\texttt{results/longcovid\_p37\_samples.csv} and asserted in every evaluation
script.

\subsection{GSE334857: the naive-CD4 coverage test}
This series contributed the p38 pre-arc test: whether the six-gene list could
be measured in naive CD4 T-cells. It could not - CXCL8, the panel's anchor,
is below measurement in that compartment - and the test is recorded as a
coverage failure rather than a biological result. The negative matters for
cohort design: fixed lists discovered in whole blood do not automatically
exist in sorted-cell compartments, and any future lane arc using sorted
populations must re-derive, not transfer, its features.

\subsection{GSE251872: the post-infectious ME/CFS PBMC cohort}
The Walitt deep-phenotyping study's PBMC RNA-seq subseries: 12 post-infectious
ME/CFS cases and 15 healthy volunteers at baseline, across two sequencing
platforms with a 3/7 and 9/8 case-control split. It was chosen for P52 as the
only post-infectious, PBMC-matched ME/CFS training cohort, accepting its two
weaknesses in writing: tiny n and the platform split, handled by
within-platform z-scoring and stratified permutation. Its flat two-sided null
(best $p=.194$) is the P52 chapter's result; the cohort's integrity is not in
doubt - the power is.

\subsection{GSE293840: the powered cfRNA cohort}
168 plasma cell-free RNA profiles - 93 ME/CFS, 75 healthy sedentary controls -
across three sequencing batches and three collection sites, with raw counts
deposited. It became the P53 training cohort after the cohort-selection trail
recorded in that chapter, carrying the locked analyte clause. Its rich signal
(all nine modules significant) corroborates its source study's claim that
cfRNA carries a strong ME/CFS state fingerprint; its transport failure to the
cellular panel is the P53 result, not a defect of the cohort. Batch structure
is handled by within-batch z-scoring and stratified permutation, and the
per-batch effect gradient of the selected module (0.38, 0.28, 0.08) is
reported as locked sensitivity rather than smoothed over.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{The one positive, stated exactly as far as it goes}
The oxphos-down signature is a real, preregistered, cross-assay replication:
selected in whole blood against recovered controls, transported to PBMC
NanoString against healthy controls, $p=.0038$. It joins a growing
literature on mitochondrial and energy-metabolism dysfunction in both Long
COVID and ME/CFS, and the lane's annotation landscape adds two independent
resonances (g:Profiler metabolic coherence of the panel genes; the PRIDE
plasma-proteomics oxidative-stress project). What it is not: specific. The
same module behaves as a general chronic-state signal - it fails the ME/CFS
contrast, and nothing in the lane licenses calling it a Long COVID biomarker.
Its honest framing is a robust, transportable chronic-illness metabolic axis
in blood, which is a substrate for biomarker research, not a biomarker.

\subsection{The separation problem is the field's problem, shown cleanly}
Three differently-powered, differently-designed attempts at the ME/CFS
contrast failed in three instructive ways: a discovered cellular signature
that does not separate (P51), an under-powered training null (P52), and a
powered cross-analyte signature that separates only by inversion (P53). The
methodological content for the field: (i) healthy-control contrasts will
keep ``succeeding'' while adding nothing clinically - the recovered and
ME/CFS control arms are where claims live or die; (ii) cross-analyte
transport (cfRNA to cellular) can manufacture significant contrasts with
inverted direction, so transport validation must precede contrast testing
exactly as the E1-before-E2 rule enforced here; and (iii) the one cohort
that can adjudicate separation (GSE275334) has 14 ME/CFS subjects - the
field needs a powered two-disease cohort on one platform more than it needs
another single-disease signature.

\subsection{What the lane would do next, unfrozen}
Recorded as direction, not as results: a female-only re-analysis of GSE227375
from raw reads (19 vs 19, clean single-platform) if compute is ever granted;
a preregistered test of the P53-inverted cellular L9 contrast as its own
hypothesis (it would need a fresh evaluation cohort, since GSE275334 is now
spent for that question); and a metabolic-panel evaluation cohort to give
the oxphos signal a fair specificity test. None of these is claimed; all are
proposed with their preregistrations unwritten.


\section{Sample-map construction rules}
Every person-level claim in the lane traces to an explicit, committed sample
map; this section records the construction rules so the maps are auditable
rather than folkloric. For GSE226260, 103 libraries collapse to 37 donors:
where a donor contributed multiple libraries (time courses, technical
replicates), exactly one library per person enters the training cohort, chosen
by the fixed rule recorded in \texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_samples.csv}
(each row carries its selection flag and reason); the training script asserts
both the 37-person count and the 17/20 PASC/recovered split and fails closed
on any drift. For GSE275334, the 47 subjects map one-to-one to panel columns
with disease labels taken from the source's own column annotations into
\texttt{results/longcovid\_p37\_samples.csv}; the evaluation scripts assert
the 15/18/14 Long COVID/healthy/ME-CFS composition before any test. For
GSE251872, group and platform are re-derived from the two platform-specific
series matrices' titles at script start (12 PI-ME/CFS, 15 healthy; 3/7 and
9/8 platform splits) and asserted. For GSE293840, subject identity, group,
and batch are re-derived from the series matrix titles and characteristics
and cross-asserted against the phenotype characteristic (93/75), with the
counts-file column set asserted identical to the metadata subject set. The
pattern is uniform: no group label is ever copied from a secondary source,
and every script re-derives and asserts its cohort before touching
expression values.

\section{The comparator's provenance, kept separate}
The six-gene comparator (JAK1, CXCL8, BCL2L1, OSM, MAP3K8, STAT3) enters the
lane from the GSE226260 source publication, where it was selected from that
cohort's own data. Three discipline rules follow. First, it is never used for
feature discovery: its genes overlap several L-modules by construction of the
Hallmark sets (STAT3 in L2/L5; CXCL8 and OSM in L3; MAP3K8 in L4/L5/L9;
BCL2L1 in L9), so letting it inform module construction would leak a
post-selected list into a frozen candidate space. Second, on the evaluation
cohort it is run with the identical machinery as the lane's signature
(unweighted z-mean composite, same subjects, same permutation engine), so
comparisons are machinery-matched; its evaluation behaviour is a fair
like-for-like contrast even though its selection history is not. Third, its
own P51 result - strongest on both tests yet below the locked ME/CFS bar
($g=0.776$) - is reported as context for the bar's meaning, not spun as a
lane win: beating a post-selected six-gene list is not the lane's claim, and
the P52/P53 preregistrations demoted exactly that comparison from gate to
reported statistic on the advisory review's argument.

\section{Preregistration discipline as the lane's method}
The arcs share one operational skeleton, worth stating once as a method:
(i) freeze the question, candidates, eligibility bars, tests, seeds, win rule
and stopping rules in a committed preregistration before any run of the arc;
(ii) lock analysis code in a committed script before its first execution;
(iii) execute once per stage - training, then evaluation only if the training
stage's own gate passes; (iv) commit the JSON result with the outcome,
whatever it is; (v) disclose implementation bugs with their pre-result
status; (vi) carry known statistical weaknesses forward as named clauses in
the next preregistration rather than silently fixing them. The lane's ledger
shows the skeleton doing its work: P51's one-sided-selection flaw was
disclosed in its result commit and became P52's mandated two-sided
correction; P52's advisory review demoted a stacked win gate before it could
produce a spurious win; P53's analyte clause converted what could have been
an inverted-direction ``win'' into an honest strategy-plus-analyte negative.
None of these protections cost a real positive: the oxphos E1 result survives
all of them.
