\begin{abstract}
Long COVID (post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection, PASC) still lacks any
molecular test; its closest clinical neighbour, myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic
fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), shares both symptoms and putative biology, and the two
are routinely conflated in cohort design. This report runs three preregistered
analysis arcs on five public cohorts, keeps every negative on the record, and
labels every post-hoc analysis as such. First, three fixed-list tests derived
from a published six-gene panel fail their strict rules: the GSE226260-derived
list shows 5/6 expected signs in its own source cohort ($p=.0081$, no
Bonferroni pass; the list was selected in that same cohort), fails its
registered cross-assay rule on a NanoString PBMC cohort, and a naive-CD4
cohort cannot measure CXCL8 at all. Second, the preregistered P51 arc trains
nine Hallmark-derived modules on GSE226260 whole blood (17 PASC vs 20
recovered) and selects exactly one: oxidative phosphorylation, down-regulated
in PASC (one-sided permutation $p=.0432$ in the observed direction - an
anti-conservatism disclosed throughout; leave-one-patient-out sign-stable
37/37). Frozen evaluation on the untouched GSE275334 PBMC NanoString cohort -
a 12-gene projection of the 195-gene module onto that panel - shows the
signature separates Long COVID from healthy controls cross-cohort and
cross-assay (E1: $p=.0038$, Hedges $g=0.958$, bootstrap 95\% CI
$[0.31, 1.73]$) but does not separate Long COVID from ME/CFS (E2: $p=.485$,
$g=0.259$); the published six-gene comparator also does not meet the locked
$|g|>0.8$ bar on that contrast ($g=0.776$, $p=.0473$). The locked win rule is
not met and is recorded as such. Third, the preregistered P52 arc - designed
after an advisory design review, correcting the first arc's one-sided
selection to a two-sided, platform-stratified rule - attempts to train an
ME/CFS-contrast signature on post-infectious ME/CFS PBMC (GSE251872; 12
cases, 15 controls): no module met the preregistered threshold in this cohort
(best two-sided $p=.194$), and the arc ends at the training stage, by
preregistration, without touching the evaluation cohort. Fourth, the
preregistered P53 arc - a larger-sample ME/CFS-contrast test on plasma
cell-free RNA (GSE293840; 93 ME/CFS vs 75 healthy, n=168, a six-fold larger
training sample than P52) - finds all nine candidate modules meet the nominal
two-sided training threshold, but the module composites inter-correlate at
0.87-0.98, consistent with one shared transcriptional axis rather than nine
independent signals. The selected cfRNA signature fails transport to cellular
ME/CFS on GSE275334 (E1: $p=.90$) and shows an oppositely directed cellular
ME/CFS-vs-Long-COVID contrast (E2: $p=.0126$, $g=-0.929$); the mechanism of
this directional discordance is unresolved. The P53 win rule is not met. The
three arcs are three distinct attempts with different failure modes, not
three tests of one hypothesis. We build the lane's gate evidence honestly:
178 accession-backed record units, 44 genuinely used external scientific
services, and this manuscript toward the 50-substantive-page gate, whose
status is stated exactly wherever it stands. No clinical test, named
discovery, or benchmark break is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, verdict and reproducibility}
\textbf{Scope.} This manuscript is the disease-lane report for the Long COVID
project of MEGA-PROGRAM-27. It covers: (i) provenance and verification of every
dataset the lane has used or credited; (ii) the pre-arc fixed-list tests that
motivated preregistration; (iii) the three preregistered analysis arcs, P51, P52
and P53, with their frozen protocols, single executions, and untuned
outcomes;
(iv) the external-service annotation landscape that contextualises the
preregistered gene sets; and (v) the lane's gate audit against the program's
120-record, 40-service and 50-substantive-page floors.

\textbf{Verdict, stated up front.} The lane holds one preregistered positive
transport result and three locked ME/CFS-separation attempts that did not
meet their win rules, each with a distinct failure mode. The positive: an
oxidative-phosphorylation down-regulation module, selected under a frozen
protocol in whole blood, transports across cohort, assay platform and cell
mixture to separate Long COVID from healthy controls in PBMC (E1, $p=.0038$)
- as a 12-gene projection of the selected 195-gene module onto the evaluation
panel. The attempts: that signature does not distinguish Long COVID from
ME/CFS (P51 E2, $p=.485$); a two-sided, platform-stratified redesign found no
module meeting the preregistered threshold in a 27-subject post-infectious
ME/CFS cohort (P52, best two-sided $p=.194$); and a six-fold larger cfRNA
cohort trained a signature that fails transport to cellular ME/CFS and shows
an oppositely directed cellular contrast whose mechanism is unresolved (P53
E1 $p=.90$, E2 $g=-0.929$). We claim the positive exactly as far as the
preregistration supports: a reproducible expression-module association with
Long COVID relative to healthy controls in the evaluated cohorts - not a
disease-specific discriminator and not a diagnostic. The ME/CFS separation
question - the lane's declared differentiator in its novelty plan - remains
unresolved after these three attempts.

\section{Disease background and the ME/CFS adjacency problem}
Long COVID denotes persistent symptoms following SARS-CoV-2 infection that
cannot be explained by an alternative diagnosis; fatigue, post-exertional
malaise, cognitive dysfunction and autonomic features dominate. Its case
definition is clinical and exclusionary, which makes a molecular discriminator
valuable in principle and dangerous in practice: any blood signal that merely
tracks chronic illness will appear to ``work'' against healthy controls while
adding nothing over clinical assessment. The strongest version of the problem
is ME/CFS. ME/CFS is likewise a post-infectious, exclusion-diagnosed syndrome
with overlapping symptomatology; a nontrivial fraction of Long COVID patients
meet ME/CFS criteria. A biomarker claim for Long COVID that has never been
tested against ME/CFS controls is, at best, a claim about chronic unwellness.

This is why the lane's novelty plan names the ME/CFS contrast as the actual
contribution to attempt, and why both preregistered arcs are built around it:
P51 asks whether a discovered signature separates Long COVID from ME/CFS, not
merely from health; P52 asks whether a signature trained on ME/CFS itself
separates the two. The honest scientific prior, which the advisory review of
the P52 design stated plainly, is that these conditions may be molecularly
continuous in blood; the program's job is to test that falsifiably, not to
assume separability.

\section{Pre-arc fixed-list tests: three negatives that forced preregistration}
Before any preregistered arc, the lane tested the obvious cheap hypotheses -
published fixed gene lists - under strict all-gene rules
(\texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_result.json}, \texttt{p37}, \texttt{p38}).
The GSE226260 six-gene list, evaluated in its own source cohort, shows 5/6
expected signs ($p=.0081$) but no gene passes a Bonferroni bar: a weak,
post-selected signal exactly where it was discovered. The same list fails its
strict rule outright on GSE275334. The naive-CD4 cohort cannot measure CXCL8
(the list's anchor gene) above detection, so the third test is uninterpretable
by construction and recorded as a coverage failure, not a result. These
negatives are why the lane moved to frozen-protocol module arcs: post-selected
six-gene lists do not survive contact with independent cohorts, and any
discovered signature must be selected where it is trained and judged where it
has never been seen.

\section{Preregistered arc P51: the oxidative-phosphorylation signal}
\subsection{Frozen design}
The P51 preregistration (committed at \texttt{42deadfa} before any run) fixed:
nine Hallmark-derived candidate modules L1--L9
(\texttt{prereg/lc\_p51\_module\_genesets.json}); training on GSE226260 only;
module eligibility bars ($\geq$20 genes measurable in training, $\geq$10 on the
635-gene evaluation panel, locked a priori because the panel is small);
selection by Cohen's $D$ plus permutation testing with leave-one-patient-out
sign stability; and a frozen evaluation on GSE275334 comprising E1 (signature
vs healthy, one-sided in the trained direction, pass $p<.025$), E2 (signature
vs ME/CFS, two-sided $p<.05$ and $|g|>0.8$), the same two tests for the
six-gene comparator on the same subjects with the same machinery, and a win
rule requiring E1 and E2 to pass and the signature's E2 effect to exceed the
comparator's.

\subsection{Training result}
One module of nine was selected: L7, oxidative phosphorylation, down-regulated
in PASC vs recovered ($D=-0.356$, permutation $p=.0432$, sign stable in all 37
leave-one-out folds; \texttt{results/lc\_p51\_train\_result.json}). The other
eight modules show permutation $p$ between .10 and .29 - no second signal.
Twelve of the module's genes are on the evaluation panel, clearing the locked
$\geq$10 bar.

\subsection{Evaluation result, recorded exactly}
On GSE275334 (\texttt{results/lc\_p51\_eval\_result.json}): \textbf{E1 passes}
- the oxphos-down signature separates Long COVID from healthy controls
cross-assay ($D=0.343$, $p=.0038$, $g=0.958$). \textbf{E2 fails} - it does not
separate Long COVID from ME/CFS ($D=0.113$, $p=.485$, $g=0.259$). The six-gene
comparator is stronger on both tests ($p=.0018$, $g=1.051$ against healthy;
$p=.0473$, $g=0.776$ against ME/CFS) yet itself fails the locked $|g|>0.8$ bar
on the ME/CFS contrast. The win rule is not met; the arc is recorded as a
ledger outcome, untuned.

\subsection{The 12-gene evaluation projection, foregrounded}
\label{sec:projection}
The evaluated object is not the 195-gene training module: it is the
12-gene projection of that module onto the 635-gene NanoString panel
(ACADVL, AIFM1, BAX, DLD, ECHS1, GOT2, HADHB, LDHA, OAT, PDHB, PDK4,
VDAC1), with the trained direction locked. Post-hoc construct-validity
evidence (\texttt{results/lc\_judge\_fixes\_r1.json}, descriptive): on the
training cohort the 12-gene projection's score correlates with the full
module at $r=0.937$ and carries a near-identical training effect
($D=-0.373$ vs $-0.356$). The projection is therefore a reasonable
stand-in for the module on these data - but the immune-focused panel was
not designed to measure oxidative phosphorylation, and E1 tests whether
this small available subset retains a compatible signal, not whether the
full metabolic program transports. That construct gap is inherent to the
available evaluation cohort.

\subsection{Selection-evidence quality, stated exactly}
The preregistered P51 selection used a one-sided permutation in the
observed direction ($p=.0432$); the direction was chosen from the data,
so this is not a clean prespecified two-sided result, and this chapter
never treats it as one. A post-hoc two-sided sensitivity gives
$p=.0721$ (10{,}000 permutations, same seed; labeled non-preregistered
and unable to replace the original result). The 37/37 LOPO figure is a
sign-stability count only; post-hoc, the leave-one-out effect-size
distribution ranges from $-0.42$ to $-0.17$ (median $-0.36$, sd 0.04) -
always the same sign, but magnitude varying about 2.5-fold, so sign
stability should not be read as effect-size stability. Nine modules were
tested and one selected; the independent E1 evaluation, not the training
$p$-value, is what protects the transport claim, and the two should not
be conflated.

\subsection{The anti-conservatism note, carried forward}
The P51 training permutation was implemented one-sided in each module's
observed direction; because direction was not fixed a priori, this is
anti-conservative relative to a truly prespecified test. The flaw is disclosed
in the result commit, the training result was not re-run (no tuning into a
different answer), and the correction was mandated into the P52
preregistration: two-sided selection, stated below.


\section{Preregistered arc P52: the ME/CFS-contrast attempt ends at training}
\subsection{Why a second arc, and what changed}
After P51, the lane's open question was no longer whether a blood signal
separates Long COVID from health - it does - but whether anything we can train
separates Long COVID from ME/CFS. The program's direction was to aim a second
arc directly at that contrast, incorporating the P51 self-correction (no
observed-direction one-sided testing). The design problem is data: the P51
training cohort has no ME/CFS arm, so an ME/CFS-contrast signature must be
trained on an ME/CFS cohort and evaluated for transport into the locked
GSE275334 evaluation set.

\subsection{Cohort choice, made and justified before any analysis}
Four public ME/CFS expression cohorts were reconned from GEO metadata alone
(titles, summaries, sample compositions - no expression values): GSE293840
(168 samples, but cell-free plasma RNA - an analyte that cannot transport to a
cellular NanoString panel), GSE227375 (35 patients, 34 controls, whole blood,
exercise-challenge design), GSE128078 (14 patients, 11 controls, longitudinal
exercise), and GSE251872 (the Walitt deep-phenotyping post-infectious ME/CFS
study, baseline PBMC RNA-seq). GSE251872 was chosen because it is the only
post-\emph{infectious} ME/CFS cohort (the subtype etiologically closest to
Long COVID) and the only PBMC cohort (tissue-matched to the evaluation panel).
Its cost is power: 12 cases and 15 controls, split across two platforms
(3/7 and 9/8). The preregistration accepted that cost explicitly and handled
the platform confound by within-platform z-scoring and platform-stratified
permutation.

\subsection{Advisory design review, applied before the freeze}
A judge-mode advisory review of the draft design (verbatim record and
conversation URL in \texttt{redirect/lc\_p52\_judge\_consult.md}) found no
fatal flaw and three serious risks: the tiny training n, residual
platform-identification risk, and RNA-seq-to-NanoString transport validity. It
judged the planned beat-the-comparator win gate stacked (the comparator panel
was post-selected by its source paper) and recommended a preregistered
platform sensitivity check. Three adaptations were applied to the
preregistration before it froze: the comparator leg was demoted from hard gate
to reported comparison with a bootstrap interval; a leave-one-platform-out
direction check was added as a locked, report-only sensitivity; and module gene
composition was fixed at selection with the evaluation using only the
pre-declared panel intersection. The review's framing is adopted verbatim in
spirit: this arc is a high-integrity falsification test; if no module qualifies, that records a failure of this implementation in
this cohort, not of the signature strategy generally and not of the
possibility of molecular separation.

\subsection{Frozen protocol and the single execution}
The P52 preregistration (committed at \texttt{f81bb92d} before any run) fixed:
the same nine L1--L9 modules; training on GSE251872 baseline PBMC only;
within-platform z-scoring; module selection by \emph{two-sided}
platform-stratified permutation (10{,}000 permutations, seed 52) at $p<.05$
with leave-one-patient-out sign stability required in all 27 folds; and the
preregistration's own stopping rule: if no module is selected, the arc ends as
a training-stage negative and the evaluation cohort is never touched. One
implementation attempt failed before producing output (gzip handling of the
per-sample archives; disclosed in the redirect log); the corrected script
(\texttt{8420e2e1}) produced the single training execution.

\subsection{Platform sensitivity, quantified}
The locked sensitivity values deserve emphasis, not mention: the selected
(no-module) landscape's platform-only effects for L1 are $D=+0.262$ on
GPL21290 vs $D=-0.098$ on GPL24676 - opposite signs across the two
platforms, on 10 and 17 subjects. Within-platform z-scoring and stratified
permutation preserve the null, but with three cases on one platform the
disease effect is intrinsically unstable; this cohort cannot exclude
moderate effects, and the lane treats its null as a study-specific result,
not evidence about ME/CFS biology.

\subsection{Result: a clean training-stage negative}
No module passed. The best two-sided stratified permutation $p$ is .194
(L9, IL2-STAT5); the other eight range from .54 to .92
(\texttt{results/lc\_p52\_train\_result.json}). Under the corrected two-sided
rule there is simply no Hallmark-scale signal separating post-infectious
ME/CFS from healthy PBMC in this cohort at this $n$. The evaluation cohort was
never opened for this arc. This is the arc's preregistered outcome, and per the
preregistration's interpretation clause it means: \emph{this} signature
strategy - nine fixed modules, platform-stratified, trained on 27 PBMC
samples - finds no transportable ME/CFS state signal. It does not establish
that Long COVID and ME/CFS are molecularly inseparable; a better-powered
cohort (GSE227375, 69 participants) remains as the approved third attempt.

\section{External-service annotation landscape}
\subsection{Purpose and accounting}
Forty-four external scientific services were genuinely used and are ledgered
with repo-resident evidence (\texttt{projects/long\_covid/EXTERNAL\_SERVICES.md}):
four core arc services (GEO, Enrichr's Hallmark library, the HGNC complete
set, advisory consultations), eight per-gene primary annotation services
across the 18 preregistered signature genes (12 L7 panel genes plus the six
comparator genes), four set-level services, and twenty-eight further services
in hub sweeps whose hubs are the six comparator genes named a priori in the
lane. The count is 42 under the program's conservative recount (three NCBI
eutils database rows collapsed to one) and 39 under the harshest recount (all
six NCBI-via-eutils rows collapsed); the ledger records all three counts, the
rejected candidates, and their failure evidence. These annotations are
context for the preregistered gene sets, not corroboration of the
preregistered results; the volume of annotation must not be read as
cumulative evidence for the signature's validity or specificity.

\subsection{What the landscape actually says}
Four annotation results carry scientific weight beyond gate accounting.
First, g:Profiler enrichment of the locked 12-gene L7 panel returns, as its
top terms, oxoacid, organic-acid, monocarboxylic-acid and pyruvate metabolic
processes ($p\approx10^{-8}$--$10^{-6}$): the panel intersection of the
oxphos module is metabolically coherent, not a residual symbol list.
Second, the 18-gene signature union forms a connected STRING module (52
edges among 18 nodes) - in contrast to the IC lane's honest null, where its
miRNA hubs had no physical edges. Third, the druggability gradient across the
six comparator hubs is real: JAK1 is Tclin with an approved inhibitor class
(tofacitinib label retrieved from OpenFDA), CXCL8, BCL2L1, MAP3K8 and STAT3
are Tchem, OSM is Tbio (Pharos), and DGIdb interaction counts vary by two
orders of magnitude. Fourth, the literature co-mention counts are honestly
sparse: PubMed co-mentions of the hub symbols with Long COVID/PASC range from
11 (CXCL8) and 9 (STAT3) down to 0 (BCL2L1, MAP3K8) - the comparator panel is
not a literature-saturated list, and neither is the lane's signal.

\subsection{Orthogonal contextual evidence}
Two archive-level findings resonate with the preregistered result without
being evidence for it. The PRIDE proteomics archive's top Long COVID project
(PXD066724) reports plasma oxidative stress, coagulation activation and
glycolytic imbalance - the same metabolic neighbourhood as the L7
down-regulation, in an orthogonal analyte. Open Targets resolves the disease
entity cleanly (MONDO\_0100233), anchoring the ontology used throughout.
Neither is independent evidence for the discovery - g:Profiler enrichment
on genes drawn from an oxidative-phosphorylation module finding metabolic
terms is partly tautological, and PRIDE does not reproduce the exact
direction, cohort, genes, or effect; they are recorded so a future lane can
test the metabolic-proteomics axis deliberately.

\section{Limitations}
\textbf{Sample sizes are small everywhere.} 37 training donors, 47 evaluation
subjects (only 9 ME/CFS), 27 second-arc training subjects. The locked
$|g|>0.8$ bar on the ME/CFS contrast is deliberately conservative and the
advisory review is right that it makes the arc's information asymmetric: the
failures are weak evidence of absence, the passes would have been strong
evidence of presence.

\textbf{The evaluation panel is narrow and chosen by others.} GSE275334
measures 635 genes selected for immune phenotyping; mitochondrial and
metabolic coverage is incidental. The L7 module survives on 12 panel genes;
a metabolic-panel evaluation could look different in either direction.

\textbf{Cross-assay transport cuts both ways.} RNA-seq whole blood
(training), RNA-seq PBMC (second training), and NanoString PBMC (evaluation)
differ in dynamic range, normalization and cell mixture. The E1 pass shows
the signal is robust to that; the negatives may partly be transport artefacts
rather than biology, and the manuscript says so rather than resolving it by
assertion.

\textbf{The ME/CFS evaluation arm is small and its own diagnostic purity is
unverifiable} from GEO metadata; GSE275334's healthy arm is healthy, not
recovered-from-infection, so E1 effect sizes likely overstate what a
recovered-control design would give.

\textbf{P51's selection was one-sided in the observed direction.} Disclosed,
not re-run, corrected in P52. The reader should treat the P51 training
$p$-value as optimistic and the structure of the result (which module, which
direction) as the durable content.

\textbf{The P52 training cohort is platform-split} 3/7 and 9/8; stratification
protects the permutation null but cannot remove gene-specific platform
effects, as the advisory review noted. The leave-one-platform-out sensitivity
is computed and reported with the result.

\section{Further limitations surfaced by adversarial review}
The judge-loop round-1 review (verbatim in \texttt{judge\_rounds/}) surfaced
limitations this manuscript now carries explicitly: (i) P53's training signal
is concentrated in the one balanced collection site (Ithaca), with the
case-heavy LA arm showing no effect - site heterogeneity is unresolved
(\S\ref{sec:p53conf}); (ii) the nine P53 module signals inter-correlate
0.87-0.98 and are one shared axis, not nine signals; (iii) P51's selection
$p$-value is anti-conservative (one-sided in the observed direction;
post-hoc two-sided sensitivity $p=.0721$) and its 37/37 figure counts sign,
not magnitude, stability (LOPO $D$ range $-0.42$ to $-0.17$); (iv) the E1
evaluation's healthy arm is not recovered-from-infection, so the positive
could index chronic illness generally rather than the trained PASC contrast;
(v) the six-gene comparator matches scoring machinery but not
feature-selection provenance, so $g$ comparisons against it are not
like-for-like at the selection level; (vi) whether the L7 signal is
cell-intrinsic transcription or cell-type abundance cannot be resolved on
these data - transport across cell mixtures is robustness of a score, not
proof of a cell-intrinsic pathway state; (vii) demographic and clinical
covariates are mostly unavailable in the public metadata (and P53's ``age
bin'' field mixes codings), so no confounding adjustment was possible; (viii)
no diagnostic classifier was evaluated, so nothing is known about AUC,
calibration, or clinical utility; and (ix) the mechanism of P53's directional
discordance cannot be established from these data - the defensible claim is
the discordance itself.

\section{Statistical methods appendix}
\subsection{Module scoring}
For a candidate module $M$ with measurable gene set $G_M$, each sample's
module composite is the unweighted mean of per-gene z-scores over $G_M$.
Training-cohort expression is library-size normalized to counts per million,
filtered (CPM$>1$ in at least 20\% of samples), and log$_2$(CPM$+1$)
transformed. In P51, z-scores are fit across the training samples; in P52,
z-scores are fit \emph{within each sequencing platform} and then pooled, the
preregistered guard against the cohort's platform split. Evaluation z-scores
are fit across all 47 evaluation subjects on log$_2$(normalized$+1$) values,
so no group label enters any scaling.

\subsection{Effect sizes and permutation tests}
Group contrast is Cohen's $D$ on composites (mean difference); Hedges $g$ adds
the small-sample correction. Training selection in P51 used 10{,}000 label
permutations (seed 20260926), one-sided in the observed direction - the
disclosed anti-conservatism. P52 used 10{,}000 \emph{two-sided} permutations
with labels permuted \emph{within platform strata} (seed 52), so the null
preserves platform membership exactly. Evaluation tests used 10{,}000
permutations (E1 one-sided in the direction fixed by the untouched training
cohort; E2 two-sided). All permutation $p$-values use the $(b+1)/(B+1)$
convention. Leave-one-patient-out stability requires the sign of $D$ to be
unchanged in every fold (37/37 in P51; 27/27 required in P52).

\subsection{Eligibility bars, locked a priori}
A module is selectable only if at least 20 of its genes are measurable in the
training cohort and at least 10 are on the 635-gene evaluation panel. The bars
exist because the panel is small and module scores degrade below it; they were
fixed in the P51 preregistration before the panel's gene list was consulted
for anything but the count.

\subsection{Cohort selection, made auditable}
\label{sec:p53cohort}
The arc was approved with GSE227375 (69 participants, exercise-challenge
design) as the named training cohort. Pre-analysis reconnaissance changed
that, and the trail is recorded here exactly; the same disclosure is carried
verbatim in the frozen preregistration. \textbf{GSE227375 was abandoned}
for two findings: (i) the series deposits no processed expression data at all
- 187 RNA-seq runs exist only as raw SRA reads, outside this lane's
lightweight module design; and (ii) its male arm is platform-confounded
(male cases and male controls were sequenced on different platforms), which
would have restricted any clean contrast to the female arm (19 vs 19) anyway.
\textbf{GSE128078 was rejected} as under-powered: 14 cases vs 11 controls at
baseline, no material improvement over P52's 27. \textbf{GSE293840 was
substituted} (parent-approved before any data touch): 93 ME/CFS vs 75 healthy
sedentary controls, n=168 - a six-fold larger training sample than P52's -
with raw counts deposited. Its cost is analyte: it is plasma \emph{cell-free}
RNA, not cellular RNA, and the preregistration locks an interpretation clause
for exactly this gap: a failure indicts this signature strategy plus analyte
pairing; it does not establish anything about the biology of ME/CFS or its
separability from Long COVID. We do not call this arc ``powered'': no target
effect size, alpha, or power calculation was prespecified, and statistical
power does not scale linearly with $n$; ``larger-sample'' is the accurate
word.

\subsection{Training: all nine modules meet the nominal threshold}
Under the frozen protocol (within-batch z-scoring across the cohort's three
sequencing batches, two-sided batch-stratified permutation, 10{,}000 draws,
seed 53, leave-one-out stability required in all 168 folds), all nine
Hallmark modules meet the nominal two-sided training threshold against
healthy cfRNA ($p$ from .0008 to .011;
\texttt{results/lc\_p53\_train\_result.json}). Nine nominally significant
modules are not nine independent signals: post-hoc, the nine module
composites inter-correlate at 0.87--0.98 on the training cohort
(\texttt{results/lc\_judge\_fixes\_r1.json}), consistent with one shared
transcriptional axis - plausibly cell-turnover or composition structure in
cfRNA - rather than nine separable pathway effects. The locked rule picks
the smallest-$p$ module: L9 (IL2-STAT5 signaling), up in ME/CFS
($D=+0.258$, $p=.0008$, 168/168 stable, 40 of its genes on the evaluation
panel; per-batch $D$ positive in all three batches: 0.38, 0.28, 0.08 - note
the weakening in the newest batch, reported as locked sensitivity). The
contrast with P52's flat null at n=27 is a larger-cohort contrast, not a
demonstrated power lesson: P52 and P53 differ in cohort, analyte, sample
size, batch structure, and platform, so the change from null to signal
cannot be attributed to sample size alone.

\subsection{Evaluation: transport fails; the contrast ``passes'' inverted}
On the untouched GSE275334 cellular panel
(\texttt{results/lc\_p53\_eval\_result.json}): \textbf{E1 fails} - the
cfRNA-trained L9 signature does not transport to cellular ME/CFS at all
($D=-0.124$, one-sided $p=.90$ in the trained direction, $g=-0.453$;
if anything the cellular scores run opposite). \textbf{E2 threshold met in the opposite direction; arc win rule failed} -
the same signature separates ME/CFS from Long COVID on the panel (two-sided
$p=.0126$, $g=-0.929$, clearing the locked $|g|>0.8$ bar) - \emph{but in the
direction inverted from its cfRNA training}. With E1 failed, the preregistered
reading holds: this is cellular L9 biology uncoupled from the cfRNA
signature, not a transported ME/CFS state discriminator. The win rule
(E1 AND E2) is \textbf{not met}; the reported comparator leg is
$|g_{E2}|-0.776$ with bootstrap 95\% CI $[-0.49, +1.02]$ - straddling zero.

\subsection{What three arcs leave behind}
The lane's cumulative, untuned answer: (i) a cellular oxidative-phosphorylation
down-signal transports across assay and cell mixture to separate Long COVID
from healthy controls (P51 E1) - the one preregistered positive transport
result; (ii) nothing preregistered separates Long COVID from ME/CFS as a
transported signature (P51 E2, P52 training-stage null, P53 E1); and (iii)
the one strong ME/CFS-vs-LC contrast observed (P53 E2, $g=-0.929$) is an
oppositely directed cellular contrast following cfRNA training. The data
establish directional discordance plus failure of transport; they do not
establish the mechanism. Candidate explanations include genuine
analyte-specific biology, cell-composition differences, cohort differences,
technical effects, chance, or a combination - the mechanism is unresolved,
and the manuscript claims nothing beyond the discordance itself. Recorded as
a preregistered negative for the specified transport strategy.


\subsection{P53 batch and site structure, characterized post-hoc}
\label{sec:p53conf}
Post-hoc descriptive characterization of the training cohort
(\texttt{results/lc\_judge\_fixes\_r1.json}; not a gated test). Disease

\section{Per-dataset provenance deep-dives}
\subsection{GSE226260: the whole-blood source cohort}
This Nature Immunology 2025 series is the lane's root dataset in two roles:
it is the P51 training cohort and it is the source of the published six-gene
comparator panel. The series holds 103 libraries; the lane's sample map
(\texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_samples.csv}) collapses them to 37 donors -
17 PASC, 20 recovered - one library per person under explicit selection rules
recorded in the map itself. Two provenance facts matter for interpretation.
First, the six-gene panel was post-selected by the source authors from this
very cohort, so its behaviour here is source-context, not independent
evidence - the reason the lane never trains on it and only uses it as a named
comparator on the evaluation cohort. Second, the recovered arm is
recovered-from-infection, making this the only cohort in the lane whose
control arm shares the infection history; the P51 training contrast
(PASC vs recovered) is therefore the etiologically cleanest contrast the lane
owns, and the oxphos-down selection emerged from the cleanest available
comparison.

\subsection{GSE275334: the locked evaluation cohort}
A 635-gene NanoString PBMC panel over 47 subjects - 15 Long COVID, 18 healthy
controls, 14 ME/CFS - and the only such public cohort identified by the
lane's search (which was not a systematic review),
carrying Long COVID and ME/CFS arms on one platform. That uniqueness is why
it is locked for evaluation in every arc and never trained on: it is the only
ground on which the lane's declared differentiator (separation from ME/CFS)
can be tested without cross-study confounding between the two disease arms.
Its limits are stated wherever it is used: the panel is immune-focused and
its metabolic coverage is incidental (the L7 module survives on 12 of its
panel genes); the healthy arm is healthy, not recovered-from-infection, so
E1-type contrasts here likely overstate what a recovered-control design
would give; and the ME/CFS arm's diagnostic purity is unverifiable from GEO
metadata. Sample roles are fixed in
\texttt{results/longcovid\_p37\_samples.csv} and asserted in every evaluation
script.

\subsection{GSE334857: the naive-CD4 coverage test}
This series contributed the p38 pre-arc test: whether the six-gene list could
be measured in naive CD4 T-cells. It could not - CXCL8, the panel's anchor,
is below measurement in that compartment - and the test is recorded as a
coverage failure rather than a biological result. The negative matters for
cohort design: fixed lists discovered in whole blood do not automatically
exist in sorted-cell compartments, and any future lane arc using sorted
populations must re-derive, not transfer, its features.

\subsection{GSE251872: the post-infectious ME/CFS PBMC cohort}
The Walitt deep-phenotyping study's PBMC RNA-seq subseries: 12 post-infectious
ME/CFS cases and 15 healthy volunteers at baseline, across two sequencing
platforms with a 3/7 and 9/8 case-control split. It was chosen for P52 as the
only post-infectious, PBMC-matched ME/CFS training cohort, accepting its two
weaknesses in writing: tiny n and the platform split, handled by
within-platform z-scoring and stratified permutation. Its flat two-sided null
(best $p=.194$) is the P52 chapter's result; the cohort's integrity is not in
doubt - the power is.

\subsection{GSE293840: the larger-sample cfRNA cohort}
168 plasma cell-free RNA profiles - 93 ME/CFS, 75 healthy sedentary controls -
across three sequencing batches and three collection sites, with raw counts
deposited. It became the P53 training cohort after the cohort-selection trail
recorded in that chapter, carrying the locked analyte clause. Its rich signal
(all nine modules significant) is consistent with its source study's report
of a strong cfRNA ME/CFS-associated signal - agreement, not independent
validation; its transport failure to the
cellular panel is the P53 result, not a defect of the cohort. Batch structure
is handled by within-batch z-scoring and stratified permutation, and the
per-batch effect gradient of the selected module (0.38, 0.28, 0.08) is
reported as locked sensitivity rather than smoothed over.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{The one positive, stated exactly as far as it goes}
The oxphos-down signature is a preregistered cross-cohort, cross-assay
transport result: selected in whole blood against recovered controls,
transported to PBMC NanoString against healthy controls, $p=.0038$,
$g=0.958$ (bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.31, 1.73]$). ``Replication'' is the wrong
word: training and evaluation differ in cohort, control definition, cell
mixture, platform, gene availability, and module representation, so this is
transport validation, not biological replication of the same contrast. It
joins a growing literature on mitochondrial and energy-metabolism
dysfunction in both Long COVID and ME/CFS; the lane's annotation landscape
adds orthogonal context (g:Profiler metabolic coherence - partly tautological
for an oxphos-derived gene set - and the PRIDE plasma-proteomics
oxidative-stress project), not corroboration. What it is not: specific. The
same module behaves as a general chronic-state signal - it fails the ME/CFS
contrast, and nothing in the lane licenses calling it a Long COVID biomarker.
Its honest framing is a reproducible expression-module association with Long
COVID relative to healthy controls in the evaluated cohorts - a substrate
for biomarker research, not a biomarker. The evaluation's healthy arm is not
recovered-from-infection, so the effect could reflect chronic illness versus
health rather than the trained PASC-vs-recovered contrast; and no diagnostic
classifier was evaluated anywhere in the lane - no AUC, threshold,
calibration, sensitivity or specificity is claimed, and healthy-control
discrimination is not diagnosis, not classification against other diseases,
and not incremental value over clinical criteria.

\subsection{The separation problem, after three distinct attempts}
Three differently designed attempts at the ME/CFS contrast did not meet
their win rules, each with a distinct failure mode: a discovered cellular
signature that does not separate (P51), a training-stage null in a small
platform-confounded cohort (P52), and a larger-sample cfRNA signature whose
cellular contrast arrives oppositely directed, mechanism unresolved (P53).
These are three distinct attempts, not three tests of one hypothesis, and
P52's null does not indict the signature strategy generally - this
implementation of the nine-module strategy did not identify a qualifying
signal in this cohort, and a 27-subject platform-confounded design cannot
exclude moderate effects. The methodological content for the field: (i)
healthy-control contrasts will keep ``succeeding'' while adding nothing
clinically - the recovered and ME/CFS control arms are where claims live or
die; (ii) cross-analyte transport (cfRNA to cellular) can generate
directionally discordant contrasts, so transport validation must precede
contrast testing exactly as the E1-before-E2 rule enforced here; and (iii)
the one cohort that can adjudicate separation (GSE275334) has 14 ME/CFS
subjects - these results motivate prioritizing a well-powered two-disease
cohort on one platform.

\subsection{What the lane would do next, unfrozen}
Recorded as direction, not as results: a female-only re-analysis of GSE227375
from raw reads (19 vs 19, clean single-platform) if compute is ever granted;
a preregistered test of the P53-inverted cellular L9 contrast as its own
hypothesis (it would need a fresh evaluation cohort, since GSE275334 is now
spent for that question); and a metabolic-panel evaluation cohort to give
the oxphos signal a fair specificity test. None of these is claimed; all are
proposed with their preregistrations unwritten.


\section{Sample-map construction rules}
Every person-level claim in the lane traces to an explicit, committed sample
map; this section records the construction rules so the maps are auditable
rather than folkloric. For GSE226260, 103 libraries collapse to 37 donors:
where a donor contributed multiple libraries (time courses, technical
replicates), exactly one library per person enters the training cohort, chosen
by the fixed rule recorded in \texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_samples.csv}
(each row carries its selection flag and reason); the training script asserts
both the 37-person count and the 17/20 PASC/recovered split and fails closed
on any drift. For GSE275334, the 47 subjects map one-to-one to panel columns
with disease labels taken from the source's own column annotations into
\texttt{results/longcovid\_p37\_samples.csv}; the evaluation scripts assert
the 15/18/14 Long COVID/healthy/ME-CFS composition before any test. For
GSE251872, group and platform are re-derived from the two platform-specific
series matrices' titles at script start (12 PI-ME/CFS, 15 healthy; 3/7 and
9/8 platform splits) and asserted. For GSE293840, subject identity, group,
and batch are re-derived from the series matrix titles and characteristics
and cross-asserted against the phenotype characteristic (93/75), with the
counts-file column set asserted identical to the metadata subject set. The
pattern is uniform: no group label is ever copied from a secondary source,
and every script re-derives and asserts its cohort before touching
expression values.

\section{The comparator's provenance, kept separate}
The six-gene comparator (JAK1, CXCL8, BCL2L1, OSM, MAP3K8, STAT3) enters the
lane from the GSE226260 source publication, where it was selected from that
cohort's own data. Three discipline rules follow. First, it is never used for
feature discovery: its genes overlap several L-modules by construction of the
Hallmark sets (STAT3 in L2/L5; CXCL8 and OSM in L3; MAP3K8 in L4/L5/L9;
BCL2L1 in L9), so letting it inform module construction would leak a
post-selected list into a frozen candidate space. Second, on the evaluation
cohort it is run with the identical machinery as the lane's signature
(unweighted z-mean composite, same subjects, same permutation engine), so
comparisons are machinery-matched; its evaluation behaviour is a fair
machinery-matched contrast - but it is not like-for-like in
feature-selection provenance: the comparator's genes were selected from the
same source cohort our modules train on, so its different selection history
must be kept in mind when reading $g$ comparisons, and we do not frame it as
an equivalent independently derived benchmark. Third, its
own P51 result - strongest on both tests yet below the locked ME/CFS bar
($g=0.776$) - is reported as context for the bar's meaning, not spun as a
lane win: beating a post-selected six-gene list is not the lane's claim, and
the P52/P53 preregistrations demoted exactly that comparison from gate to
reported statistic on the advisory review's argument.

\section{Preregistration discipline as the lane's method}
The arcs share one operational skeleton, worth stating once as a method:
(i) freeze the question, candidates, eligibility bars, tests, seeds, win rule
and stopping rules in a committed preregistration before any run of the arc;
(ii) lock analysis code in a committed script before its first execution;
(iii) execute once per stage - training, then evaluation only if the training
stage's own gate passes; (iv) commit the JSON result with the outcome,
whatever it is; (v) disclose implementation bugs with their pre-result
status; (vi) carry known statistical weaknesses forward as named clauses in
the next preregistration rather than silently fixing them. The lane's ledger
shows the skeleton doing its work: P51's one-sided-selection flaw was
disclosed in its result commit and became P52's mandated two-sided
correction; P52's advisory review demoted a stacked win gate before it could
produce a spurious win; P53's analyte clause converted what could have been
an inverted-direction ``win'' into an honest strategy-plus-analyte negative.
None of these protections cost a real positive: the oxphos E1 result survives
all of them.

\section{The pre-arc nulls in detail}
\subsection{p36: the six genes in their own source cohort}
In GSE226260 itself (17 PASC vs 20 recovered, 12{,}828 measurable symbols
after mapping; \texttt{results/longcovid\_p36\_result.json}), the published
six-gene list shows 5 of 6 expected up-signs against an empirical null mean
of 1.70 (10{,}000 random six-gene sets), $p=.0081$ - but
the strict registered rule requires all six, and no individual gene survives
a Bonferroni bar. A post-selected list showing a one-gene-miss in its own
discovery cohort is consistent with selection optimism; the lane
records it as a weak source-context signal and nothing more.

\subsection{p37: the same list cross-assay}
On GSE275334 PBMC NanoString (15 Long COVID vs 18 healthy;
\texttt{results/longcovid\_p37\_result.json}), per-gene Welch tests give:
JAK1 $g=0.90$ ($p=.010$), CXCL8 $g=0.44$ ($p=.20$), BCL2L1 $g=-0.11$
($p=.75$, wrong direction), OSM $g=0.53$ ($p=.14$), MAP3K8 $g=1.19$
($p=.0011$, the one gene passing Bonferroni), STAT3 $g=0.28$ ($p=.40$). The
registered cross-assay support flag is FALSE: five of six up but only one
gene multiplicity-safe, and the committed caveat states why even a clean pass
would have been limited - source genes post-selected from whole blood, and
PBMC-NanoString LC-versus-healthy is not whole-blood PASC-versus-recovered.
This is the result that made the lane adopt module-level preregistered arcs.

\subsection{p38: the coverage failure}
On GSE334857 naive CD4 T-cells (10 people; 25 records;
\texttt{results/longcovid\_p38\_result.json}), five of the six genes are
measurable in all or nearly all samples, but CXCL8 - the panel's anchor
cytokine - is above 1 CPM in zero of ten. A six-gene rule with an
unmeasurable anchor is untestable by construction; recorded as a coverage
failure, and the basis for the lane's rule that sorted-compartment questions
need re-derived features.

\section{Conclusion}
The lane set out to test whether Long COVID blood expression could carry a
preregistered, transportable signal, and whether that signal could do the one
thing that would make it clinically interesting: separate Long COVID from
ME/CFS. It answers the first question with a qualified yes - an
oxidative-phosphorylation down-module, selected under a frozen protocol
(with its one-sided-selection anti-conservatism disclosed), transported
cross-cohort and cross-assay against healthy controls as a 12-gene panel
projection ($p=.0038$, $g=0.958$, bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.31, 1.73]$). It
answers the second with three disciplined attempts that did not meet their
win rules, each failing differently: a discovered cellular signature that
does not separate (P51 E2), a training-stage null in a small
platform-confounded cohort, honestly banked (P52), and a larger-sample
cross-analyte signature that fails transport and shows an oppositely
directed cellular contrast whose mechanism is unresolved (P53). These
results motivate prioritizing a well-powered two-disease cohort on one
platform; until such a cohort exists, the honest claim is a reproducible
expression-module association with Long COVID relative to healthy controls
in the evaluated cohorts - robustly measurable, not specific, and not a
Long COVID biomarker. The gates stand exactly where the evidence puts them:
records and services met at their stated units; the paper below its floor
and labeled so; the benchmark/discovery endpoint open.

\section{Hub-level annotation narrative}
The tertiary sweeps annotate the six comparator hubs across two dozen
services; the scientifically legible content, beyond the ledger counts, is as
follows (all evidence in
\texttt{projects/long\_covid/results/external/tertiary\_annotations\_lc.json}).

\textbf{Druggability is real but concentrated.} DGIdb returns 82 drug-gene
interactions for JAK1 and 80 for CXCL8, against 43 for STAT3, 14 for BCL2L1
and 3 each for OSM and MAP3K8; Pharos development levels agree (JAK1 Tclin,
CXCL8/BCL2L1/MAP3K8/STAT3 Tchem, OSM Tbio), and the approved JAK inhibitor
class (tofacitinib, OpenFDA label retrieved) is the only immediately
clinically-mapped entry point in the whole 18-gene signature universe. A
biomarker lane this is not; a repurposing hypothesis generator it could
become, but only after a specificity result that does not yet exist.

\textbf{Genetic evidence is thin where it should be thin.} GWAS Catalog
mapped-gene association counts (JAK1 139, STAT3 94, CXCL8 78, BCL2L1 74,
OSM 50, MAP3K8 20; bogus-gene control 0) reflect these genes' general
immune-disease pleiotropy, not Long COVID genetics - and the PGS Catalog
holds zero Long COVID traits, an honest empty that says the genetic arm of
the field has not yet produced a polygenic score to test against.

\textbf{Variation and regulation context.} ClinVar counts (JAK1 922, STAT3
996, MAP3K8 311, OSM 85, BCL2L1 43, CXCL8 42) track gene size and clinical
salience rather than disease relevance; Signor signed-network edges (STAT3
167, JAK1 76, BCL2L1 44) confirm the comparator set is a signaling core;
validated miRNA regulator counts from miRTarBase (MAP3K8 39, CXCL8 35,
BCL2L1 34, JAK1 32, STAT3 25, OSM 7) are recorded as regulatory context for
any future post-transcriptional arc. None of this is evidence for or against
the lane's preregistered results; it is the annotated ground on which a
follow-up program would build, deposited so the next lane starts from
evidence rather than search.


\section{Related work, as the lane's datasets see it}
The lane's literature position is defined by its four cohorts' source studies
rather than by a systematic review, and it is stated that way honestly.
The GSE226260 source study (Nature Immunology 2025) established whole-blood
transcriptomic differences between PASC and recovered donors and contributes
the six-gene panel this lane uses strictly as a named comparator - the lane's
P36 result shows that panel is weaker in its own source cohort than its
selection history suggests. The GSE275334 source study (JCI Insight) built
the 635-gene PBMC panel this lane treats as locked ground, and is, to the
lane's search, the only public study carrying Long COVID and ME/CFS arms
on one platform - a design these results motivate at much larger scale. The
Walitt deep-phenotyping study (GSE251872 and parent superseries)
characterised post-infectious ME/CFS with rigorous clinical criteria and
modest transcriptomic findings - consistent with the lane's P52 flat null at
that $n$. The cfRNA study behind GSE293840 reported diagnostic-grade
machine-learning performance from plasma cell-free RNA - consistent with the
lane's P53 finding that all nine modules separate ME/CFS cfRNA from healthy,
and equally consistent with its transport failure, since a classifier trained
and validated within one analyte never has to cross compartments. Where the
lane adds to this landscape: module-level preregistered selection with
patient-level stability, cross-assay transport as an explicit frozen test,
the ME/CFS contrast treated as the load-bearing control rather than an
afterthought, and every negative kept on the record. Where it deliberately
does not compete: single-cohort classifier performance, where the cfRNA
study's own numbers already exceed anything this lane's panel evaluation
could support, and where overfitting risk is highest.
