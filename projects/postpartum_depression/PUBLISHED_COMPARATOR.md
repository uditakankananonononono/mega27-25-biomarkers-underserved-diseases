# Published comparator gate for antenatal PPD methylation

**Do not claim novel HP1BP3 or TTC9B.** The primary [2013 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23689534/) already identified both loci and reported AUC 0.87 for antenatally euthymic women, 0.12 in an antenatally depressed replication cohort, and 0.96 after adding blood counts across strata. Its [2016 replication paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26503311/) reports prospective AUC 0.81 (95% CI 0.69-0.92) in an independent N=51 with first-trimester antenatal gene-expression levels, and cross-sectional AUC 0.81 for DNA methylation in N=240 women without prior psychiatric diagnosis. These are published comparator claims, not performance we reproduced.

Our GSE44132 source audit covers the original antenatal methylation cohort, so it cannot serve as an independent validation set for the published original marker. Our GSE335141 postpartum T0/T4 methylation source uses EPICv2 at postpartum draws, not the prospective antenatal use case. It also has 41 rather than 82 mothers. Therefore a simple pooled AUC or a cross-study prediction at the same outcome timing would be misleading. A positive next experiment must predeclare a clinically sensible later endpoint, compare against the named published loci and clinical covariates, use mother-level splits, and reserve a truly external cohort. For now the two exploratory scans found zero FDR-significant probes and are retained as null evidence.

The source's methylation assay difference matters. GSE44132 uses GPL13534 HM450 antenatal blood arrays, while GSE335141 uses GPL33022 EPICv2 postpartum whole-blood arrays at T0/T4. Any direct CpG overlap requires platform probe manifests and an explicit common-probe crosswalk; it cannot be inferred from similar gene symbols or the two series' accession counts. More importantly, a postpartum T0 case/control contrast does not replicate an *antenatal prediction* claim even when the CpGs overlap.
