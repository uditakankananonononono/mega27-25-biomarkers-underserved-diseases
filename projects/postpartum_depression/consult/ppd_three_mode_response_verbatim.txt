1. Skeptical ISEF-judge assessment
What is genuinely weak

1. The effective sample size is much smaller than the accession count.

Your 139 methylation GSM/accession records do not represent 139 datasets or 139 independent observations. GSE335141 has repeated measurements from the same mothers; GSE44132 contains technical repeats that you already excluded. Treating accessions as independent would be a major pseudoreplication error.

2. The most important dataset has only ~50 people.

GSE44132's 12/11/7/20 antenatal-mood/outcome structure is scientifically interesting, but it is too small to support an unconstrained genome-wide discovery followed by an elaborate machine-learning model. A model can produce an impressive apparent AUC while having essentially no reliable external generalization.

3. Your genome-wide null is real evidence—but only against the tested analysis.

The absence of BH-FDR<0.05 among 483,266 probes in GSE44132 and 880,068 probes in GSE335141 should be reported as:

No genome-wide differential-methylation signal survived the prespecified/unstratified multiple-testing procedure in these datasets.

It should not become “there is no methylation relationship with PPD.” The latter is much stronger and isn't established by these tests.

4. GSE335141 does not validate the original prediction question.

T0/T4 postpartum measurements address a different biological/time axis from antenatal prediction of subsequent PPD. Its null result is useful, but it cannot be presented as a failed external validation of an antenatal biomarker.

5. The platform mismatch is substantial.

GPL13534 and GPL33022 share many recognizable CpG prefixes, but that does not make their measurements interchangeable. Probe chemistry, annotation, preprocessing, and cohort timing can all introduce systematic differences. A naive intersection followed by pooled modeling would be vulnerable to technical leakage/confounding.

6. GSE290313 is currently unusable for patient-level supervised validation.

The 119 RNA libraries are potentially valuable, but without the donor/person crosswalk you cannot safely establish independent patient labels. The 80 prefixes, repeated libraries, and 15 discordant case/control prefixes are precisely the sort of structure that makes accession-level ML dangerous.

7. Batch adjustment is not optional if batch correlates with outcome.

But “adjust batch” cannot mean “try several corrections and keep whichever gives the strongest result.” That would turn preprocessing into an implicit optimization step.

8. Existing biomarkers create a particularly important baseline.

HP1BP3/TTC9B are not novel discoveries available for claiming novelty. They have already been tested prospectively in independent cohorts, including work examining antenatal mood status and independent cohorts.

2. The feasible redirect

I would redirect it to:

“A preregistered robustness audit of prospective PPD methylation biomarkers across patient-level mood strata and pregnancy timepoints.”

The central hypothesis should not be “we will discover a biomarker.”

Instead:

Do previously reported PPD-associated methylation features retain discriminative information when evaluated using strictly patient-level splits, antenatal-mood stratification, and independently defined validation data?

That is falsifiable.

Primary comparator

Start with a deliberately boring baseline:

3. Preregistration skeleton
Population

Unit: participant, never GSM/accession.

Eligibility: unique participant with:

antenatal methylation;

known future PPD outcome;

known antenatal depression stratum where available.

Technical repeats excluded before modeling.

No participant appears in both training and test partitions.

Primary outcome

Choose one before looking at results:

Future PPD case/control status.

Do not switch between diagnosis, EPDS threshold, postpartum timing, or symptom severity after seeing performance.

Primary metric

I would use:

patient-level AUROC, supplemented by sensitivity/specificity or balanced accuracy.

But with ~50 people, report uncertainty prominently. A point estimate such as AUC=0.85 is not sufficient evidence of a useful biomarker.

Splitting

For this cohort, ordinary random train/test splitting is weak.

Use:

Leave-one-participant-out cross-validation (LOPO)

with every preprocessing/model-fitting operation nested inside the training portion.

Critically, feature selection must occur separately inside each training fold.

No genome-wide feature selection on all participants before LOPO.

Baseline

Pre-register:

B0: intercept-only classifier.

B1: antenatal clinical/mood baseline.

B2: published HP1BP3/TTC9B model.

B3: proposed new molecular model.

The proposed model must beat B1/B2 under exactly the same patient-level validation procedure to claim incremental value.

Go/no-go

Because the cohort is small, don't choose an arbitrary “AUC > 0.80 = discovery.”

Instead:

Go

Proceed to an external-cohort test only if the molecular model demonstrates:

improvement over the clinical baseline;

improvement survives patient-level LOPO;

the direction is consistent across antenatal-mood strata;

performance does not disappear after prespecified batch adjustment;

no negative-control feature set produces comparable performance.

Stop

Stop the biomarker-discovery claim if:

molecular model ≤ clinical baseline;

performance collapses under LOPO;

performance depends on one or two participants;

performance reverses across antenatal-mood strata;

batch adjustment eliminates the signal;

or negative controls perform similarly.

A null outcome is then a legitimate result rather than something to rescue with increasingly flexible modeling.

4. Negative controls

These are particularly important for an ISEF-level project.

Negative control 1 — shuffled labels

Permute PPD labels within the permitted splitting framework and rerun the complete pipeline.

If the real pipeline does not clearly outperform the null distribution, stop.

Negative control 2 — technical features

Use features expected to encode technical variation rather than biology, such as appropriately defined QC/batch variables.

If these predict outcome unusually well, investigate cohort confounding before interpreting biology.

Negative control 3 — random CpGs

Sample prespecified random CpG sets matched for feature count and rerun the entire pipeline.

If random sets routinely approach the proposed biomarker's performance, the model isn't demonstrating meaningful biological specificity.

Negative control 4 — temporal mismatch

Do not allow postpartum T4 information into an antenatal prediction model.

GSE335141 can be analyzed separately as a temporal/biological contrast, not as validation of antenatal prediction.

5. Leakage defenses

These should be written explicitly into the preregistration.

Before splitting:

remove technical duplicates;

establish participant identity;

define outcome;

freeze feature definitions.

Inside each training fold only:

normalization parameters;

batch correction parameters;

feature selection;

dimensionality reduction;

imputation;

hyperparameter selection.

Never:

select significant CpGs using the whole dataset;

tune the model against the held-out participant;

use postpartum information to predict an antenatal outcome;

treat GSMs as independent people;

merge repeated maternal samples as separate observations.

6. The most interesting scientifically testable extension

If the basic HP1BP3/TTC9B model fails, don't immediately search 500,000 CpGs for another winner.

Instead test a narrower hypothesis:

Is the apparent PPD methylation signal primarily conditional on antenatal mood status rather than a universal case/control methylation signature?

That is directly motivated by the published evidence.

8. What would actually make this ISEF-worthy

The strongest possible endpoint from your current material is not a flashy positive AUC.

It would be something like:

A preregistered, participant-level evaluation demonstrates whether previously reported PPD methylation predictors retain information after controlling for antenatal mood, technical structure, and strict out-of-sample validation, while showing whether apparent genome-wide signals survive multiple-testing and negative-control tests.

If that analysis is null, the project can legitimately conclude:

The available cohorts do not provide evidence that the tested blood-methylation signatures generalize as standalone prospective PPD predictors under the preregistered validation framework.

That's a scientifically meaningful negative result.

If it is positive, the bar is higher:

The signal must outperform the clinical baseline, survive LOPO, remain directionally coherent across mood strata, and then ideally reproduce in a genuinely independent participant cohort.

And that last step is where your current data hit a hard boundary: you presently do not have enough independent, correctly mapped participants to establish external validation.

So my recommendation is to freeze the discovery search for now and make the central project a rigorous patient-level prospective-validation/robustness study. Only unlock novel-feature discovery if the preregistered baseline analysis establishes that there is reproducible residual signal to explain.
