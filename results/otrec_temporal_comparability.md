# OTRec published-benchmark comparability screen (2026-09-25)

Source: OTRec's public repository https://github.com/LinialLab/OTRec and its exact `Outputs/S7-temporal_predictions.parquet` at https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LinialLab/OTRec/main/Outputs/S7-temporal_predictions.parquet (6,426,396 bytes, SHA-256 recorded below after audit); published output table https://github.com/LinialLab/OTRec/blob/main/Outputs/Table%202%20-%20Temporal%20Prospective%20validation.csv . The source table describes 2022-to-2025 prospective clinical-trial target prediction. The authors report aggregate ROC-AUC 0.872 and PR-AUC 0.288 for OTRec, versus Open Targets score 0.559/0.082, on their **own** benchmark. We did not reproduce the model or compare our tool on the task. The source parquet has 403,132 disease-target pairs, 16,074 positive labels, 11,624 distinct disease identifiers and 1,521 distinct target identifiers. It contains `diseaseId,targetId,label,score_past,otrec,ottree`.

We matched the ten exact disease ontology identifiers from `scripts/fetch_opentargets.py` against the released pair table using the `diseaseId` field, with no synonym remapping and no rerun. `results/otrec_temporal_overlap_audit.csv` gives all ten counts. Exactly one disease has any positive-labeled target pairs in that benchmark: long COVID, 186 pairs and 90 positives. Chagas has 32 pairs but zero positives; interstitial cystitis has 17 but zero positives; the other seven diseases have no exact-ID rows. A per-disease ROC/PR head-to-head cannot be run for the other nine under this released test without new source-grounded ontology mappings and prospective labels. Even long COVID currently has a different task: our gene ranking recovers same-snapshot Open Targets **non-expression** evidence under a 5-fold gene split, not new clinical-trial gene targets across a historical release. The author's temporal scores and our same-snapshot scores are not comparable. All 186 released long-COVID rows have `score_past=0`, so the historical Open Targets score offers no within-disease rank ordering; this is another warning against using the authors' **aggregate** benchmark as the disease-specific break target. OTRec's `otrec` column varies, but its long-COVID-only AUROC/AUPRC must be calculated under the exact released pair set and will be a different number from the aggregate.

The closest newer peer-reviewed reference is TargetPro/TargetBench https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-47765-3 , with clinical-stage target labels and an AUPRC benchmark across 38 diseases. Its custom code is proprietary and available on request, not a freely reproducible drop-in run; the disease and label universe still must be matched. KG-Bench https://github.com/cmbi/Benchmark_GNN_OpenTargets/ is a useful leakage-aware temporal protocol, but predicts **drug-disease** links, not disease-gene links, so its scores are not a direct comparison. The user's world's-best tool criterion is unmet. Next step, before claiming a benchmark: freeze a genuinely common disease-gene task (possibly long COVID on OTRec's released pairs), confirm pre-2022 input features and candidate overlap, avoid future-label leakage, and compare methods under the same split/negatives/metrics. No model-score claim follows from this screen.

A read-only score audit of the **released** 186 long-COVID pairs (`scripts/audit_otrec_temporal.py`, checksum-locked source, unique targets and both label classes required) yields OTRec ROC-AUC 0.939583 and PR-AUC 0.874571; the authors' released OTTree scores have 0.920370 and 0.835192; the constant 2022 Open Targets scores have ROC-AUC 0.500000 and PR-AUC equal to prevalence 0.483871. See `results/otrec_long_covid_released_scores.csv`. These are reproduced evaluations of authors' prediction files, **not** scores of ubiomark, not an independent benchmark win, and not identical to the across-disease averages in their Table 2. The 90/186 high-positive fraction makes PR-AUC strongly prevalence-dependent. A model developed after inspecting these labels cannot use them as untouched validation; reserve a genuinely new blind split for a comparative success claim.

Implementation audit (September 25): `train_node_model` seeded the optimizer/training RNG **after** a PyTorch model had already been constructed, making initialization depend on unrelated prior RNG state. A controlled two-run test showed up to 0.02294 prediction difference despite passing the same `seed=42` training argument. The code now calls `seeded_node_model` before each MLP/GCN/SAGE initialization, preserving the caller's RNG state, and adds deterministic initialization/training tests. Earlier gene-ranking tables were produced before this fix and are historical, not updated measurements. Rerun full benchmark before using any corrected comparison numbers; the fix itself is not an accuracy improvement claim.

A further transductive benchmark correction fits per-feature mean and standard deviation using only training genes within each fold, then transforms held-out genes with those frozen training statistics. The final all-label candidate ranker may use whole-universe feature scaling, but it must not be mistaken for a held-out evaluation. Pre-correction ranking tables have not been rerun and remain historical. Network topology is still transductively visible to every fold, and current Open Targets labels are same-snapshot; neither this normalization fix nor initialization seeding removes those fundamental limitations.

Win threshold clarified by owner on WhatsApp at 20:01:47 IST: a tool must either beat the best comparable benchmark, **or** match its accuracy with a concrete proven advantage. A bare tie fails. For this project, an advantage could be lower measured CPU/GPU runtime or memory under identical hardware/inputs, broader supported disease coverage under the same labels, or a predeclared interpretability metric evaluated objectively. These are hypotheses for test design, not measured advantages; the current pipeline has neither a same-task accuracy match nor a proved plus point.

A residual transductive-scaling audit caught the hybrid GNN random-walk feature: although held-out labels were masked in its random-walk seeds, its log-score mean and standard deviation were still fitted over all genes. The code now fits those two scalars on training genes and applies them to the held-out fold. This makes no new measured performance claim. A corrected full benchmark remains outstanding, and the earlier tables must not be silently reused.

The sample CNN trainer had the same initialization-order issue as the gene GNNs: it seeded before optimization but after `CNN1D` weight construction. It now instantiates inside the seed-preserving constructor and tests equal predictions after unrelated RNG draws. This changes future reruns; the earlier 17-cohort CNN table and its 0.578 mean were generated before the fix and remain historical rather than certified corrected results. The gene/CNN tasks must remain separate.

The corrected CNN initialization was rerun on the same 17 held-out GEO cohorts without overwriting the historical table. The new `results/cnn_crosscohort_seeded.csv` has an unweighted mean PPI-ordered CNN AUROC 0.576230, random-order CNN 0.575203, and logistic regression 0.627559. The corrected CNN-minus-logistic mean paired gap is -0.051330, 10,000-cohort-bootstrap 95% percentile interval [-0.135824, 0.029833], 7 CNN wins, 8 losses and 2 ties. Fourteen of 17 PPI-CNN held-out cohort scores changed from the historical unseeded output, while logistic scores were identical. A small shift in negative mean is not benchmark progress; the fair in-project comparator still leads on average and this has no published same-task baseline. `results/cnn_crosscohort_seeded_summary.json` records the descriptive uncertainty and caveats. The old pre-fix table is preserved for audit, not treated as the current corrected number.
